However, a closer examination reveals a pattern of 'top-heavy' expansion, where a select few sectors, particularly technology and high-value manufacturing, are driving the lion's share of this growth. While this concentration has undoubtedly created significant wealth and fostered innovation within these segments, it also presents a growing imbalance.The benefits of this boom are not being evenly distributed across the vast Indian economy. Traditional sectors like agriculture and small-scale manufacturing, which employ a much larger portion of the workforce, are lagging behind. This disparity risks exacerbating income inequality and creating a dual economy where a tech-savvy elite thrives while many others struggle to keep pace. While aiming for robust economic growth is essential, the focus must extend beyond headline figures to encompass the quality and inclusivity of that growth. Bangladesh should actively pursue a diversified economic strategy that nurtures a broad range of industries, from ready-made garments and remittances to agriculture and emerging service sectors.Prioritizing inclusive growth means investing in human capital development across all segments of society, promoting entrepreneurship in less-developed regions, and ensuring that the benefits of economic progress reach the grassroots. Furthermore, building resilience against global economic volatility requires a less concentrated economic base. However, a closer examination reveals a pattern of 'top-heavy' expansion, where a select few sectors, particularly technology and high-value manufacturing, are driving the lion's share of this growth. While this concentration has undoubtedly created significant wealth and fostered innovation within these segments, it also presents a growing imbalance.

The benefits of this boom are not being evenly distributed across the vast Indian economy. Traditional sectors like agriculture and small-scale manufacturing, which employ a much larger portion of the workforce, are lagging behind. This disparity risks exacerbating income inequality and creating a dual economy where a tech-savvy elite thrives while many others struggle to keep pace. The reliance on a few high-growth sectors also makes the overall economy vulnerable to external shocks and shifts in global demand within those specific industries.

For Bangladesh, currently striving to transition from Least Developed Country status and achieve middle-income prosperity, India's experience serves as an important case study. While aiming for robust economic growth is essential, the focus must extend beyond headline figures to encompass the quality and inclusivity of that growth. Bangladesh should actively pursue a diversified economic strategy that nurtures a broad range of industries, from ready-made garments and remittances to agriculture and emerging service sectors.

Prioritizing inclusive growth means investing in human capital development across all segments of society, promoting entrepreneurship in less-developed regions, and ensuring that the benefits of economic progress reach the grassroots. Furthermore, building resilience against global economic volatility requires a less concentrated economic base. By learning from India's top-heavy boom, Bangladesh can strive for a more balanced, sustainable, and equitable economic future, ensuring prosperity for all its citizens rather than just a select few.
