The idyllic tranquility of Lynton, a picturesque village nestled in the heart of Exmoor National Park, has been shattered by the grim discovery of Ann Widdecombe's body. Police were called to her cottage on Tuesday morning following concerns for her welfare, only to find the scene of a brutal crime.  Details remain scarce as the investigation intensifies, but preliminary reports suggest a violent altercation led to Ms. Widdecombe's death.  Local residents, many of whom knew Ms. Widdecombe for decades, have expressed profound shock and sadness.  'She was such a kind, gentle soul,' remarked Margaret Jenkins, a neighbour. 'We're all just numb. You never think something like this could happen here, not in our quiet little village.'  The identity of the alleged perpetrator is reportedly known to police, but an appeal for information from the public has been issued.  A cordon remains in place around Ms. Widdecombe's property as forensic teams meticulously comb the scene for evidence.  The village, usually a hub of local activity and community spirit, is now steeped in an atmosphere of fear and uncertainty.  Floral tributes have begun to appear outside her home, a poignant testament to the void her loss has left.  Authorities have assured residents that they are working tirelessly to bring the perpetrator to justice and restore a sense of security to Lynton.
