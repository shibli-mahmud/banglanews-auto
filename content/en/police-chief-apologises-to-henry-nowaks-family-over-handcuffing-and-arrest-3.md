West Yorkshire Police's Chief Constable Alexis Boon has expressed deep regret and offered a sincere apology to the family of Henry Nowak, a man who was tragically murdered, following the release of distressing footage depicting his forceful handcuffing and subsequent arrest. The video, which has circulated widely, shows Mr. Nowak being treated in a manner that has understandably caused significant upset to his loved ones. 

Speaking to the BBC, Chief Constable Boon stated that the footage was 'distressing' and acknowledged that the actions taken by officers were not in line with the standards expected of West Yorkshire Police. He assured the public that a thorough internal investigation has been launched to understand the full circumstances surrounding the incident and to determine if any disciplinary measures are necessary. The primary focus of this review will be to assess the proportionality and justification of the officers' actions. 

"We understand the profound distress this footage will have caused to Mr. Nowak's family, and we extend our sincerest apologies to them," Boon said. "The actions depicted are not representative of the values we uphold as a police force. We are committed to ensuring that all our officers conduct themselves with professionalism, respect, and appropriate force at all times." The Chief Constable emphasised that the investigation is ongoing and that further updates will be provided once the review is complete. The incident has raised questions about police procedures and accountability, particularly in cases involving victims of serious crime.
