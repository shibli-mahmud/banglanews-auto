Ecuador, once a tranquil transit nation, is now a bloody battleground in the global war on drugs.  A dramatic surge in drug trafficking has fueled unprecedented levels of violence, transforming cities into zones of fear.  BBC journalists were granted rare access to patrol with police in Guayaquil, the country's largest city and a hotbed of cartel activity.  The scenes are stark: heavily armed officers, young and determined, conducting raids on suspected gang hideouts, their faces etched with the constant tension of potential ambush.  The sheer volume of illegal weapons seized is staggering, a testament to the sophisticated reach of transnational criminal organizations.  Yet, the fight is not just against external forces.  The shadow of corruption looms large, with reports of officers colluding with cartels posing a significant impediment to justice.  "It's a daily struggle," admits a senior officer, his voice low. "We're outnumbered, outgunned sometimes, and we can't always trust everyone. But we have to keep going. For the people."  The economic and social fabric of Ecuador is being torn apart by this crisis, with communities living under constant threat.  The police, despite immense pressure and personal risk, remain the fragile bulwark against total anarchy, their bravery a crucial, though often overlooked, aspect of this desperate fight.
