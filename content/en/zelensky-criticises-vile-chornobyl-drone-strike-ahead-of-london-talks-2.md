President Volodymyr Zelensky of Ukraine has vehemently denounced a Russian drone strike that impacted a storage facility for spent nuclear fuel adjacent to the Chornobyl nuclear power plant. The incident, which occurred on Saturday, has heightened international concerns over the safety of nuclear facilities in Ukraine amidst the ongoing conflict. Ukrainian authorities confirmed that the drone targeted a facility housing highly radioactive materials, though they stated that no critical damage occurred and the radiation levels remained within normal parameters. However, the very nature of the target has drawn widespread condemnation.

Speaking ahead of his planned participation in a major international conference in London focused on Ukraine's post-war reconstruction, President Zelensky described the strike as 'vile' and a demonstration of Russia's disregard for international safety protocols and the potential for catastrophic environmental disaster. He reiterated that such actions underscore the urgent need for continued international support and robust air defence systems to protect Ukraine's critical infrastructure.

The Chornobyl Exclusion Zone, site of the 1986 nuclear disaster, has been a point of contention since Russian forces occupied it in the early days of the full-scale invasion. While Russia has since withdrawn from the area, the potential for indirect impacts on the zone's security and environmental stability remains a significant concern for Ukraine and the global community. The incident is expected to feature prominently in discussions at the London conference, with Ukraine seeking further assurances and tangible aid for its ongoing defence and recovery efforts.
