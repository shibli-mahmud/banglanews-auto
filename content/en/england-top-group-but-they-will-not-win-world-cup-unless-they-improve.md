England have achieved the first objective: topping their World Cup group. However, the manner of their progression has done little to quell anxieties surrounding their genuine potential to lift the coveted trophy. While a draw against the USA and a victory over Iran were solid enough to secure their passage, the performances lacked the attacking fluency and defensive solidity expected of a nation harbouring serious championship ambitions. Manager Gareth Southgate's side have shown flashes of brilliance, particularly in individual moments of skill, but a cohesive and dominant team performance has remained elusive. The midfield, while possessing talent, has struggled to impose itself consistently on games, and the defence has appeared vulnerable to counter-attacks.  Against stronger opposition in the knockout stages, these shortcomings will be ruthlessly exposed.  The narrative surrounding England often centres on their immense potential, but potential alone does not win World Cups. They have demonstrated resilience, a crucial trait, but the lack of a clear, cutting edge and a consistent tactical blueprint is a significant concern.  Unless Southgate can unlock a higher gear from his squad and address these fundamental issues, their journey, however promising it might have seemed after topping the group, will likely be a short one.
