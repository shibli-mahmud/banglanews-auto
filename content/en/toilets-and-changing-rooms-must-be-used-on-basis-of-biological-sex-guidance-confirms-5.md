The UK government has issued definitive guidance confirming that single-sex toilets and changing rooms should be used based on biological sex. This clarification comes after a landmark Supreme Court ruling last year, which has prompted a review of existing policies and practices concerning sex and gender in public spaces. The newly published guidance, released on Thursday, aims to provide clear direction to businesses, public institutions, and service providers on how to manage facilities while respecting the rights of all individuals.

The Supreme Court's decision, while not directly about bathroom access, has had broader implications for the interpretation of sex and gender recognition laws in the UK. In response, the government has sought to establish a consistent approach. The guidance emphasizes that while transgender individuals may have rights to access services, the provision of single-sex spaces can be legally justified based on biological sex to ensure privacy, dignity, and safety, particularly for women and girls. This stance is intended to reconcile potentially conflicting legal obligations and public concerns.

Service providers are advised to consider the specific context of their facilities and the needs of their users when implementing these provisions. The guidance suggests that for most single-sex facilities, restricting access to those of a specific biological sex is a permissible and often necessary approach. While the guidance does not preclude the provision of unisex or gender-neutral facilities, it prioritizes the legal basis for maintaining sex-segregated spaces. The publication is expected to spark further debate and may lead to adjustments in how public facilities are managed across the country.
