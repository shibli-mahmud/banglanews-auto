The Metropolitan Police Service has issued a formal apology following a significant data breach that exposed the personal information of individuals who had subscribed to receive their monthly newsletter. The breach occurred when the force inadvertently copied in all subscribers on an email, revealing their email addresses to each other. This incident is particularly sensitive as it is understood to involve individuals who have previously come forward with allegations of harassment and surveillance by private investigators working for the late Mohamed Al Fayed.

Sources indicate that the email was sent out on Monday, and it was only later discovered that the recipient list had been exposed. The Metropolitan Police have confirmed that an investigation is underway to determine the exact cause of the breach and to ensure that such an incident does not happen again. They have stated that they are contacting all affected individuals directly to inform them of the breach and to offer support.

The force has acknowledged the distress and concern this incident may cause, especially given the historical context of the allegations involving Al Fayed.  The investigations into Al Fayed's private investigators have been a complex and lengthy process, with many victims coming forward over the years.  The Metropolitan Police have stressed that they take data protection very seriously and are reviewing their internal procedures to prevent future occurrences.  The Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) has reportedly been informed of the breach.
