Prime Minister Rishi Sunak has reportedly asked Justice Secretary Alex Chalk to explore all possible avenues to prevent the early release of Henry Long and Albert Bowers, who were convicted of the manslaughter of PC Andrew Harper in 2019. PC Harper was tragically killed while responding to a burglary in Berkshire, dragged to his death by a car driven by Long.

Sources indicate that Downing Street is keen to find a way to keep the killers in prison, potentially by reviewing or amending the early release scheme under which they are eligible. Long, who admitted manslaughter, was sentenced to 16 years, while Bowers, who was convicted of manslaughter, received a 10-year sentence. Both are eligible for release after serving half of their sentences.

The BBC understands that the Prime Minister is confident a solution can be found, reflecting a strong public desire to see justice fully served for the fallen officer. Campaigners and PC Harper's family have expressed deep concern and anger over the prospect of early release, arguing that the sentences do not adequately reflect the severity of the crime.

Justice Secretary Alex Chalk is understood to be examining legislative options and case law to identify any potential grounds for intervention. This could involve exploring whether the current early release provisions can be reinterpreted or if new legislation is required to address such egregious cases. The government's focus is on reassuring the public and demonstrating a commitment to upholding justice for victims and their families.
