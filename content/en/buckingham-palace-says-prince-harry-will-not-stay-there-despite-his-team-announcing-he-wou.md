Buckingham Palace has confirmed that Prince Harry will not be accommodated at a Royal residence during his planned trip to the United Kingdom. The Duke of Sussex's team had previously indicated that he would be staying at a Royal property, leading to initial reports of a potential step towards reconciliation. However, sources close to the Palace have stated that Harry's team failed to respond to an offer of accommodation by a specified deadline last week. This missed opportunity has cast a shadow over the ongoing narrative of familial rapprochement and suggests that the rift within the Royal Family remains significant. 

The Duke's visit is reportedly for the Invictus Games anniversary service, a cause close to his heart. While his attendance at such a prominent event is a positive sign of his continued engagement with his former military life and charitable endeavours, the accommodation issue highlights the complex and delicate nature of his current relationship with his father, King Charles III, and his brother, Prince William.

Royal observers are now closely watching for any further interactions or statements from Prince Harry and the Palace. The decision not to stay at a Royal residence, whether by design or default, underscores the ongoing challenges in bridging the divide that has existed since the Duke and Duchess of Sussex stepped down as senior working royals. The lack of a formal offer of accommodation, or the failure to accept one, points to a continued distance that will likely be a subject of intense media scrutiny in the coming days.
