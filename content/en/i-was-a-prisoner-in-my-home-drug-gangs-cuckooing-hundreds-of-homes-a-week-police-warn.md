Law enforcement agencies are sounding the alarm over a disturbing trend where drug gangs are systematically taking over the homes of vulnerable individuals, a practice known as 'cuckooing'. Police estimate that hundreds, if not thousands, of properties across the United Kingdom are being infiltrated and exploited by criminal networks every single week. This modus operandi typically targets individuals struggling with addiction, mental health issues, or those who are elderly and isolated.

Gangs gain access to these homes, often through coercion, intimidation, or by exploiting existing vulnerabilities, and then use them as bases for drug dealing, production, and distribution. The legitimate residents are effectively turned into prisoners in their own properties, subjected to threats, violence, and the constant fear of reprisal. "I was a prisoner in my own home," recounted one victim who wished to remain anonymous, describing how dealers controlled her flat, intimidated her, and forced her to facilitate their operations.

Authorities emphasize that cuckooing is a serious form of exploitation that leaves victims traumatized and their lives in ruins. The National Crime Agency (NCA) and local police forces are working collaboratively to disrupt these networks, identify victims, and offer support. However, they acknowledge the scale of the problem is significant, requiring a multi-faceted approach involving community awareness, early intervention, and robust prosecution of offenders. The long-term psychological impact on victims is a major concern, with many requiring extensive support to recover from the ordeal.
