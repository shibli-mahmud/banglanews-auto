Forecasters are predicting a sharp rise in temperatures on Thursday, with the mercury potentially hitting 38 degrees Celsius in some areas. This anticipated heat spike has prompted the Met Office to issue an amber extreme heat warning, a rare alert designed to highlight the significant risks associated with prolonged periods of high temperatures. The warning is in effect for large swathes of England and Wales, from midnight on Thursday until 11:59 PM the same day.

Residents are being advised to take precautions as the heat could lead to widespread impacts, including potential health issues for vulnerable populations such as the elderly and those with pre-existing medical conditions. Infrastructure may also be affected, with the possibility of disruptions to transport services and increased strain on energy supplies. The public is urged to stay hydrated, seek shade during the hottest parts of the day, and avoid strenuous outdoor activities. This latest heatwave follows a period of unseasonably warm weather, and meteorologists suggest that more national records could be set as the mercury climbs. The Met Office has emphasized the importance of heeding the warning and taking proactive steps to stay safe and well during this period of extreme heat.
