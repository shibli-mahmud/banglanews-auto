The appointment of Jason Arday, a scholar with a focus on sociology of race, education, and inequality, to a professorship at Cambridge University's sociology department has ignited controversy among some within the academic community. A coalition of academics, led by figures like Professor Priyamvada Gopal of Cambridge itself, has publicly voiced concerns about the transparency and fairness of the hiring process. Professor Gopal, who is part of the group advocating for scrutiny, expressed her dissatisfaction with the university's internal review mechanisms, arguing that a truly impartial assessment is necessary. "Cambridge should not be allowed to mark its own homework in this situation," she stated, emphasizing the need for external oversight to ensure public trust and academic integrity.

The academics' call for an independent inquiry stems from a belief that the usual internal procedures may not adequately address the complexities and potential biases that could have influenced the decision. While the specific grounds for their unease are not yet fully detailed publicly, the demand suggests a perception that the selection committee's decision-making process might have been flawed or lacked sufficient due diligence. The University of Cambridge has yet to issue a formal response to these demands. The situation highlights a broader discussion within academia regarding accountability, diversity in hiring, and the robust evaluation of candidates for senior academic positions. The outcome of this call for an independent inquiry could set a precedent for how such appointments are scrutinized in the future, especially when concerns about the process are raised by members of the academic community.
