Chief Constable Alexis Boon has offered his deepest apologies to the family of Henry Nowak, who was a victim of murder, after reviewing footage that showed him being handcuffed and arrested in a manner deemed unacceptable. The incident, which has drawn widespread criticism, prompted a swift response from the head of West Mercia Police.

Speaking to the BBC, Chief Constable Boon stated, "I have seen the footage of how Mr. Nowak was treated. It is distressing, and I want to apologise to his family for the unacceptable way he was treated."

The footage, which has circulated on social media, reportedly shows Mr. Nowak being restrained and taken into custody. While details surrounding the circumstances of the arrest remain unclear, the manner in which it was conducted has been widely condemned.

"We are taking this matter extremely seriously and will be taking appropriate action. We are conducting a thorough investigation into the officers' conduct and the circumstances surrounding the arrest," Boon added. He assured the public that the force is committed to upholding the highest standards of policing and that such incidents would not be tolerated.

The apology comes at a sensitive time for the Nowak family, who are already grieving the loss of their loved one. The focus now shifts to the internal investigation and the accountability of the officers involved. The Chief Constable's direct and public apology signals an intent to address the issue transparently and to rebuild trust with the community.
