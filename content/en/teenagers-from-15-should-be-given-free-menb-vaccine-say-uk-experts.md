Leading scientific advisors in the United Kingdom are urging the government to consider introducing the Meningococcal serogroup B (MenB) vaccine as a routine immunization for teenagers around the age of 15. The recommendation comes from a desire to bolster public health defenses against meningococcal disease, a rare but severe illness caused by bacteria that can lead to meningitis and septicaemia.  While the vaccine is currently offered to infants and young children, experts believe that extending this protection to adolescents is crucial for establishing broader herd immunity and safeguarding a demographic known to be at a higher risk during certain periods of their lives.  The Joint Committee on Vaccination and Immunisation (JCVI) and other health bodies are reportedly pushing for this change, citing evidence that suggests a significant portion of outbreaks and severe cases occur in this age group.  Introducing the vaccine routinely at secondary school age could ensure a high uptake rate and provide a more comprehensive shield against this life-threatening infection.  The move would require a significant investment and logistical planning, but proponents argue that the long-term benefits in preventing serious illness, disability, and even death far outweigh the costs.  Discussions are understood to be ongoing with the Department of Health and Social Care to evaluate the feasibility and potential impact of such a policy shift.
