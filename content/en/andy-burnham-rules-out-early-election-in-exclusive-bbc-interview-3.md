Prime Minister Andy Burnham has unequivocally dismissed any notion of an early general election, stating his primary focus is on delivering the promises made in the last electoral mandate. Speaking to Laura Kuenssberg on BBC's Sunday with Laura Kuenssberg, Burnham emphasized the democratic will of the people, arguing that voters "voted for a manifesto" and that his government would "honour that".

When directly pressed on the prospect of going back to the polls sooner than constitutionally required, the Prime Minister presented a united front, suggesting that the focus should be on governance and policy implementation, not premature electoral campaigning. He highlighted the challenges facing the nation and the need for stability and consistent leadership to address them. "We have a clear mandate, a set of promises that we are working tirelessly to deliver," Burnham stated, signaling a commitment to the established parliamentary term. This stance is likely to be met with mixed reactions, with opposition parties potentially accusing the government of avoiding scrutiny, while supporters will view it as a sign of steadfastness and a focus on practical solutions. The Prime Minister's clear message aims to quell speculation and project an image of a government focused on its core nhiệm vụ rather than electoral opportunism.
