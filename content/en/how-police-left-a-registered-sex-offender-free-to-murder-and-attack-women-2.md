A damning admission from a senior Metropolitan Police officer has revealed shocking lapses in the force's handling of registered sex offender Simon Levy, allowing him to commit further atrocities. Levy, who had a history of offending, was free to murder and attack women, with the latest victim, Sheryl Wilkins, potentially being saved had the police acted more effectively. The officer, speaking anonymously to maintain professional standing, detailed how communication breakdowns, inadequate risk assessments, and a lack of robust follow-up procedures contributed to Levy remaining at large and posing a severe threat to the public.

This admission comes as the investigation into Levy's crimes intensifies, raising serious questions about the Met's effectiveness in managing dangerous individuals. Sources close to the investigation suggest that intelligence regarding Levy's movements and potential intentions was not adequately shared or acted upon by different departments within the force. The officer highlighted a systemic issue where the focus on reactive policing often overshadows proactive measures needed to prevent such tragedies. The failure to adequately monitor Levy, despite his known risk factors, has led to a devastating outcome and profound distrust among the communities the police are sworn to protect. The Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC) has confirmed it is reviewing the circumstances surrounding Levy's case, with a focus on the Met's engagement with him and the decisions made by officers throughout his period of offending. The Met has stated it is cooperating fully with the investigation and is committed to learning from these failures to prevent future occurrences.
