The acrid smell of smoke still hung heavy in the air over parts of north Belfast on Thursday morning, a grim reminder of the destructive night that had unfolded. Residents surveyed the damage, their faces etched with disbelief and anger, as the echoes of sirens and shouting faded, replaced by a stunned silence. The unrest, which police confirmed was linked to a serious knife attack on a shopkeeper earlier in the day, spiralled into widespread public disorder, with petrol bombs and bricks hurled at officers and emergency services.

Several homes were engulfed in flames, forcing terrified families to abandon their properties and seek refuge. "I'll never get over watching my home burn," said one distraught resident, who asked to remain anonymous, tears streaming down her face as she pointed towards the smouldering remains of her house. "We lost everything. All our memories, just gone in the flames." The scenes were replicated across several streets, with images emerging of masked individuals engaged in violent confrontations with police lines. Fire crews worked tirelessly through the night to contain the fires, facing significant danger from thrown projectiles. A spokesperson for the Northern Ireland Fire and Rescue Service stated that multiple crews were deployed and that their safety was a primary concern throughout the incident. The scale of the destruction has left many questioning the motivations behind the violence and its impact on an already fragile peace. The police have launched a full investigation into the events, appealing for witnesses and condemning the rioting, which they described as "utterly unacceptable."
