Andy Burnham, the Mayor of Greater Manchester, has stated he is 'confident' that steps can be taken to halt the potential early release of the individuals convicted of the murder of PC Andrew Harper. The tragic death of PC Harper, who was killed in August 2019 while responding to a burglary, sent shockwaves across the nation. His killers, Henry Long, Jessie Cole, and Albert Bowers, were sentenced for manslaughter and conspiracy to pervert the course of justice, not murder. However, concerns have been raised about the possibility of them being eligible for parole after serving a portion of their sentences.

In response to these anxieties and broader public unease regarding early releases for serious offenders, Mr. Burnham revealed that the Secretary of State for Justice is preparing to unveil a new plan. This plan, expected in September, is designed to introduce significant reforms to the early release system. While the specific details of the proposed changes are yet to be fully disclosed, the intention is reportedly to create a more robust framework that prevents early parole for individuals convicted of such grave offenses. The Mayor's statement signals a proactive governmental approach to address public concerns and potentially amend existing legislation or sentencing guidelines to ensure justice for victims and their families.
