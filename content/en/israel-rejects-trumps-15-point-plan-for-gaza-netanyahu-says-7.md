Jerusalem – Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has firmly rejected a 15-point peace proposal reportedly put forth by former US President Donald Trump concerning the future of Gaza. In a statement released earlier today, Netanyahu emphasized that Israel's military operations in the besieged enclave will continue until Hamas is "genuinely" disarmed and its military capabilities are dismantled. The Prime Minister's office elaborated that any security arrangements for Gaza must include Israel's continued presence and oversight to prevent the resurgence of militant groups.

Sources close to the Israeli government suggest that the plan, which was not publicly detailed by Trump's team, likely included provisions for an international peacekeeping force and a roadmap for Palestinian self-governance. However, Netanyahu's administration views such proposals with significant skepticism, arguing that they do not adequately address Israel's fundamental security requirements. The primary concern remains ensuring that Gaza does not again become a launchpad for attacks against Israel.

Netanyahu's stance underscores Israel's commitment to achieving what it describes as "total victory" over Hamas, a goal that has been met with international pressure due to the ongoing humanitarian crisis in Gaza. The rejection of Trump's plan, a prominent figure with significant influence in Republican circles, signals a potential divergence in approaches to resolving the conflict, with Israel prioritizing its security imperatives above all else. The future governance of Gaza and the path to a lasting peace remain profoundly uncertain as these competing visions clash.
