A recent investigation by the National Audit Office (NAO), a UK public spending watchdog, has shed light on the property dealings of senior royals, with a particular focus on Prince Andrew, Duke of York. The report, which examines the stewardship of royal assets and public funds, reportedly details how Prince Andrew has been sub-letting cottages located on the grounds of Royal Lodge, his official residence in Windsor Great Park.

While the exact terms and beneficiaries of these sub-lettings remain under scrutiny, the revelations have sparked questions about transparency and the appropriate use of royal properties funded, at least in part, by the public purse. Royal Lodge itself is a Grade II listed building and its upkeep is a significant expense.

The NAO's findings are expected to be released in full soon, but early indications suggest a detailed analysis of how royal residences are managed and whether any commercial activities conducted on these properties align with public expectations. This investigation follows a period of increased public interest in the financial affairs of the Royal Family, particularly concerning expenses and the use of official residences.

Buckingham Palace has yet to issue a formal statement regarding the specific allegations of sub-letting at Royal Lodge. However, the watchdog's involvement signifies a rigorous examination of Prince Andrew's use of the property. The report aims to ensure accountability and provide greater clarity on the financial arrangements underpinning the monarchy's extensive property portfolio.
