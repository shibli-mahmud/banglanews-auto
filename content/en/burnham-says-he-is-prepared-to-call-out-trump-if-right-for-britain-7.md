In an exclusive interview with the BBC, Prime Minister Keir Starmer articulated a firm position on how he intends to engage with the United States under President Donald Trump. Starmer stated unequivocally that he is prepared to 'call out' the US president if such actions are deemed necessary for the advancement of Britain's interests. This assertion underscores a foreign policy approach centered on pragmatism and the unwavering defense of national sovereignty.

"You have to defend your own national interest," Starmer emphasized, drawing a clear line between diplomatic niceties and the core responsibility of a head of government. He revealed that his initial conversation with President Trump had been positive, but stressed that this goodwill would not preclude him from challenging policies or statements that he believes could be detrimental to the United Kingdom. This suggests a calibrated approach, where relationships will be nurtured, but not at the expense of core British objectives.

The Prime Minister's remarks signal a departure from a purely deferential stance towards the US, indicating a more assertive role for Britain on the global stage. This could involve trade negotiations, security arrangements, or any other bilateral issue where divergence of interests might arise. Starmer's administration appears poised to navigate complex international waters with a keen eye on domestic well-being, ensuring that Britain's voice is heard and its priorities are respected. The implications of this assertive posture for the broader transatlantic relationship remain to be seen, but it signals a commitment to a strong and independent foreign policy.
