Andy Burnham, the Mayor of Greater Manchester, has voiced strong opposition to new industry rules being considered by the European Union, asserting that they pose a substantial threat to the UK's economic interests. According to Burnham, these regulations, if implemented as proposed, could create an uneven playing field and disadvantage British businesses operating within the EU or trading with the bloc.

The mayor's office stated that Burnham intends to make his concerns a central point of discussion when he meets with the President of the European Commission. The specific nature of the proposed rules has not been fully detailed, but the mayor's office indicated that they relate to sectors crucial for the UK's industrial base and export market. He is expected to argue that the legislation could lead to increased costs for UK companies, hinder innovation, and potentially result in job losses.

Burnham's intervention highlights the ongoing complexities of the UK's relationship with the EU post-Brexit, particularly concerning regulatory alignment. He emphasized the need for the EU to consider the broader implications of its policy decisions on its trading partners and to foster a collaborative approach rather than one that could be perceived as protectionist. The upcoming meeting presents a critical opportunity for the UK to formally register its objections and seek assurances that its economic well-being will be taken into account.
