The Royal Navy is in mourning following the loss of three of its brave crew members in a tragic helicopter crash. Lieutenant Commander Chris Gayson, 42, Lieutenant Lily-Mae Fisher, 31, and Petty Officer Owen Green, 24, were all lost when their AW101 Merlin helicopter crashed off the coast of Scotland on Tuesday evening. The aircraft was reportedly engaged in routine training operations at the time of the incident. 

All three were members of 820 Naval Air Squadron, which operates from the carrier HMS Prince of Wales. The Ministry of Defence has confirmed the tragic deaths and stated that a full investigation into the cause of the crash is underway. Senior naval officers have expressed their profound sadness and offered their deepest condolences to the families and friends of the deceased. 

Lieutenant Commander Gayson, from Liverpool, was described by his commanding officer as a 'highly respected and experienced pilot' who was a 'dedicated leader and a friend to many'. Lieutenant Fisher, from Brighton, was a 'talented observer' and a 'valued member of the squadron'. Petty Officer Green, from Manchester, was hailed as a 'promising young aviator' who was 'passionate about his service'. 

Floral tributes have begun to appear at naval bases across the country, and a period of national remembrance is expected. The loss of these three individuals is a significant blow to the Royal Navy and highlights the inherent dangers faced by those serving in its ranks. The focus now shifts to supporting the grieving families and understanding the circumstances that led to this devastating accident.
