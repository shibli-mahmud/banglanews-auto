The Houthi forces stated they targeted two oil tankers navigating the Bab el-Mandeb strait, a crucial chokepoint for global shipping. While details of the attack remain scarce, the claim represents the first time the Iran-backed group has asserted responsibility for striking oil carriers in the Red Sea since they announced a "maritime embargo" against Saudi Arabia and its allies. This declaration was part of their broader stance against the ongoing conflict in Yemen and perceived Western involvement.

The reported Houthi action follows a series of retaliatory strikes launched by the United States against Iran and its proxies in the region. The US has been conducting air and missile strikes on targets it attributes to Iran, including missile launch sites, radar installations, and drone facilities, in response to attacks on US forces and its allies in the Middle East. These US actions have been framed as a deterrent against further aggression and an effort to degrade Iran's military capabilities and its ability to project power through its network of allied militias.

The concurrent events in the Red Sea and the broader region amplify fears of a wider proxy war and a significant disruption to international trade, particularly oil shipments. The Bab el-Mandeb strait is one of the world's busiest maritime routes, and any sustained threat to its security could have severe economic repercussions globally. International observers are closely monitoring the situation, urging de-escalation and dialogue to prevent further bloodshed and instability.
