The UK has officially recorded its hottest May day in history, as temperatures soared well past the 34-degree Celsius mark across various regions on Monday. The mercury climbed to an astonishing 34.1C in Coningsby, Lincolnshire, shattering previous records for this late May period. This exceptional heatwave has brought with it a stark reminder of the rapidly changing climate, prompting public health warnings and advice to stay hydrated and seek shade.

The record-breaking heat is a continuation of a trend that saw the UK endure its warmest May night just 24 hours prior. On Sunday night, temperatures failed to drop below a balmy 19.4C in parts of the country, making for an uncomfortable and unusual sleeping environment for many. Forecasters from the Met Office have attributed the intense heat to a 'tropical continental airmass' being drawn up from the south, exacerbated by clear skies and strong sunshine.

While the prolonged sunshine and warmth have been welcomed by some, particularly for outdoor activities, the extreme temperatures pose a risk, especially to vulnerable populations. Health authorities are urging people to take precautions, including drinking plenty of fluids, avoiding strenuous activity during the hottest parts of the day, and checking on elderly relatives and neighbours. The unprecedented nature of this heatwave has also raised concerns about its impact on ecosystems and the agricultural sector, with potential implications for water resources and crop yields. The Met Office has indicated that while the heat is expected to subside slightly in some areas towards the end of the week, warmer-than-average conditions are likely to persist.
