Romania's Ministry of National Defence announced that debris from a drone, suspected to be of Russian manufacture, was discovered near the village of Plauru, close to the Danube River border with Ukraine. The ministry clarified that the drone was likely hit by Ukrainian air defense systems while operating over Ukrainian territory, causing it to deviate from its intended path and crash in Romania. This development has sparked immediate concern and condemnation from key Western alliances.

NATO Secretary General Jens Stoltenberg stated that the alliance takes such incidents very seriously and that any potential attack on NATO territory would be met with a united and resolute response. He reiterated NATO's commitment to the security of its member states and emphasized that the alliance is closely monitoring the situation. The EU's High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, Josep Borrell, echoed these sentiments, labeling the incident as a "very serious" development. He stressed that such actions demonstrate a disregard for international law and the sovereignty of neighboring countries.

Romanian President Klaus Iohannis condemned the act and called for an immediate investigation. He emphasized that Romania, as a NATO member, is fully secure but that this incident underscores the persistent threats emanating from Russia's ongoing aggression against Ukraine. The incident has fueled further calls for increased air defense capabilities and heightened vigilance along NATO's eastern flank. While Romania insists the drone was a consequence of Ukrainian defensive actions, the presence of Russian military hardware falling onto NATO soil remains a significant cause for alarm and a clear indicator of the escalating regional tensions.
