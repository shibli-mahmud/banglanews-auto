Bangladesh's rapid economic development over the past decade has been commendable, but it has come at a cost. The country's energy infrastructure is heavily dominated by imported fossil fuels, primarily coal and natural gas, which are not only expensive but also contribute significantly to greenhouse gas emissions. This dependence makes Bangladesh susceptible to global price volatility and supply chain disruptions, impacting both its economy and energy security. Moreover, the nation is a frontline state in the climate crisis, experiencing the brunt of rising sea levels, extreme weather events, and erratic rainfall. Continuing down the path of fossil fuel dependency will only exacerbate these vulnerabilities.

The argument for a decisive pivot to renewable energy – solar, wind, and potentially hydropower – is multifaceted. Firstly, it aligns with Bangladesh's climate commitments and its role as a responsible global citizen. Secondly, renewable energy offers a pathway to energy independence, reducing reliance on volatile international markets and expensive imports. The declining costs of solar power, in particular, make it an increasingly attractive and competitive option for a country blessed with abundant sunshine. Thirdly, a green energy transition can create new economic opportunities and skilled jobs, fostering a sustainable and inclusive growth model. Investing in domestic renewable energy infrastructure will stimulate local economies and build a resilient energy system. Finally, by mitigating climate change impacts through reduced emissions, Bangladesh can protect its land, its people, and its future prosperity from the devastating consequences of environmental degradation. The time for incremental changes is over; a bold and immediate pivot to renewable energy is essential for Bangladesh's survival and thriving.
