In a significant directive that underscores his administration's cautious approach to international diplomacy, President Donald Trump has reportedly advised his negotiators not to be pressured into a swift resolution with Iran. The comments, which emerged through US media outlets, suggest a desire for a meticulously considered agreement rather than a rushed pact. Central to the ongoing discussions appears to be a proposal for a 60-day extension of a current ceasefire. This extension, if agreed upon, would reportedly be contingent on Iran facilitating the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil trade. The implications of such a deal are substantial, potentially impacting regional stability and energy markets. Trump's directive signals a preference for thorough vetting and a strategic, rather than hurried, engagement with Tehran, reflecting a broader pattern of his foreign policy approach. The exact nature of the concessions and guarantees being sought by both sides remains a subject of intense speculation, but the emphasis on avoiding haste suggests a deliberate strategy to extract maximum advantage and minimize potential risks. This latest development adds another layer of complexity to the already intricate geopolitical landscape surrounding Iran.
