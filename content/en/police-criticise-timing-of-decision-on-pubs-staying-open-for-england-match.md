The National Police Chiefs' Council (NPCC) has voiced significant disappointment over the late notification from the government regarding the extension of pub opening hours for the Euro 2020 final, which kicks off at 8:00 PM BST on Sunday and could potentially run late into Monday morning. The decision, allowing pubs to remain open until 5:00 AM on Monday, was announced with insufficient notice, according to the NPCC.

Senior police figures have highlighted that the "late announcement" has forced police forces across the country to make last-minute adjustments to their operational plans. This includes reallocating officers who would have otherwise been engaged in other crucial policing activities, such as responding to emergencies, tackling crime, and maintaining visible patrols. The NPCC argues that such unforeseen shifts in deployment strain resources and divert attention from regular law enforcement responsibilities. While acknowledging the national significance of the England match and the desire for fans to celebrate, the council emphasised the importance of timely communication for effective policing. The lack of advance warning, they contend, compromises their ability to adequately prepare and deploy resources efficiently, potentially leading to increased pressure on services and a reduced capacity to respond to other incidents. This situation underscores a recurring tension between major public events and the operational demands placed upon the police service, often exacerbated by last-minute decision-making.
