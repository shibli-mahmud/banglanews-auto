Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has asserted that Israel's military operations in the Gaza Strip will continue until Hamas is "genuinely" disarmed, effectively rejecting a purported 15-point peace plan allegedly proposed by former US President Donald Trump. Sources within the Israeli government have indicated that the plan, which reportedly included provisions for a potential Palestinian state and a timeline for Israeli withdrawal, does not align with Israel's security objectives.

Netanyahu's office has not officially confirmed the existence or details of Trump's proposal. However, the Prime Minister's firm stance on continued military presence in Gaza underscores Israel's commitment to dismantling Hamas's military capabilities and ensuring its long-term security. He reiterated that any potential peace agreement must prioritize the complete disarmament of the militant group and the return of hostages held by Hamas.

The Israeli leadership has consistently maintained that a full withdrawal from Gaza before achieving these objectives would be premature and would not guarantee regional stability. While the specifics of Trump's reported plan remain unclear, the rejection signals a significant divergence in approaches to resolving the ongoing conflict and achieving lasting peace in the region. International mediators are expected to continue their efforts to broker a resolution, but Israel's current position suggests a protracted military engagement in Gaza remains the government's primary strategy.
