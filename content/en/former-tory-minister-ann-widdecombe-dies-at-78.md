Ann Widdecombe, a former Conservative MP who became a prominent figure in the Brexit movement and a familiar face on television, has died at the age of 78. Her death was announced on Monday, prompting tributes from across the political spectrum. Widdecombe served as the MP for Maidstone from 1987 to 1997 and then forxrightarrow from 1997 to 2010, establishing a reputation for her forthright views and passionate oratory.  During her parliamentary career, she held several junior ministerial positions but was perhaps most recognised for her vocal stances on social and cultural issues, often aligning herself with more traditionalist viewpoints.  However, it was arguably in her later career, post-parliament, that she gained an even wider public profile. She was a leading and articulate advocate for Britain's exit from the European Union, consistently arguing for national sovereignty and greater control over borders. Her presence in televised debates and public meetings was a constant feature of the Brexit campaign, where she effectively communicated her message to a broad audience.  Following her political career, Widdecombe became a regular fixture on current affairs programmes and reality television shows, further cementing her status as a well-known public personality. Her unapologetic approach and clear convictions ensured she remained a topic of conversation long after leaving Westminster. She was a Member of Parliament for over two decades and leaves a legacy as a formidable and unforgettable voice in modern British politics.
