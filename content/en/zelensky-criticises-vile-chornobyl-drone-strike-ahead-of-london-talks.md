President Volodymyr Zelensky has vehemently condemned a Russian drone strike that reportedly hit a facility storing spent nuclear fuel near the Chornobyl nuclear power plant. The incident, occurring just ahead of crucial talks in London focused on Ukraine's reconstruction and security, has amplified concerns over Russia's continued disregard for critical infrastructure and potential nuclear safety.  Ukrainian authorities stated that the drone targeted a facility within the Chornobyl exclusion zone, a region still heavily affected by the 1986 nuclear disaster. While the immediate impact on the spent fuel rods is being assessed, initial reports suggest no radioactive leak occurred. However, the symbolic and potentially dangerous nature of the attack has sent shockwaves through diplomatic circles.

In his evening address, President Zelensky labelled the strike as 'vile' and a 'terrorist act,' emphasizing that targeting such a site demonstrates a blatant disregard for international safety protocols and the well-being of millions. He stressed that this act further underscores the urgent need for robust international support and air defence systems to protect Ukraine from further aggression.  The timing of the strike, so close to the London conference where Ukraine is seeking billions in aid for rebuilding and strengthening its resilience, is seen by Kyiv as a deliberate provocation.  Analysts suggest Russia may be attempting to sow further fear and instability, or perhaps test the international community's reaction to attacks on sensitive sites.  The international community is closely monitoring the situation, with many nations expressing deep concern and calling for a thorough investigation into the incident.
