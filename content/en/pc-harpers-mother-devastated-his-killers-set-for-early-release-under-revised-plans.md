The mother of PC Andrew Harper, who was brutally murdered in August 2019, has voiced her profound grief and anger following government announcements that could lead to the early release of his killers. Lisa Harper stated she is 'devastated' by the revised plans, which aim to restart an early release scheme for certain prisoners from October.  While the government insists that individuals convicted of rape and child sex offences will be explicitly barred from this scheme, the fact that those responsible for PC Harper's death could potentially benefit has caused immense pain.

PC Harper was tragically killed when he was dragged behind a car by a group of teenagers during a burglary investigation in Berkshire. The crime sent shockwaves across the nation, highlighting the dangers faced by frontline police officers. The killers, who were all under 18 at the time of the offence, were convicted of manslaughter and sentenced to a combined total of over 60 years.  However, under previous sentencing guidelines and now potentially revised ones, the possibility of early release, even for the most heinous crimes, remains a contentious issue.

Mrs. Harper has been a vocal advocate for justice and harsher penalties for those who harm police officers. She has previously spoken out against perceived leniency in sentencing and has campaigned for greater protection for law enforcement.  The latest news has brought her campaign back into sharp focus, with many supporting her call for perpetrators of such violent crimes against officers to face the full extent of the law without the prospect of early freedom. The government's clarification that serious sexual offenders will be excluded offers some solace, but for the Harper family, the underlying prospect of their son's killers being released sooner than expected is a source of deep distress.
