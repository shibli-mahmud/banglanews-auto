FIFA President Gianni Infantino has strongly refuted allegations that an alleged former lover received a substantial severance payment from UEFA during his time as the organization's general secretary. The claims, brought to light by a Daily Telegraph investigation, suggest that a payment was made to the woman in question, reportedly to settle matters related to an alleged affair.

Infantino, who led UEFA's football administration before transitioning to the helm of world football's governing body, has dismissed these accusations as baseless and a smear campaign. His representatives issued a statement asserting that all payments made by UEFA were handled through proper channels and in accordance with established procedures. The statement further emphasized that Infantino was not directly involved in the specific approval of such payments.

The Daily Telegraph report, however, cites sources and internal documents suggesting a different narrative, implying a degree of personal involvement and awareness on Infantino's part. The investigation raises questions about transparency and potential conflicts of interest within UEFA during his tenure. This latest controversy adds to a series of challenges and scrutiny that Infantino has faced since becoming FIFA President, including investigations into alleged corruption and ethical breaches.

UEFA itself has reportedly stated that it cannot comment on specific employment matters or past payments. However, the revelations have reignited debates about the governance and accountability within major footballing bodies. As more details emerge, the pressure on Infantino to provide further clarification and address these allegations directly is likely to intensify.
