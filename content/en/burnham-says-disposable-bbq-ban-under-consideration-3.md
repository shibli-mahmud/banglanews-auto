In a statement released earlier today, Prime Minister John Burnham revealed that the government is actively exploring the possibility of implementing a nationwide ban on disposable barbecues. The announcement comes in the wake of a series of devastating wildfires that have ravaged several parts of the country, exacerbated by unseasonably high temperatures and dry conditions.

Burnham stressed the urgency of the situation, stating, "We are asking the public, for now, not to use disposable barbecues." He highlighted the inherent risks associated with these portable grills, which are often discarded carelessly and can ignite dry vegetation, leading to widespread fires. "The current dry spell and high temperatures create a perfect storm for these fires to spread rapidly and cause immense damage to our natural landscapes and communities," the Prime Minister added.

Environmental groups and fire services have been vocal in their calls for stricter regulations on disposable barbecues, citing them as a frequent cause of outdoor fires, particularly in parks and nature reserves. While some local authorities have already imposed temporary bans, a national measure would provide a more comprehensive approach to mitigating the risks. The government is reportedly in consultation with various stakeholders, including industry representatives and environmental agencies, to assess the feasibility and potential impact of a ban. Details regarding the timeframe and scope of any potential ban are expected to be released following these consultations. The public is urged to exercise extreme caution and adhere to any existing fire safety regulations in their areas.
