Mayor of Greater Manchester, Andy Burnham, has refused to rule out the possibility of tax increases as a means to fund a sustainable solution for England's struggling social care system. Speaking amidst ongoing cross-party discussions aimed at reforming the sector, Burnham emphasized the urgent need for significant investment to address the deep-seated issues plaguing social care, including underfunding, workforce shortages, and an aging population's growing needs.

Burnham, a vocal critic of the current state of social care, has been a prominent advocate for a more robust and equitable system. He stressed that while difficult decisions are necessary, the current trajectory is unsustainable and risks further deterioration of services, impacting vulnerable individuals and their families. The proposed tax rises, he suggested, could be part of a broader funding package designed to provide long-term stability and improve the quality of care available.

The acceleration of the social care review by the government signifies a recognition of the severity of the problem. However, the debate over funding remains a central sticking point. Various options are reportedly being explored, but the prospect of raising taxes, a historically sensitive issue, highlights the scale of the financial challenge. Burnham's willingness to consider this path underscores his commitment to finding a workable solution, even if it proves politically unpopular. The coming weeks are expected to be crucial as discussions continue, with the aim of presenting a clear plan to Parliament.
