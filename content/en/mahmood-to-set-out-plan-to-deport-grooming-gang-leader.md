The calls for the deportation of Shabir Ahmed, a key figure in the notorious Rochdale grooming gang, have intensified following his early release from a prison sentence. Ahmed, who was convicted for his role in orchestrating the exploitation of vulnerable underage girls, has become a focal point for public anger and demands for stricter immigration enforcement. Sources close to the Home Office indicate that Home Secretary James Cleverly is preparing to unveil a comprehensive strategy aimed at deporting Ahmed, signaling a tough stance against individuals convicted of serious offenses, particularly those involving child exploitation.

The government has faced criticism for perceived leniency in dealing with foreign national offenders, and the case of Shabir Ahmed has amplified these concerns. Families of the victims and community leaders have been vocal in their demands for Ahmed's removal, arguing that his presence in the UK undermines justice and poses a continued risk. It is understood that the Home Office is exploring all available legal avenues to facilitate his deportation, despite potential complexities. The planned announcement is expected to outline the specific measures being taken, including any diplomatic efforts or legal challenges required to achieve this outcome. This move by the Home Secretary aims to demonstrate a commitment to protecting children and ensuring that those who inflict such profound harm are held accountable, even if it means expedited removal from the country.
