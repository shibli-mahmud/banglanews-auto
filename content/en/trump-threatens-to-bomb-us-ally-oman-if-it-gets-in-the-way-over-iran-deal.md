Former US President Donald Trump has dramatically escalated tensions with the Gulf nation of Oman, issuing a direct threat of bombing if Muscat interferes with US policy concerning Iran. The unprecedented warning, reportedly made in private conversations, signals a volatile approach to foreign relations that could destabilize the region. Trump’s alleged statement indicates a willingness to use extreme force if Oman's diplomatic efforts or national interests are perceived to conflict with Washington's objectives, particularly regarding the contentious Iran nuclear deal.

This threat comes at a time when both the United States and Oman have been independently engaged in efforts to ensure the safe passage of vessels through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical global oil transit chokepoint. The waterway is a vital artery for global energy supplies, and any disruption poses significant economic risks worldwide. Oman, known for its neutral foreign policy and its role as a mediator in regional disputes, has historically maintained diplomatic ties with both the US and Iran. Trump's aggressive stance appears to disregard Oman's established role and its strategic importance in maintaining regional stability.

The former president's rhetoric, if accurate, underscores a departure from conventional diplomatic engagement and raises serious concerns about the potential for heightened geopolitical instability. The international community will be closely watching how these developments unfold and the broader implications for Middle East security and global energy markets.
