Alan Milburn, who previously served as Secretary of State for Health and Work and Pensions, has delivered a stark warning about the state of youth employment and the efficacy of the UK's welfare provisions. Speaking at a recent policy forum, Milburn expressed profound disappointment that the government continues to spend significantly more on sustaining young people through welfare payments than on proactive measures to equip them with skills and secure them jobs or places in education.  He described this imbalance as 'shameful,' arguing that it perpetuates a cycle of dependency and fails to unlock the potential of a crucial segment of the population.

Milburn's commentary comes amidst persistent concerns over the number of young people categorised as 'economically inactive,' meaning they are neither employed nor in further education or training. He asserted that the current welfare framework, while providing a safety net, is not sufficiently geared towards fostering long-term economic independence and personal development for this demographic.  He called for a fundamental rethink of how resources are allocated, advocating for a shift towards substantial investment in tailored training programmes, apprenticeships, and support services designed to address the specific barriers faced by young jobseekers. The former minister stressed that such investments are not merely an expense but a crucial investment in the future prosperity of the nation.  He concluded by emphasizing that a comprehensive overhaul of the welfare system is imperative to ensure that young people are empowered to contribute meaningfully to society and the economy, rather than remaining reliant on state support.
