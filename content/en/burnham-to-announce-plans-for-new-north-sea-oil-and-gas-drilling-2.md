Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham is reportedly set to announce plans that would pave the way for new oil and gas drilling in the North Sea, a move that directly challenges the Labour Party's recently established policy. The 2024 general election manifesto explicitly pledged that a Labour government would not issue any new licenses for fossil fuel extraction. This potential divergence from the national party line is likely to generate significant debate and potentially expose rifts within the Labour ranks, particularly between frontbench policy and regional ambitions.

Burnham, a prominent figure within the Labour Party, has often championed policies that he believes will create jobs and secure energy supplies for the UK. However, his reported inclination towards supporting new North Sea exploration comes at a time when the urgency of the climate crisis is increasingly recognized by the scientific community and environmental activists. The contradiction between the manifesto's commitment to a green transition and proposals for continued fossil fuel extraction in sensitive marine environments could prove difficult to reconcile. 

Sources close to the Mayor suggest that the rationale behind the potential announcement centres on energy security and the economic benefits associated with the North Sea oil and gas industry, including job creation in the North West of England. Critics, however, are expected to argue that such a move undermines the UK's climate targets and its commitment to net-zero emissions. The announcement, if it proceeds, will undoubtedly place the Labour Party under intense scrutiny from both environmental groups and within its own parliamentary party.
