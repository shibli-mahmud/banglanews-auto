The narrative leading into the 2026 World Cup often focused on the individual brilliance of nations like France, boasting a constellation of footballing superstars. Yet, it was Spain, a team that has perhaps flown under the radar, who demonstrated the true power of collective effort in their commanding performance against the French. From the outset, Spain's tactical discipline was evident. Their midfield, a symphony of precise passing and intelligent movement, controlled the tempo of the game, suffocating France's usually potent attacks. Players like Gavi and Pedri orchestrated the play with an almost telepathic understanding, consistently finding pockets of space and exploiting the slightest French defensive lapse.

While France possessed moments of individual magic, their disjointed approach was no match for Spain's well-drilled unit. The Spanish defence, marshalled by a resolute Laporte, was rarely tested beyond speculative long shots. In attack, Spain's fluidity was a constant threat. Their wingers stretched the French defence wide, creating space for midfielders to surge forward and for the striker, Morata, to consistently be in dangerous positions. The goals, when they came, were not just moments of individual genius, but the culmination of intricate build-up play, showcasing the team's attacking prowess. This victory wasn't just about a favourable scoreline; it was a statement of intent, proving that in the theatre of the World Cup, a cohesive, tactically astute team can indeed triumph over a collection of brilliant, yet sometimes isolated, individuals. The question now hangs in the air: can this silky Spanish side continue their impressive run and go all the way to lift the coveted trophy?
