Peter Murrell, a pivotal figure within the SNP for many years, appeared at the High Court in Glasgow today and entered a guilty plea to charges of embezzlement. Prosecutors allege that Murrell, who stepped down as chief executive in March 2023 amid a police investigation into party finances, misappropriated funds totalling around £400,000.  The investigation, codenamed Operation Branchform, has been ongoing for over a year and has scrutinised the party's funding and donations.  Murrell's admission of guilt marks a significant turning point in the inquiry.  He was taken into custody immediately after his plea, with his sentencing deferred pending further reports.  The case has sent shockwaves through Scottish politics, given Murrell's long-standing influence and his close relationship with Nicola Sturgeon, who led the SNP and the Scottish government for nearly a decade.  While Sturgeon herself is not accused of any wrongdoing in this matter, her husband's plea is expected to cast a long shadow over the party and its recent history.  The SNP has stated it is cooperating fully with the ongoing investigation and has made no further comment on the specifics of the case.
