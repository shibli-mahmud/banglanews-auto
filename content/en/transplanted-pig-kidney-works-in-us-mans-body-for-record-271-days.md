In a remarkable feat of medical innovation, a 55-year-old man in the United States has lived for an unprecedented 271 days with a kidney transplanted from a genetically modified pig. The recipient, Richard Slayman, who had been suffering from end-stage kidney failure, received the organ in March 2023 at Massachusetts General Hospital. This duration significantly surpasses previous records for pig-to-human kidney transplants, offering immense hope for the millions worldwide struggling with organ shortages.

Slayman, who had previously received a human kidney transplant that failed after 18 years, opted for the experimental procedure. The pig kidney, provided by eGenesis, had undergone genetic modifications to reduce the risk of rejection and the transmission of porcine retroviruses. Doctors reported that the organ functioned well, producing urine and filtering waste products effectively, allowing Slayman to live without dialysis.

This latest success builds upon a series of advancements in xenotransplantation, the practice of transplanting organs between different species. While previous attempts at pig-to-human transplants have shown promise, the extended functionality observed in Slayman's case represents a crucial step forward. The research team believes this development could pave the way for a more readily available supply of organs for transplantation, potentially saving countless lives and alleviating the strain on existing organ donation systems.
