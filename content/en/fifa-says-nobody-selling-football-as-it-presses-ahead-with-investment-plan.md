Fifa remains steadfast in its commitment to its investment strategy, brushing aside the escalating tensions with Uefa. The global football association has reiterated its stance that the sport is not for sale, in direct response to concerns raised by Uefa, which fears that the proposed plans could lead to a commercialization that undermines the integrity and equitable distribution of football's revenue. Uefa has publicly declared its intention to boycott World Cup tournaments if Fifa does not retract its proposals, a move that would have profound implications for the sport's flagship event.

However, Fifa's leadership has emphasized that the consultation process is far from over. They aim to engage with all stakeholders, including national federations and continental confederations, to foster a collaborative approach. The governing body believes that its investment plan is crucial for the long-term development and growth of football globally, particularly in emerging markets. Despite the threat of a boycott, Fifa is proceeding with its discussions and seeking to build consensus. The organization insists on maintaining dialogue and transparency throughout the process, assuring that the best interests of football will be prioritized. The coming weeks are expected to be critical as both Fifa and Uefa navigate this complex dispute, with the future of international football competitions hanging in the balance.
