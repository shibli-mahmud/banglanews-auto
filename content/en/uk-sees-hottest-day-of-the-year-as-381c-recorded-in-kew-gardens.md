The nation experienced an unprecedented heatwave on Tuesday, with thermometers registering a blistering 38.1 degrees Celsius at Kew Gardens in London. This figure officially designates the day as the hottest recorded in the UK for 2023. The sweltering conditions prompted the Met Office to issue an amber extreme heat warning, which is currently in place for large swathes of England and is set to continue until midnight. 

The extreme heat has led to widespread disruption and warnings from health officials. The public has been advised to stay hydrated, avoid strenuous activity during the hottest parts of the day, and check on vulnerable individuals such as the elderly and those with pre-existing health conditions. The high temperatures have also placed a strain on public services, with reports of railway lines buckling under the heat and increased demand on the NHS. 

Authorities are urging people to be mindful of the risks associated with prolonged exposure to such high temperatures, including heatstroke and dehydration. The amber warning signifies a high likelihood of impacts on people and infrastructure. As the heatwave persists, emergency services are on high alert to respond to any heat-related incidents. Residents are encouraged to follow the guidance provided by the Met Office and local authorities to stay safe during this period of intense heat.
