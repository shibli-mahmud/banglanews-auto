Sir Keir Starmer has directly challenged Apple and Google, two of the world's most influential technology companies, to take immediate action in safeguarding children from accessing nude and sexually explicit imagery on their smartphones. The Labour leader has made it clear that he expects these firms to proactively activate existing built-in safety features designed to filter and block such content. This directive comes amidst growing concerns about the ease with which minors can encounter inappropriate material online, even on devices intended for their use.

Starmer's intervention signals a significant push for greater corporate responsibility in protecting vulnerable young people. The emphasis is on ensuring that safeguards are not merely an optional setting but are enabled by default, requiring parents to actively opt-out rather than opt-in to protection. This approach aims to shift the burden of protection towards the technology providers, arguing that they possess the technical capability to implement more robust controls.

The call to action highlights the ongoing debate surrounding online safety for children and the role of major tech platforms in this critical area. Critics have long argued that current measures are insufficient, leaving children exposed to harmful content. Starmer's stance suggests a potential legislative or regulatory approach if voluntary action by the companies is deemed inadequate. The expectation is that Apple and Google will demonstrate their commitment to child safety by embracing these stronger, default protections.
