Counter-terrorism officers have disrupted a potential attack targeting Manchester's Jewish community, with two men arrested in connection with the plot. The arrests were made on Sunday in the north-west of England, although specific details about the nature of the threat or the individuals involved have not yet been released by Greater Manchester Police. 

Assistant Chief Constable Tony Heywood of Greater Manchester Police stated that the arrests were made as a result of "intelligence-led operations." He emphasized that the investigation is ongoing and that "there is no immediate threat to the public." The arrests were part of a wider counter-terrorism investigation. 

Sources close to the investigation indicated that the men were arrested on suspicion of preparation for acts of terrorism. While the focus of the alleged plot was reportedly the Jewish community, the full scope of the investigation remains confidential. 

The Jewish Representative Council of Greater Manchester and North Wales acknowledged the arrests and expressed gratitude to the police for their swift action. A spokesperson for the council stated, "We are deeply concerned by these reports. We have been in contact with GMP and have been assured that they are doing everything they can to keep the community safe. We urge our community to remain vigilant and report any suspicious activity." 

This incident underscores the persistent threat of terrorism in the UK and the vital role of counter-terrorism units in preventing attacks. The authorities are urging anyone with information related to terrorism to contact the police.
