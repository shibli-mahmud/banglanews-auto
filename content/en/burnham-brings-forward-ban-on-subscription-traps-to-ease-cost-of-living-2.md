Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham has announced plans to expedite the introduction of a ban on 'subscription traps', a significant step in his ongoing campaign to address the UK's escalating cost of living crisis. These 'traps' are digital marketing tactics and website designs that deliberately obscure or complicate the process for consumers to cancel recurring subscriptions or memberships, often leading to unwanted charges.  Burnham's initiative aims to shield consumers from these deceptive practices, which can disproportionately affect those on lower incomes or who are less digitally savvy.

The accelerated ban is a key component of a broader UK tour where Burnham plans to unveil a series of proposals designed to provide tangible relief to households struggling with rising inflation, energy bills, and essential goods prices. By targeting subscription traps, the Mayor intends to put more money back into the pockets of consumers who are currently losing out due to opaque cancellation policies.  The move has been welcomed by consumer advocacy groups, who have long called for stronger regulations to protect individuals from unfair commercial practices.  The forthcoming legislation is expected to mandate clearer cancellation procedures, prominent opt-out options, and stricter enforcement mechanisms for businesses operating online.  This proactive stance by Burnham underscores a growing political awareness of how digital marketplaces can inadvertently harm consumers, and his commitment to fostering a fairer economic environment for all.
