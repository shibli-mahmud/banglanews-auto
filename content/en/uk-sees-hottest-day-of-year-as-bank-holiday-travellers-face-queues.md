The mercury climbed to 28.4 degrees Celsius in Chertsey, Surrey, on Friday, making it the hottest day of the year to date and significantly warmer than many Mediterranean holiday destinations. This late May heatwave has brought welcome sunshine for many but has also coincided with a surge in travel, leading to considerable congestion.

Authorities at the Port of Dover reported substantial queues for both ferries and the Eurotunnel, with delays expected to continue throughout the day as holidaymakers embark on trips to France and beyond. Similar scenes of disruption unfolded at Birmingham Airport, where passengers experienced lengthy waits for security checks and check-in desks.  Airlines have urged travellers to allow extra time for their journeys and to check for any updates regarding their flights.

The Met Office has forecast that the warm spell will persist over the weekend, with temperatures remaining in the mid-to-high 20s across much of the UK. While the high temperatures are a boon for those seeking to enjoy outdoor activities, health officials are reminding the public to stay hydrated, use sun protection, and be mindful of vulnerable individuals.  The unexpected heat also raises questions about the country's readiness for increasingly warm summers, with concerns about water resources and the impact on infrastructure.

Despite the travel frustrations, the unseasonably warm weather has provided a much-needed boost for outdoor hospitality venues, with pubs and restaurants anticipating busy periods. However, the focus for many remains on navigating the travel chaos to reach their destinations and make the most of the long weekend.
