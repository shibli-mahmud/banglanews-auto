Documents obtained by The Times newspaper reveal that Buckingham Palace received a significant trove of emails dating back to Prince Andrew's tenure as the UK's Special Representative for International Trade and Investment. The correspondence, numbering in the thousands, was reportedly handed over in 2020, amidst escalating scrutiny over the Duke of York's business affairs and his association with controversial figures, most notably the late convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

The emails are understood to shed light on Prince Andrew's extensive network of contacts and the nature of his financial dealings during his more than decade-long role as an envoy. His position, which he held until 2011, saw him travel extensively, promoting British trade and investment abroad. However, it also led to accusations that he used his royal connections for personal financial gain and forged relationships with individuals who later became embroiled in scandal.

The revelation that the Palace had these emails for several years before they were publicly disclosed raises further questions about the Royal Family's awareness of and handling of Prince Andrew's affairs. While the exact contents of the emails have not been detailed, their existence suggests that the Royal Household possessed information that could have provided insight into the Duke's controversial activities and potential conflicts of interest. This development comes at a time when Prince Andrew continues to face reputational damage following his settlement of a civil sex assault lawsuit brought against him in the United States.
