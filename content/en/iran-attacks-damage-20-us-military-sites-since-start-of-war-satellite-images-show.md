New findings from BBC Verify, based on an independent analysis of satellite imagery, indicate that Iran's missile and drone attacks since the beginning of the current regional conflict have affected approximately 20 US military sites. This number is substantially higher than the publicly acknowledged figures, suggesting a more extensive and widespread campaign of retaliation than has been officially reported.

Analysts, who reviewed the imagery, stated that while not all sites sustained critical damage, the sheer volume of impacts points to Tehran's considerable reach and intent. The strikes appear to have targeted bases and facilities utilized by the United States and its allies in various locations across the Middle East, underscoring the deepening regional instability.

The intelligence assessment, corroborated by the satellite evidence, suggests that Iran's objective may have been to demonstrate its retaliatory capability and to inflict a level of disruption on US operations without triggering an all-out war. The specific nature of the damage varies, from minor structural impacts to more significant disruptions at certain key installations. This revelation comes amid heightened diplomatic efforts to de-escalate the ongoing conflict and prevent a wider conflagration.

The extent of the damage, as revealed by this independent analysis, is likely to put further pressure on international actors to engage in robust de-escalation talks. The implications for regional security and the future of US military presence in the area remain a significant concern for policymakers and analysts alike.
