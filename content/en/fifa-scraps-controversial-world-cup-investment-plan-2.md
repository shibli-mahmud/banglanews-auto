Zurich – FIFA has officially withdrawn its proposal to divest minority stakes in its major tournaments, a move that had generated considerable debate and criticism within the football community. The plan, spearheaded by President Gianni Infantino, aimed to raise substantial funds through private equity investment, potentially allowing for greater financial stability and investment in football development globally. However, the prospect of outside commercial entities gaining influence over the World Cup and other flagship events proved deeply unpopular.

Critics voiced concerns about the potential dilution of football's integrity, the risk of commercial interests overriding sporting decisions, and the long-term implications for the democratic governance of the sport. Numerous national football associations, fan groups, and even some commercial partners expressed reservations, creating a unified front of opposition. This widespread dissent appears to have prompted FIFA's leadership to reconsider the initiative.

In a statement released earlier today, FIFA confirmed the suspension of the investment plan, citing the need to "ensure a consensus-driven approach to the future commercialization of our events." The organization emphasized its commitment to safeguarding the interests of football and its stakeholders. While the exact details of the opposition and the internal discussions remain confidential, the U-turn signals a significant victory for those advocating for a more traditional and less commercially driven model for football's governance. The future commercial strategy of FIFA is now expected to be re-evaluated, with a greater focus on inclusivity and consultation with its member associations.
