A significant controversy is brewing around Prince Andrew's property dealings, as a public spending watchdog is said to have raised concerns regarding the management of his official residence and associated properties. Reports indicate that the National Audit Office (NAO), an independent body responsible for scrutinising public spending, has been examining the financial arrangements at the Royal Lodge in Windsor Great Park. 

Central to these concerns are allegations that Prince Andrew may have been sub-letting cottages located on the Royal Lodge grounds. These properties, while part of the Crown Estate, are typically allocated for the use of royal family members and their staff. The watchdog's investigation is reportedly focusing on whether these arrangements were transparent and financially beneficial to the public purse. 

Details are emerging that suggest some of the cottages, which are not typically considered primary residences, may have been rented out to third parties. This practice, if confirmed, could raise questions about the appropriate use of royal residences and the financial oversight involved. The Royal Lodge itself, a Grade II listed building, has been Prince Andrew's principal residence for many years, with a significant proportion of its upkeep reportedly covered by taxpayer funds through the Sovereign Grant. 

The NAO's findings are expected to shed light on the exact nature of these sub-letting arrangements and their financial implications. The revelations come at a time when public scrutiny of royal finances and property management remains high, particularly in the wake of various controversies involving members of the Royal Family. Further details are anticipated as the watchdog's report is formally released, potentially prompting further debate on the governance of royal estates.
