Detectives from the counter-terrorism unit, who were already engaged in a sensitive investigation related to the passing of former Member of Parliament Ann Widdecombe, have reportedly widened their scope to include an incident at the home of prominent Brexit figure Nigel Farage. According to information obtained by the BBC, this new line of inquiry focuses on an alleged attempted burglary that occurred at Mr. Farage's property approximately a year ago.

While the exact nature of the connection between the two investigations remains undisclosed, the decision to revisit the Farage residence incident suggests a potential link or a perceived threat that warrants further scrutiny by specialist police units. Sources close to the matter indicate that the initial investigation into Ms. Widdecombe's death has led investigators to uncover new information that prompted the reopening of the case at Mr. Farage's home. It is understood that the reopened investigation is not directly related to Ms. Widdecombe's death itself, but rather that the broader inquiry has uncovered evidence suggesting a separate criminal incident at Mr. Farage's property.

Details surrounding the attempted burglary are scarce, and authorities have yet to issue an official statement confirming the reopened investigation or its specific parameters. However, the involvement of counter-terrorism police highlights the seriousness with which the incident is being treated. Mr. Farage, a highly public figure, has been the subject of both support and considerable opposition throughout his political career, which may be a factor considered by investigators. The police are expected to provide further updates as the investigation progresses.
