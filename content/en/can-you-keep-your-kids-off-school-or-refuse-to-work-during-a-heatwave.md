As the mercury climbs and a severe heatwave grips the nation, many parents and employees are wondering about their rights and responsibilities. When extreme temperatures pose a health risk, questions inevitably arise: can children be kept home from school, and can employees refuse to work?

Schools typically have policies in place for severe weather. While not always a blanket closure, if temperatures inside classrooms become dangerously high and ventilation is inadequate, parents may have grounds to keep their children home. It's advisable to check your specific school district's guidelines or contact the school administration directly. Some jurisdictions may have specific mandates regarding school closures during heatwaves, prioritizing student welfare. However, absence policies will likely still apply, so proactive communication with the school is key.

For employees, the situation is more nuanced. In many regions, there isn't a specific law dictating automatic work stoppages due to heatwaves, unless working conditions become immediately dangerous. Employers have a duty of care to ensure a safe working environment. This might include providing adequate ventilation, access to water, and breaks in cooler areas. If your workplace becomes dangerously hot and these measures are not in place, you may have grounds to raise concerns or even refuse work if it poses an imminent risk to your health. Consulting your employment contract, union representative, or relevant labor laws in your area is recommended. Documenting the conditions and your concerns can also be beneficial. Prioritizing health and safety is paramount during these extreme weather events.
