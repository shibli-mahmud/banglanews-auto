Jerusalem, Israel – In a firm stance against a proposed peace initiative, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu stated today that the Israeli Defense Forces (IDF) will maintain their presence in the Gaza Strip until the militant group Hamas is completely disarmed. This declaration comes in response to reports of a 15-point plan for Gaza, allegedly championed by former US President Donald Trump, aimed at de-escalating the ongoing conflict.  Netanyahu's office clarified that while Israel is open to discussions regarding post-conflict scenarios, the immediate priority remains the dismantling of Hamas's military capabilities. He emphasized that any withdrawal of Israeli troops is contingent upon the 'genuine' disarmament of the organization, ensuring that Israel's security concerns are fully addressed. The Prime Minister's office did not provide specific details about the Trump plan but indicated that it did not align with Israel's essential security requirements.  The rejection signals a continuation of Israel's current military operations in Gaza, which have been ongoing since the October 7th attacks by Hamas.  International efforts to broker a lasting ceasefire and a comprehensive peace agreement have faced numerous hurdles, with differing objectives between regional actors and key international players. Netanyahu's pronouncement suggests that a significant shift in Israel's security posture in Gaza is unlikely in the near future, pending demonstrable progress in neutralizing Hamas's threat.
