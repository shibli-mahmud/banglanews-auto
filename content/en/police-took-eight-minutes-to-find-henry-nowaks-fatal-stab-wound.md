Henry Nowak, a student at the University of Southampton, succumbed to his injuries following an incident that led to his apprehension by Hampshire police. Reports indicate that officers were on the scene and had Nowak in custody, handcuffed and on the ground, for approximately eight minutes before the severity and location of his fatal stab wound were identified. This critical delay in recognizing the life-threatening injury has sparked concern and is now the subject of an internal review.

While the exact circumstances leading to Nowak's stab wound remain under investigation, the timeline of the police response is a central point of focus. Eyewitness accounts and preliminary reports suggest that despite the presence of multiple officers, the wound was not immediately apparent or addressed. It was only after a significant period that the gravity of his condition became clear, by which time it was tragically too late to save his life. 

The university community is in mourning following the devastating loss. Friends and fellow students have described Nowak as a bright and promising individual. The incident has also prompted discussions within the local community and among policing experts regarding the protocols for assessing individuals in police custody, especially when there are indications of severe injury. Hampshire police have stated that they are cooperating fully with the investigation into the events, emphasizing their commitment to understanding how this tragedy unfolded and to ensuring that appropriate procedures are followed in all their operations. The internal review aims to scrutinize the actions of the officers involved and identify any potential shortcomings in their training or immediate response procedures.
