In a bid to create safer journeys for passengers, the Department for Transport is launching a "scorecard" system that will evaluate train operating companies on their effectiveness in addressing sexual harassment. This initiative marks a significant step towards holding the industry accountable for the well-being of its passengers.  The government believes that by making performance metrics public, train firms will be incentivized to invest more resources and implement more robust strategies to prevent and respond to incidents of sexual harassment.  This could include enhanced staff training, improved reporting mechanisms for passengers, and more visible security presence on services.  While the immediate threat of financial or legal repercussions for underperforming companies is not part of the initial rollout, the transparency of the scoring system is intended to exert considerable pressure. Reputational damage and potential loss of customer trust are anticipated to be strong motivators for improvement.  Industry insiders suggest that a low score could impact franchise negotiations and public perception, indirectly influencing commercial outcomes.  Campaign groups have welcomed the move but are urging the government to consider stronger enforcement measures if the scorecard proves insufficient in driving meaningful change.  The focus, for now, is on fostering a culture of responsibility and proactive prevention within the rail network, with the ultimate goal of making train travel a secure and comfortable experience for everyone.
