The National Drought Group, comprising water companies, environmental agencies, and government representatives, has officially declared a drought across all of Wales. This move signifies the gravity of the situation, highlighting the sustained lack of rainfall and its cascading impacts. Rivers are running at critically low levels, reservoirs are significantly depleted, and groundwater sources are under immense pressure.  The dry spell has been prolonged, with little respite in sight for the coming weeks.  The agricultural sector is particularly hard-hit, facing crop failures and challenges in providing adequate water for livestock. Farmers are being urged to implement water-saving measures and seek advice on drought resilience.  Beyond agriculture, the environmental consequences are also a major concern. Wildlife habitats are suffering, with potential impacts on biodiversity.  Water companies are implementing hosepipe bans and urging the public to be extremely mindful of their water usage.  Non-essential uses, such as watering gardens and washing cars, are being discouraged.  The declaration of a drought is a stark reminder of the vulnerability of water supplies to changing climatic conditions and underscores the need for long-term strategies to manage water resources more sustainably.  Further updates on the evolving situation and recommended actions are expected in the coming days.
