The highly anticipated solar eclipse is fast approaching, and with many retailers reporting sold-out eclipse glasses, a sense of urgency has set in. However, concerned viewers need not despair. The sun's dazzling display can be observed safely using indirect viewing methods that don't require looking directly at the sun, even without certified eclipse glasses. These techniques are simple, accessible, and crucially, protect your eyes from the harmful ultraviolet and infrared radiation that the sun emits, which can cause permanent eye damage.

One of the most popular and straightforward methods is the pinhole projector. This can be easily constructed using two pieces of white paper or cardstock. On one piece, create a small, clean hole (about the size of a pinhead) in the center. During the eclipse, hold the paper with the hole above your head, allowing sunlight to pass through it and project an image of the eclipsed sun onto the second piece of paper held below. The projected image will show a crescent shape as the moon begins to cover the sun. Another effective technique involves using a colander. By holding a colander up to the sun, the small holes will act as multiple pinholes, projecting numerous small images of the eclipsed sun onto a flat surface, such as the ground or a piece of paper. This method offers a wider view of the projected eclipse.

For those with a mirror, a simple solar eclipse viewer can be made. Take a small mirror and place it in a box or a large envelope, with the reflective side facing upwards. Cut a hole in the box or envelope so that sunlight can hit the mirror. The mirror will then reflect the projected image of the sun onto a surface inside the box or on a nearby wall. This indirect projection method ensures your eyes are shielded from direct solar rays. Remember, never look directly at the sun without proper protection, even during an eclipse. These DIY methods offer a fantastic and safe alternative for everyone to enjoy the wonder of the solar eclipse.
