Middlesbrough's Longlands ward, once a relatively peaceful neighbourhood, is now shrouded in an atmosphere of fear and distrust. The fatal crash on the A66, which claimed the lives of three young men last month, has served as a catalyst for an explosion of violence and anti-social behaviour, according to residents.  The sense of unease is palpable, with many locals recounting instances of intimidation and aggression that have become commonplace.  "We don't feel safe anymore," stated one resident, who wished to remain anonymous for fear of reprisal. "The streets are full of young people causing trouble, and nobody seems to be doing anything about it."

The deep-seated distrust of the police is a recurring theme. For some, the perceived lack of effective response to their concerns has eroded any faith they once had in law enforcement. "I'd rather deal with it myself, or just keep my head down, than ring the police," admitted another resident. "What's the point? They don't listen, and it often makes things worse for us."

This sentiment of being left to fend for themselves has created a fertile ground for vigilante attitudes and a cycle of retaliation. Local community leaders are struggling to bridge the gap between frustrated residents and the authorities. They acknowledge the escalating problems but are also hamstrung by the community's reluctance to cooperate with official channels. The fatal A66 crash, while a tragedy in itself, has exposed underlying tensions and vulnerabilities within Middlesbrough, leaving a community grappling with fear and a profound sense of abandonment.
