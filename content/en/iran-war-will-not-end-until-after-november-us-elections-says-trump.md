Donald Trump, the presumptive Republican nominee for the upcoming US presidential election, has publicly stated that he believes a resolution to the current tensions involving Iran is contingent upon the outcome of the November elections. "This war with Iran will not end until after the November US elections," Trump declared, articulating a timeline that places the resolution of the conflict firmly in the post-election period.  This statement implies a strategic consideration of the international landscape and its potential impact on domestic political events.

Adding another layer to his pronouncements, the former president also addressed the issue of oil prices. He posited that global oil prices are unlikely to see a substantial decline until after the November races have concluded. This assertion suggests a belief that current market dynamics or geopolitical factors influenced by the election cycle are keeping prices elevated.  Trump did not provide specific data or economic analyses to support this claim, but it aligns with a broader narrative that international events can be manipulated for electoral advantage.

Furthermore, Trump directly accused Iran of seeking to interfere with or influence the American electoral process. "Iran wants to impact the election," he stated without offering any concrete evidence or intelligence to substantiate this assertion.  This accusation, if true, would represent a significant geopolitical concern, highlighting potential foreign interference in the democratic process. However, in the absence of supporting evidence, these statements remain as strong political rhetoric from a figure known for his direct and often provocative communication style. The pronouncements have undoubtedly fueled further discussion on the interconnectedness of international affairs and US domestic politics.
