The judicial process in the high-profile Ramisa rape and murder case has taken a dramatic turn with the prime accused, Sohel Rana, implicating a new individual in his testimony. During the recent framing of charges, Rana reportedly mentioned a man known only by the moniker 'Dollar,' suggesting his involvement in the heinous crime. This unexpected disclosure has sent ripples through the legal fraternity and has the potential to reopen lines of inquiry that were previously considered closed.

The case, which has garnered significant public attention due to its brutal nature and the age of the victim, has already seen several twists and turns. The initial investigation focused on a limited number of suspects, and the evidence presented thus far has primarily pointed towards Sohel Rana. However, the introduction of 'Dollar' into the narrative by the prime accused raises crucial questions about the extent of the conspiracy and the possibility of other perpetrators.

Legal experts are closely observing the implications of this new naming. The prosecution will now face the challenge of substantiating Rana's claims and potentially investigating the whereabouts and identity of this 'Dollar.' Defense lawyers might leverage this to argue for a broader investigation, potentially seeking to cast doubt on the existing evidence or to introduce the idea of external pressure on their client. The court's decision on whether to formally include 'Dollar' as a suspect and to pursue leads related to him will be a critical juncture in the ongoing trial. The outcome of this case, already deeply scrutinized, now hinges on how effectively these new allegations are addressed and integrated into the judicial proceedings.
