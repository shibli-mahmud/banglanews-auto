David Hockney, celebrated as one of the most influential British artists of the post-war generation, passed away peacefully at his home at the age of 88. Born in Bradford in 1937, Hockney rose to international prominence in the 1960s with his distinctive Pop Art style, characterized by bold colours and a playful approach to subject matter. His iconic "A Bigger Splash" (1967) remains a defining image of that decade, epitomizing the sun-drenched optimism and glamorous lifestyle of Los Angeles.

Throughout his extensive career, Hockney proved to be a remarkably versatile artist, refusing to be confined to a single medium or style. He explored portraiture, still life, and landscape with equal passion, famously relocating to his native Yorkshire in the 1990s to paint the rolling hills and dramatic weather of the English countryside. These latter works, often executed in vibrant acrylics and even incorporating digital drawing tools, demonstrated his enduring commitment to observation and his desire to capture the essence of his surroundings.

Hockney was also a pioneer in his embrace of technology. He experimented with fax machines, photocopiers, and later, iPads, to create art, demonstrating a forward-thinking approach that challenged traditional notions of artistic tools. His exhibitions were consistently major events, drawing large crowds and critical acclaim worldwide. He leaves behind a rich and diverse body of work that has profoundly shaped contemporary art and inspired countless artists. His legacy is one of innovation, joy, and an unwavering dedication to the act of seeing.
