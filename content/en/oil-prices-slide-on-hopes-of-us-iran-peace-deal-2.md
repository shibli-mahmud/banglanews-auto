The international benchmark Brent crude fell sharply in early trading on Monday, shedding over 2%, while West Texas Intermediate (WTI) also saw considerable losses. This decline is largely attributed to the perception that a de-escalation in US-Iran relations would ease supply concerns and reduce the risk of disruptions to crude shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil trade. President Trump's remarks, made on Saturday, hinted at a breakthrough without elaborating on the specific terms of the proposed deal. However, the mere prospect of renewed diplomatic engagement has been enough to significantly alter market sentiment. Analysts suggest that if an agreement is indeed reached and the Strait is reopened without impediment, it could lead to a substantial increase in global oil supply, potentially pushing prices lower in the medium term. The situation remains fluid, with close monitoring of any further developments from both Washington and Tehran. Investors and traders will be keenly watching for concrete details and signs of progress to gauge the long-term impact on oil markets.
