The vital ferry terminal at the Port of Dover was brought to a standstill for several hours today, as a demonstration organized by a group calling themselves 'stop the boats' escalated into road blockades. Protesters, many wearing masks, deliberately obstructed the routes leading to the port, preventing vehicles from entering or leaving.

The demonstration, which appeared to be aimed at highlighting opposition to the government's immigration policies, specifically concerning small boat crossings, caused extensive tailbacks stretching for miles. Commuters, holidaymakers, and freight drivers were all caught in the gridlock, leading to cancelled sailings and significant delays for passengers and goods.

Authorities were quickly on the scene, with police working to clear the roads and make arrests. While the exact number of protesters and arrests is yet to be confirmed, the disruption was substantial. The incident has sparked widespread debate about the tactics used by activist groups and the impact of such protests on essential infrastructure and the public.

In response to the unfolding situation, a government spokesperson issued a statement condemning the "behaviour in Dover" and the disruption it has caused. The statement emphasized that while peaceful protest is a democratic right, actions that impede public safety and disrupt critical services are unacceptable and will not be tolerated. The port has since partially reopened, but significant delays are expected to persist throughout the day as operations return to normal.
