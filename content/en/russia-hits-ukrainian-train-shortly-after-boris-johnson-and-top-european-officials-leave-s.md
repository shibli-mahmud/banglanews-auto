A train station in Ukraine's eastern region was targeted by Russian missiles on Saturday, an attack that occurred mere minutes after a delegation of international leaders had concluded their visit and departed the premises. Among the high-profile figures present were former British Prime Minister Boris Johnson, along with leaders from Poland and other European nations, who were on a solidarity visit to Kyiv.

Initial reports from Ukrainian officials indicated that several projectiles struck the station, causing damage to infrastructure and igniting fires. Fortunately, no casualties were reported among the civilians at the station at the time of the attack, as the visiting delegation had already left the area. However, the incident has intensified concerns over the safety and security of international figures engaging in diplomatic visits to Ukraine amidst the ongoing conflict.

Former Prime Minister Boris Johnson, speaking from a nearby location shortly after the incident, expressed his shock and condemned the attack. "I don't know what warped logic drove Putin to blow up a stationary Ukrainian locomotive on the Polish border this morning," Johnson stated, though clarifying the attack occurred in Ukraine. He emphasized that such actions only serve to strengthen the resolve of those supporting Ukraine. The precise target and intent behind the strike remain under investigation, but the timing strongly suggests a deliberate act aimed at disrupting or intimidating the visiting dignitaries or signaling a broader disregard for international engagement.

This event underscores the volatile nature of the conflict and the continued risks faced by both Ukrainian civilians and international visitors. The Ukrainian government has reiterated its calls for increased air defense capabilities, citing such attacks as proof of Russia's disregard for civilian lives and diplomatic efforts. The international community is expected to condemn the strike and reiterate its support for Ukraine's sovereignty and territorial integrity.
