Downing Street is said to be under mounting pressure from a significant faction of the Labour party, encompassing both backbench Members of Parliament and government ministers, to publicly announce a concrete timetable for the Prime Minister's exit. Whispers within the party suggest a growing consensus that a clear roadmap is now essential to restore stability and provide direction. The BBC has learned that the Transport Secretary is among those advocating for such a move, believing it would be beneficial for the party and the country to have a defined timeline.

This internal push comes at a time of heightened political scrutiny, with opposition parties and segments of the public also calling for clarity on the Prime Minister's future. The lack of a declared exit strategy has been cited as a contributing factor to a perceived lack of decisiveness and a potential impediment to addressing pressing national issues. Allies of the Prime Minister are reportedly working to manage these internal dissentions, while proponents of an early departure argue that announcing a timetable would allow for a more orderly transition and prevent prolonged speculation. The coming days are expected to reveal whether these calls for a defined exit plan will translate into a public commitment from the Prime Minister's office.
