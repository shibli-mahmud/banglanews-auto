Wildfires have been scorching the Gironde department for several days, fuelled by dry conditions and high temperatures. The situation escalated dramatically on Tuesday when authorities issued an order for the complete evacuation of the entire Cap Ferret peninsula. This popular area, known for its stunning beaches, pine forests, and oyster farming traditions, is now a scene of airborne firefighting efforts and a desperate exodus of people.  Over 10,000 people, including tourists and locals, have been compelled to leave their properties, many with little notice.  Temporary accommodation centres have been set up in nearby towns to house the displaced population. Firefighters from across France, and even international support, are on the ground battling the inferno, which has already destroyed significant swathes of natural landscape and threatened properties. The dense pine forests, a signature feature of the region, are highly flammable, making containment a formidable challenge.  Officials have warned that the fire is difficult to control and urged continued vigilance. The economic impact on the vital tourism sector is expected to be substantial, with cancellations already mounting. The long-term ecological damage to the protected natural areas is also a major concern, adding to the gravity of the situation unfolding at this beloved French destination.
