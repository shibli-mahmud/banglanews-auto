The Three Lions' highly anticipated knockout tie against Mexico, originally slated for an earlier slot, will now commence in the early hours of Monday morning at 01:00 BST. The dramatic shift in schedule, confirmed by FIFA after initial considerations to bring the match forward, has sent ripples through hospitality venues and policing bodies nationwide. 

Pubs, which had initially prepared for a mid-afternoon or early evening kick-off, are now scrambling to adjust their staffing and licensing arrangements to accommodate the late-night audience. Many establishments are anticipating a significant influx of fans eager to cheer on their national team in this crucial World Cup fixture. However, the extended operating hours present logistical challenges, including ensuring adequate security and managing potential public disorder.

Law enforcement agencies are also stepping up their presence and planning for a heightened demand on services. With the match extending well into the early hours, concerns about drunk driving and anti-social behaviour are at the forefront of police preparations. Commuters and residents in areas with high concentrations of pubs are being advised of potential disruptions. 

This late kick-off, a consequence of FIFA's reconsideration of the tournament's timetable, places an unusual demand on fans and the services supporting them. While the revised timing might be driven by broader tournament considerations, the immediate impact is a late night for England supporters and a significant logistical undertaking for those tasked with ensuring public safety and facilitating the collective experience of watching the match.
