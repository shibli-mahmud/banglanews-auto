The leadership of the Labour party has a new, familiar face. As Keir Starmer steps down, Andy Burnham is stepping up, becoming the fifth leader of the opposition in the space of four years. This rapid turnover has become a concerning trend for Labour, reflecting internal divisions and a persistent struggle to connect with the wider electorate. Burnham, however, is no stranger to the challenges of leadership. His tenure as Mayor of Greater Manchester has been marked by a distinct and often lauded style – one of pragmatic delivery and a focus on the devolved powers of regional government.

His supporters argue that this hands-on, Manchester-centric approach is precisely what Labour needs. Burnham has carved out a reputation for being a fighter for his region, securing significant funding and devolution deals. This ability to deliver tangible results for the people he represents is seen as a strength that could resonate across the country. The former Health Secretary, who has long harbored ambitions for national leadership, will now attempt to translate this success onto the national stage. His challenge will be to broaden his appeal beyond his loyal Northern base and convince a diverse electorate that he offers a credible alternative to the current government. The question remains: can he break the cycle of short-lived leadership and guide Labour back to power, or will he be another name in a growing list of opposition leaders who couldn't buck the trend?
