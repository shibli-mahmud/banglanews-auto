A woman, whose identity is being protected, has spoken out to the BBC about a terrifying experience at a Travelodge hotel, alleging that hotel staff inadvertently facilitated further abuse by providing her domestic abuser with access to her private room. The survivor recounted how her attacker, after being denied entry, was subsequently given a key by reception, allowing him to gain access to her room.

The incident escalated dramatically when the abuser, now able to enter the room, reportedly "kicked the door off its hinges." This act of violence not only caused significant damage to the hotel property but also exposed the victim to extreme fear and further physical danger. The woman described the moment she realised he had a key as "a nightmare unfolding" and expressed her shock and disbelief that hotel staff would act in a way that put her safety at such grave risk.

The BBC's report highlights a severe lapse in hotel security protocols and staff training, raising serious questions about how such a breach could occur. While Travelodge has not yet provided a detailed public statement on this specific incident, hotels are generally expected to have strict procedures in place to prevent unauthorized access to guest rooms, particularly when concerns about safety or a history of abuse are known or suspected. The victim's account serves as a stark reminder of the critical importance of robust security measures and the devastating consequences when these fail, especially for vulnerable individuals seeking refuge.
