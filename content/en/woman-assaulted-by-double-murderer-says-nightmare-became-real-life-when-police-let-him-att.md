The victim, whose identity has been protected, described the profound trauma of being attacked by Simon Levy, a man who would later be convicted of murdering two women and raping a third. Her ordeal was compounded by the agonizing realization that her assailant was not only free but allegedly allowed to commit further acts of violence after initial encounters with the justice system. 'It felt like a nightmare that became real life,' she stated in an interview, her voice trembling with the residual fear of her experience.

Levy, a registered sex offender, had a history of violent offenses. However, a series of what her statement refers to as 'disastrous decisions' by law enforcement and judicial bodies meant he was not adequately monitored or detained, despite evidence of his escalating dangerousness. This alleged systemic failure allowed him to continue to pose a significant threat to the public. The victim's account highlights a critical breakdown in the system designed to protect citizens from dangerous individuals. Her testimony serves as a stark reminder of the devastating consequences when safeguarding measures fail, leaving victims re-traumatized and raising serious questions about accountability and the effectiveness of current protocols for managing high-risk offenders. The case has ignited calls for a thorough review of how such individuals are handled, with a focus on preventing future tragedies.
