David Sullivan, a prominent figure in West Ham United's ownership, has made the decision to step down from his roles as joint chairman and director of the Premier League club. The announcement, made with immediate effect, comes as Sullivan has been made aware of "serious historic allegations" that are reportedly set to be published.  While the specific nature of these allegations has not yet been detailed by the club or Sullivan's representatives, their seriousness has prompted this swift action.

Sullivan, along with his business partner David Gold, purchased a controlling stake in West Ham in 2010, ushering in a new era for the East London club. His tenure has been marked by significant developments, including the controversial move to the London Stadium and various managerial changes. The allegations, though unspecific at this stage, cast a shadow over his long-standing involvement with the club.

West Ham United has issued a brief statement acknowledging Sullivan's departure, stating that the decision was taken after the club was informed of the impending publication of these serious historical claims. No further information regarding the allegations or the timeline for their release has been provided. The club is expected to release further statements in due course as the situation develops. This unexpected resignation leaves a significant void in the club's leadership and is likely to be a major talking point among the fanbase and within the wider football community.
