Bangladesh, a nation highly vulnerable to the impacts of climate change, stands at a critical juncture regarding its energy policy. The country's current energy mix is heavily dominated by fossil fuels, primarily natural gas, which is becoming increasingly scarce and expensive. This dependency not only exacerbates air pollution and greenhouse gas emissions, contributing to the global climate crisis, but also poses a significant threat to national energy security. Fluctuations in global fuel prices directly impact the national economy, leading to increased import bills and potential energy shortages.

The argument for a swift pivot to renewable energy – solar, wind, and hydro – is compelling. Solar energy, in particular, holds immense potential given Bangladesh's abundant sunshine. Despite initial investment costs, renewable energy sources offer long-term cost savings, price stability, and a cleaner environment. Furthermore, embracing renewables can foster technological innovation, create green jobs, and enhance energy independence. International climate finance and technological advancements are making the transition more feasible than ever. Delayed action risks entrenching unsustainable practices and missing out on the economic and environmental benefits that a green energy future promises. The time to act is now to secure a resilient and sustainable Bangladesh.
