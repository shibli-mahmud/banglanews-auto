Earl Spencer has stood firm in his account of Princess Diana's alleged conversation with Prince Charles, asserting his integrity in a recent BBC interview. The book, 'A Very Private Grief,' details the emotional turmoil Diana experienced following her separation and divorce from the then-Prince of Wales. A key passage in the book, and the focus of much debate, recounts a conversation where Charles supposedly told Diana that the public would "forget her soon enough" after their marital breakdown. This claim has been met with strong denials from royal sources, but Earl Spencer remains resolute.

"I'm telling the truth," Spencer stated unequivocally to Laura Kuenssberg, the BBC's political editor. He elaborated on the profound hurt and sense of abandonment Diana felt, suggesting that such a remark from Charles would have amplified these feelings. Spencer's defense is rooted in his close relationship with his sister and his access to her personal accounts and perspectives. He emphasized that his motive in writing the book is to present a more accurate and nuanced portrayal of Diana, moving beyond the sensationalism that often surrounded her life.

He expressed his concern that Diana's memory might fade over time, a sentiment he believes Diana herself feared. The interview underscored the ongoing sensitivity surrounding the royal family's past actions and relationships, particularly concerning Princess Diana, who remains a figure of global fascination. Spencer's willingness to publicly defend his controversial claims highlights his commitment to what he perceives as his sister's legacy and his frustration with what he views as attempts to sanitize the historical narrative.
