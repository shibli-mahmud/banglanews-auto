The scars of the attack, which claimed the lives of approximately 120 children, are still visible in the eyes of the survivors in Minab, Iran. Nearly five months have passed since the tragic incident, yet the pain is as fresh as if it happened yesterday. A BBC investigation has brought to light the personal testimonies of those who experienced the horror firsthand, offering a glimpse into the profound grief that continues to grip the community.

A teacher, her voice trembling with emotion, recounted the chaos and terror that unfolded that fateful day. She spoke of the desperate attempts to shield the children and the agonizing moments of realization as the scale of the tragedy became apparent. "What was their crime?" she questioned, her voice choked with anguish, referring to the innocent lives cut short. The questions of culpability and justice hang heavy in the air, with families still searching for answers and accountability.

A mother, who miraculously survived the strike, shared her heart-wrenching story of loss. She spoke of the unimaginable pain of losing her child and the constant ache of emptiness that has filled her life since. Her narrative painted a vivid picture of the vibrant young lives that were extinguished, emphasizing that they were merely children with futures ahead of them. The report underscores the immense human cost of such violence, highlighting the devastating impact on individuals, families, and the wider community. The BBC's visit to the school, now a somber reminder of the tragedy, aims to shed light on the enduring impact of the event and to give voice to those who have suffered so immensely.
