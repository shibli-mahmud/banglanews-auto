In a significant move towards promoting sustainable transport and public health, UK ministers have announced a bold target: to have 60% of all primary and secondary school children actively travelling to school, either by walking or cycling, within the next decade. This ambitious goal, set to be achieved by 2035, is part of a broader national strategy to combat childhood obesity, reduce carbon emissions, and create more liveable urban environments. 

The Department for Transport has pledged substantial investment in infrastructure improvements designed to make active travel safer and more appealing. This includes plans to create thousands of new 'school streets' – temporary or permanent road closures around schools during drop-off and pick-up times – to deter car use and provide a safer space for children. Furthermore, the investment will focus on expanding and improving existing walking and cycling paths, ensuring they are well-maintained, direct, and accessible for all children, regardless of their location. 

Key to the success of this initiative will be the development of a comprehensive network of safe crossings, particularly in areas with high traffic volumes. Ministers are reportedly exploring innovative solutions, such as raised crossings and improved signage, to give parents and children greater confidence when navigating busy roads. Educational programmes promoting road safety and the benefits of active travel are also expected to be a significant component of the rollout. The government hopes that by making these changes, it will not only encourage a generational shift towards healthier habits but also contribute to a reduction in traffic congestion and air pollution in school catchments.
