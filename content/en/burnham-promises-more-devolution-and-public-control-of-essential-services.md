Andy Burnham, the newly installed Prime Minister, has pledged to accelerate the process of devolution, promising to transfer more decision-making powers from the central government to regional and local authorities. His vision centers on empowering communities to have a greater say in the services that directly impact their lives, from transport and housing to healthcare and education.  This move, Burnham argued, is essential for creating a more responsive and effective public sector, tailored to the specific needs of different regions across the country.

Central to his agenda is the concept of bringing key essential services back under public control. Burnham believes that privatized utilities and public services have often prioritized profit over people, leading to diminished quality and accessibility. He aims to reverse this trend by exploring various models for public ownership and community stewardship, ensuring that services like water, energy, and public transport are run for the benefit of the public good, not private gain. This approach, he contends, will foster greater accountability and allow for more strategic, long-term planning that benefits citizens.

However, the proposals have already drawn criticism. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch, in response to Burnham's address, accused him of advocating for "more government control, more taxes and more politicians." She expressed concerns that such a shift could lead to increased bureaucracy and financial burdens on taxpayers, questioning the economic feasibility and efficiency of expanded public sector involvement. The debate is set to intensify as Burnham's government begins to flesh out the details of its devolution and public control agenda.
