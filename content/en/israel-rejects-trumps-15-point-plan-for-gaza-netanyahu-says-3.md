Jerusalem – Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has publicly dismissed a 15-point peace proposal put forth by former U.S. President Donald Trump regarding the future of Gaza. Speaking to reporters on Tuesday, Netanyahu emphasized that any potential end to the current military operations in the Gaza Strip is contingent upon the complete and "genuine" disarmament of Hamas. He stressed that Israeli forces would not withdraw from the territory until this critical security objective is met, highlighting concerns about the lasting threat posed by the militant group.

The former president's plan, details of which remain largely undisclosed to the public, reportedly outlines a pathway for de-escalation and reconstruction in Gaza. However, the Israeli leadership appears unconvinced of its efficacy in addressing their primary security imperatives. Netanyahu's remarks signal a firm commitment to pursuing a military solution that ensures Hamas can no longer pose a threat to Israel, a position that has been a cornerstone of his government's policy throughout the protracted conflict.

This rejection comes amid ongoing international pressure for a ceasefire and a diplomatic resolution. While details of Trump's proposal are scarce, the Israeli Prime Minister's swift and unequivocal dismissal suggests a significant gap between the former U.S. administration's vision and the current Israeli government's security demands. The focus remains squarely on military objectives, with the disarmament of Hamas presented as a non-negotiable prerequisite for any withdrawal or long-term settlement in the region. The implications of this divergence for future peace efforts remain a subject of intense international observation.
