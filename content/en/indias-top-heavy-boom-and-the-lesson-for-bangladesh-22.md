India's economic expansion over the past decade has been a subject of much admiration, consistently outpacing many global economies. However, a closer examination reveals a less equitable distribution of this prosperity. Data indicates a widening income gap, with the wealthiest segment of the population experiencing substantial wealth accumulation, while the middle and lower classes have seen more modest gains. This phenomenon, often described as a 'top-heavy boom,' is driven by factors such as rapid growth in the IT and financial sectors, which primarily benefit highly skilled professionals and capital owners.

This development model, while generating headline growth figures, raises concerns about social cohesion and sustainable development. Critics argue that such an imbalanced growth pattern can lead to increased social stratification, reduced consumer demand from the majority, and a potential for political instability. The focus on high-growth sectors, while important, has sometimes overshadowed investments in sectors that employ a larger portion of the workforce and cater to broader societal needs, such as agriculture, manufacturing, and services that reach rural populations.

For Bangladesh, which is also pursuing ambitious economic goals, India's experience offers a valuable case study. As Bangladesh strives to transition from a least-developed country to a developing one, it must consider how to foster growth that benefits all its citizens. A key lesson is the importance of inclusive policies that promote widespread job creation, equitable wage growth, and access to essential services like education and healthcare for all. Simply chasing aggregate growth numbers without ensuring broad-based prosperity could lead to similar social and economic challenges in the future. Therefore, Bangladesh should prioritize strategies that support small and medium-sized enterprises, invest in human capital across all skill levels, and ensure that the benefits of economic progress are shared more equitably throughout society to build a truly sustainable and resilient economy.
