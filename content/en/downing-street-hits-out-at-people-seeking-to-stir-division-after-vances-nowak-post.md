Downing Street has publicly rebuked what it terms 'people seeking to stir division' following a controversial social media post by US Vice President J.D. Vance. Vance took to the platform X (formerly Twitter) to comment on the death of Henry Nowak, stating that 'the only response is righteous anger.' The remark, which has been widely interpreted as a highly charged and potentially inflammatory response to Nowak's passing, has drawn criticism from UK government officials.  Sources close to Number 10 indicated that while the specific circumstances surrounding Nowak's death are still emerging, the immediate attempt to frame it as a catalyst for 'righteous anger' by a prominent political figure is seen as unhelpful and potentially divisive.  The UK government is committed to maintaining strong international relations and believes that such rhetoric can undermine collaborative efforts to address complex global issues.  While not directly commenting on the specifics of Henry Nowak's case, a spokesperson for the Prime Minister's office stated, "We must be vigilant against those who seek to exploit sensitive situations for political gain and foster animosity. Constructive dialogue and understanding are paramount, especially in times of uncertainty." The incident underscores the delicate balance required in public discourse, particularly when commenting on matters with the potential for significant emotional and political impact. The focus, according to Downing Street, should remain on finding solutions and fostering unity, rather than exacerbating divisions.
