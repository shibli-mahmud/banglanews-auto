Belfast was plunged into chaos on Tuesday night as riots and arson attacks tore through residential areas, forcing terrified families to abandon their homes. The unrest, which followed a brutal knife attack on a local community worker earlier in the day, saw masked youths hurling petrol bombs and engaging in running battles with police.  "I'll never get over watching my home burn," sobbed Margaret Reilly, a resident of the Shankill Road, as she surveyed the smouldering ruins of her house. "We had to run out with nothing but the clothes on our backs. The flames were just… unbelievable."  The violence, which began in the north of the city, quickly spread, leaving a trail of destruction and a community grappling with fear and anger. Police deployed water cannons and riot vans in an attempt to regain control, but the situation remained volatile for hours.  Local community leaders have condemned the violence, urging calm and calling for an end to the cycle of retaliation. "This is not the way forward," stated a visibly shaken community worker. "We need to come together, not tear each other apart." The long-term impact of the night's events on the already fragile peace in Northern Ireland remains a significant concern, with residents left to pick up the pieces of their shattered lives.
