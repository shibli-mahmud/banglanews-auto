The proposed digital ID, a project intended to streamline access to government services and enhance security, has been a subject of debate for months. However, in his first major policy pledge since the election, Mr. Burnham declared that the scheme would be shelved indefinitely. A spokesperson for the incoming Prime Minister stated, "Our Government will put its focus where people need it right now. The immediate and pressing concern for millions across the nation is the soaring cost of everyday essentials – from energy bills to groceries. These are the issues that demand our urgent attention and resources."

The decision to abandon the digital ID project is seen as a pragmatic response to public sentiment and the dire economic conditions faced by many households. Critics of the digital ID had raised concerns about data privacy, the potential for surveillance, and the substantial cost of implementation, arguing that these resources could be better allocated elsewhere. The new administration appears to have heeded these concerns, signaling a commitment to tangible support for citizens struggling with inflation and economic uncertainty.

This pivot underscores Mr. Burnham's campaign promise to prioritize "bread and butter" issues. By deferring the digital ID, the government aims to demonstrate a clear understanding of the immediate pressures on ordinary people and to project an image of a responsive and action-oriented leadership. The exact details of the cost of living support packages are expected to be revealed in the coming weeks, but the announcement of the digital ID's demise is already generating considerable discussion about the government's fiscal priorities and its approach to public service delivery.
