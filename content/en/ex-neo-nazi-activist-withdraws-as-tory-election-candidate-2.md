Joshua Bonehill-Paine, a figure previously associated with far-right and neo-Nazi movements, has been removed from his position as a Conservative election candidate. This development comes after scrutiny of his past affiliations and public statements. Instead of standing for election, Bonehill-Paine is understood to be transitioning to an advisory capacity, tasked with assisting the Conservative Party in its strategies to combat extremism. 

Sources within the party have indicated that the decision was made to ensure the candidate's past activities would not overshadow the party's core message and electoral objectives. The shift to an advisory role suggests an attempt by the Conservatives to leverage Bonehill-Paine's perceived insights into extremist ideologies, albeit from a removed and ostensibly rehabilitated perspective. 

This move is likely to attract considerable attention, given the sensitive nature of Bonehill-Paine's history. Critics may question the wisdom of involving an individual with such a background in counter-extremism work, while supporters might argue for the potential benefits of understanding extremist recruitment and propaganda from someone who has been intimately involved. The Conservative Party has yet to issue a formal statement detailing the specifics of Bonehill-Paine's new role or the rationale behind this decision. The appointment raises questions about the party's vetting processes and its approach to engaging with individuals who have held extremist views.
