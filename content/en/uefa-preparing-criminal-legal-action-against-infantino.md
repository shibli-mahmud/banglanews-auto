UEFA is reportedly on the verge of taking the unprecedented step of launching criminal legal action against FIFA President Gianni Infantino. Sources close to the situation indicate that the European football confederation is pursuing proceedings related to a secret meeting Infantino had with Swiss Attorney General Michael Lauber. The meeting, which took place in 2017, was concerning a proposed deal to sell off stakes in the World Cup, a plan that UEFA and its member associations vehemently opposed.

Lauber himself was later forced to resign amid an investigation into his conduct, including his handling of the FIFA corruption scandal and his meetings with Infantino. UEFA's move signals a significant escalation in the ongoing tensions between the two powerful football organizations. The European body has long been critical of Infantino's leadership and his policies since taking over the helm of FIFA. 

While the exact charges UEFA intends to press remain unclear, the focus is believed to be on allegations of abuse of office and breach of official secrecy. The potential legal action could have far-reaching implications for Infantino's presidency and the future governance of global football. FIFA has yet to issue an official statement in response to these developing reports. This development underscores the deep divisions and mistrust that have characterized the relationship between UEFA and FIFA in recent years, potentially leading to a protracted and contentious legal battle.
