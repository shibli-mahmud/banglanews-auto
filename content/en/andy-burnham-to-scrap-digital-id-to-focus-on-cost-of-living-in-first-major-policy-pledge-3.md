Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham, poised to become the next Prime Minister, has delivered his first major policy pledge: the immediate termination of the planned national digital identity scheme. This decisive action underscores a strategic pivot, redirecting government focus and resources towards the urgent and escalating cost of living crisis that is impacting millions across the nation.  A spokesperson for Burnham's transition team articulated the rationale behind the decision, stating, “Our incoming government will put its focus where people need it right now. The cost of living is the defining issue for families, and our priority will be delivering tangible relief and support.”

The digital ID project, which had been in development for several years, faced considerable opposition from various quarters. Privacy campaigners warned of the potential for unprecedented state surveillance and the creation of a society where every interaction could be tracked and recorded. Trade unions and civil liberties groups also expressed deep reservations, citing concerns about data security, the potential for discrimination, and the erosion of fundamental freedoms. The significant financial investment already allocated to the digital ID initiative is now expected to be reallocated to programs aimed at alleviating financial hardship, such as energy bill support, food security initiatives, and assistance for vulnerable households.

This policy reversal is being hailed by many as a pragmatic and responsive approach to the current economic climate. It reflects a clear understanding of public anxieties and a willingness to adapt government priorities in line with the most pressing societal needs. The Prime Minister-elect's spokesperson further emphasized, “We will not be pursuing a costly and potentially intrusive digital identity system when families are struggling to heat their homes and put food on the table. Our mandate is to serve the people, and that means tackling the immediate economic challenges they face head-on.” The details of the new cost of living support measures are expected to be unveiled in the coming weeks, following Burnham's official assumption of office.
