US officials have conveyed to the BBC that a significant breakthrough may be imminent in the ongoing negotiations between the United States and Iran. According to these sources, the core elements of a ceasefire extension deal have been hammered out, with the framework now awaiting the ultimate endorsement of President Donald Trump and the highest echelons of Iranian leadership. While this represents a potentially crucial step forward, officials emphasized that the agreement is not yet finalized and considerable hurdles remain.

The specifics of the proposed ceasefire extension have not been fully disclosed, but it is understood to be a critical component of broader efforts to foster stability in the Middle East. The protracted period of heightened tensions has raised international concerns, and any de-escalation would be met with considerable relief. The White House has been tight-lipped regarding the exact nature of the discussions, but the acknowledgement of a near-agreement suggests that high-level diplomatic channels have been actively engaged.

This development, if successfully concluded, could pave the way for a more sustained period of calm. However, the 'not there yet' qualifier from US officials underscores the delicate nature of the situation. The approval process, particularly in Iran, is expected to be complex, involving various political factions. Likewise, President Trump's final sign-off will be closely watched. The coming days will be critical in determining whether this reported proximity to a deal translates into a concrete agreement that can effectively reduce friction between the two nations.
