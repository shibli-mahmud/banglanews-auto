A prominent former Member of Parliament has issued a strong plea to the Conservative Party, urging them to take decisive action to block a prospective council candidate who has a documented history of neo-Nazi activism and was previously jailed for issuing antisemitic abuse against the former MP. The individual, identified as Joshua Bonehill-Paine, has reportedly been selected to contest a newly formed seat on the Somerset Council, with elections scheduled for 2027.

The former MP, who has chosen to remain unnamed for her safety, expressed profound concern and dismay at the prospect of Bonehill-Paine potentially entering local government. She highlighted the severity of his past convictions, which included charges related to harassment and incitement of racial hatred, and specifically referenced the deeply personal and vitriolic antisemitic abuse he directed towards her. The sentencing in her case underscored the legal ramifications of such hate speech and its impact on individuals.

Sources close to the former MP indicate that she has contacted senior figures within the Conservative Party, including local constituency leaders and national officials, to implore them to intervene. The argument is that allowing an individual with such a history to stand under the Conservative banner would severely damage the party's reputation and undermine its commitment to inclusivity and the fight against extremism and hate crimes. Concerns are also being raised about the potential for such a candidate to embolden far-right elements within the community. The party is now under pressure to address this controversial selection and reiterate its stance against antisemitism and extremism in public life.
