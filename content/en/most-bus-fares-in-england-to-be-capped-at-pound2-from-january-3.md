In a move designed to support millions of commuters and families struggling with rising inflation, the Department for Transport has confirmed that a cap of £2 will be applied to single bus fares on hundreds of routes across England. This ambitious plan, which will be rolled out from the beginning of next year and run until the end of March, is expected to benefit passengers in rural and urban areas alike. The government has stated that the policy's primary aim is to "help with the cost of living and give people the breathing space they need" during a challenging economic period.  It is hoped that by reducing the cost of bus travel, more people will be encouraged to leave their cars at home, thereby easing traffic congestion and contributing to environmental goals. Bus operators have been invited to participate in the scheme, and it is understood that the government will be providing financial support to cover any revenue shortfalls.  The £2 cap represents a substantial saving for many, particularly for longer journeys where fares can often exceed this amount.  The success of this temporary measure will likely be closely monitored, with potential implications for future public transport policy.
