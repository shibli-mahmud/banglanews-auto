Speaking at a press conference on Saturday morning, Prime Minister Mark Carney stated that Canada's decision to withdraw from the trade talks was a necessary step to safeguard its economic independence and protect its vital industries. He emphasized that Canada had entered the negotiations in good faith, seeking a mutually beneficial agreement. However, the U.S. insistence on imposing tariffs, even as discussions were ongoing, was seen as a direct contradiction to the spirit of cooperation. "These tariffs are a miscalculation by the administration," Carney declared. "They are not just punitive to Canadian businesses and workers, but they also pose a significant risk to the integrated North American supply chains that have been built over decades."

The Prime Minister further elaborated that the tariffs, which target Canadian steel and aluminum exports, would inevitably lead to retaliatory measures from Canada, impacting American businesses and consumers. He underscored that Canada has always been a reliable trading partner for the United States, and that resorting to such protectionist measures would only serve to damage the long-standing bilateral relationship. The collapse of these latest talks signals a deepening rift between the two neighboring countries, raising concerns about the future of the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) and broader economic stability in the region. Carney reiterated Canada's commitment to finding a fair and equitable trade arrangement but warned that it would not compromise on its principles or its ability to act in its own national interest.
