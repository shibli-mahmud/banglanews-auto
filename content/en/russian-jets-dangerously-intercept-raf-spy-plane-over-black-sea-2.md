An unarmed Royal Air Force (RAF) Rivet Joint surveillance aircraft was conducting what the Ministry of Defence (MoD) described as a "routine international flight" over the Black Sea when it was intercepted by Russian fighter jets. The MoD stated that the Russian aircraft approached the surveillance plane in a manner deemed "dangerous," although specific details of the encounter were not immediately disclosed.  The Rivet Joint is an intelligence-gathering aircraft equipped with advanced sensors and communication interception capabilities, designed to operate in international airspace.

The incident has drawn strong criticism from the UK government, which has labelled the Russian military's actions as reckless and a potential threat to aviation safety.  A spokesperson for the MoD emphasized that the RAF aircraft was operating lawfully within international airspace and was not posing any threat. "These intercepts are becoming increasingly frequent and it is imperative that they are conducted in a way that is safe and does not jeopardize the safety of those involved," the spokesperson added.

The Black Sea has been a focal point of geopolitical tensions, particularly since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.  Both NATO and Russian forces frequently operate in the airspace above the sea, leading to a heightened risk of aerial encounters.  This latest incident underscores the ongoing friction between Russia and Western powers, with the UK and its allies closely monitoring Russian military activity in the region.
