India's economic narrative of late has been one of remarkable growth, often cited as a global success story. However, a closer examination reveals a developing trend that warrants careful consideration, particularly for neighboring Bangladesh. The nation's boom appears increasingly 'top-heavy,' meaning its expansion is disproportionately driven by a select few sectors, notably technology and advanced manufacturing, and a concentration of wealth at the apex of society. While these sectors contribute significantly to GDP and attract foreign investment, they are not creating broad-based employment opportunities for the vast majority of India's population.

This concentration of economic power has led to a widening income inequality. The benefits of India's growth are not filtering down effectively to the lower and middle classes, leading to social and economic stratification. The reliance on a few high-value sectors, while a testament to India's innovation, also makes the economy vulnerable to global shocks affecting these specific industries. A downturn in the tech sector, for instance, could have a more significant impact than if growth were more diversified.

For Bangladesh, currently striving for sustained development and aiming to graduate from Least Developed Country status, India's experience offers a salient warning. While embracing technological advancement and attracting investment is vital, policymakers must ensure that growth is inclusive and benefits are shared widely. This involves fostering labor-intensive industries, investing in education and skill development for the broader workforce, and implementing policies that promote equitable distribution of wealth. A balanced approach, prioritizing not just GDP growth but also employment generation and social equity, will be key to Bangladesh avoiding the pitfalls of a 'top-heavy' economic model and building a more resilient and prosperous future for all its citizens.
