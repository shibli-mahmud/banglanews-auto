A harrowing account has emerged of a shocking security lapse at a Travelodge hotel, where staff reportedly gave a domestic abuser the key to his victim's room. The woman, who has chosen to remain anonymous and is referred to as Sarah by the BBC, described a terrifying experience where her attacker managed to enter her hotel room after reception staff provided him with a spare key.

According to Sarah's testimony, the incident escalated violently, with her abuser kicking the door "off its hinges" after gaining access. The ordeal left her in a state of extreme fear and distress, highlighting a critical failure in the hotel's duty of care. "I was absolutely terrified. I thought he was going to kill me," Sarah told the BBC. She recounted her efforts to secure her room and her disbelief when she realized the key had been handed over without proper verification.

This incident raises serious questions about Travelodge's security protocols and staff training. The practice of readily handing over room keys to individuals without unequivocally confirming their identity and their right to access a guest's room is a clear breach of trust and safety. Hotel security is paramount, especially for vulnerable individuals seeking refuge. The apparent ease with which the abuser obtained the key suggests a systemic issue that needs immediate investigation and rectification. The woman's account underscores the devastating impact such negligence can have on victims of domestic abuse, who may be seeking safety in temporary accommodations. Travelodge has been approached for comment regarding the alleged incident.
