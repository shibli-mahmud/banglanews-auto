The Metropolitan Police are reportedly assisting the Electoral Commission in its inquiry into significant political donations made to Reform UK by a company linked to the party's deputy leader, Richard Tice. The donations, amounting to £250,000 each, were made by Britain Means Business, a firm directly owned by Mr. Tice. These significant financial contributions occurred in the period preceding the 2024 general election, a crucial time for political campaigning and expenditure.

The Electoral Commission, the independent body responsible for regulating political donations in the UK, confirmed it is investigating the matter. While the precise nature of the investigation remains undisclosed, it is understood to focus on the transparency and legality of the donations. Concerns are likely to centre around whether the funds were declared appropriately and if there were any breaches of electoral finance regulations.

Reform UK has faced scrutiny over its funding in the past. Richard Tice, a prominent businessman and co-founder of the Brexit-supporting group Leave.EU, has been a significant financial backer of the party. The latest donations, however, appear to have triggered a more formal regulatory review, with police involvement suggesting potential irregularities that warrant further investigation. The outcome of this inquiry could have implications for Reform UK's financial probity and its standing in the run-up to future electoral contests.
