In a significant U-turn, FIFA has decided to shelve a highly controversial plan that aimed to secure billions of dollars through the sale of minority stakes in its major commercial and sporting assets. The proposal, championed by FIFA President Gianni Infantino, sought to generate substantial revenue by bringing in external investors for a share in the lucrative broadcasting and marketing rights of tournaments like the FIFA World Cup and the Women's World Cup. The rationale behind the plan was to fund ambitious development projects and football initiatives globally.

However, the initiative quickly drew widespread criticism. Many expressed concerns that ceding control over such vital assets to private investors could compromise the integrity and long-term interests of the sport. Fears were raised about potential influence on football's governance, increased commercialization, and the dilution of FIFA's authority. Member associations, in particular, voiced their apprehension about the potential loss of sovereignty and the long-term implications for their own football development.

The backlash from various quarters, including fan groups and football experts, was swift and vociferous. Social media platforms buzzed with discussions highlighting the risks associated with commercializing the very heart of the sport. Facing mounting pressure and recognizing the significant opposition, FIFA's leadership has now formally withdrawn the proposal. A FIFA spokesperson stated that the organization has "listened to the concerns" and is committed to exploring alternative funding models that align with the best interests of global football and its stakeholders. The decision is seen as a victory for those who advocate for greater transparency and protection of the sport from excessive commercial interests.
