Lissie Harper has fiercely criticized proposals for the early release of prisoners, describing the possibility as "deplorable." Her strong reaction comes after news emerged that Andrew Harper's killers, potentially including Henry Long, Albert Stables, and Jessie Cole, might be considered for early parole. PC Harper was tragically killed in August 2019 while responding to a burglary. The circumstances of his death, involving a high-speed chase and the officer being dragged to his death, sent shockwaves across the nation and highlighted the dangers faced by law enforcement officers.

The news that some of those responsible for his murder could be freed before serving their full sentences has understandably caused immense pain and anger for Lissie Harper and her family. She argues that such releases undermine the justice system and fail to adequately reflect the severity of the crimes committed. Her statement emphasizes the profound impact of their actions and the need for justice to be served comprehensively.

In response to public and political pressure, it has been reported that the Justice Secretary, Simon Burnham, is set to review the controversial early release scheme. The government is understood to be examining the criteria and procedures governing these releases, particularly in cases involving serious offenses. The review aims to address concerns about public safety and the perceived leniency of the current system, especially in high-profile cases that have deeply affected public sentiment. Lissie Harper hopes this review will lead to meaningful changes that uphold the gravity of offenses and provide a stronger sense of closure for victims' families.
