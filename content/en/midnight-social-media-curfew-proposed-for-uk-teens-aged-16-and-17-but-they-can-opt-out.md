In a significant move to address concerns over excessive social media use among young people, the UK government has put forward a proposal for a midnight curfew on social media platforms for teenagers aged 16 and 17. This initiative, if implemented, would require social media companies to restrict access for this age group between midnight and 6 am. However, in a concession to personal autonomy, the government has indicated that teenagers would retain the right to opt out of this curfew, meaning they could choose to continue using social media beyond the proposed hours.

Beyond the time restrictions, the government is also targeting the design of social media applications themselves. The proposal includes a push to disable features widely considered to be 'addictive,' such as auto-play video content and the infinite scroll function. The rationale behind this is to reduce the compulsion for users to continuously engage with platforms, thereby promoting healthier digital habits. While the specifics of how these features would be managed and enforced are still under discussion, the intention is to create a less habit-forming online environment for young users.

This proposal comes amidst growing societal concern about the potential negative impacts of prolonged social media engagement on the mental health and well-being of adolescents. Experts have often highlighted issues like sleep disruption, cyberbullying, and the pressure to maintain an online persona as significant challenges faced by teenagers today. The government hopes that these measures, by encouraging breaks from digital consumption and reducing the allure of platform design, will contribute to a more balanced approach to social media use among the nation's youth.
