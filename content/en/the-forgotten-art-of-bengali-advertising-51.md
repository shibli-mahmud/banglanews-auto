Before the advent of slick digital campaigns and sophisticated market research, Bengali advertising was an art form in itself. In the bustling streets of Kolkata and other towns across West Bengal and Bangladesh, shopfronts were adorned with meticulously hand-painted signs. These weren't just functional; they were miniature masterpieces, often featuring charming illustrations, intricate calligraphy, and a distinct sense of local character. The use of vibrant colours, persuasive slogans penned in elegant Bengali script, and sometimes even playful rhymes made these advertisements not only eye-catching but also deeply ingrained in the daily lives of people.

Radio, too, was a powerful medium for Bengali advertising. Memorable jingles, often sung by popular artists or employing catchy folk tunes, became earworms that resonated for generations. These audio advertisements possessed a unique ability to connect with audiences on an emotional level, weaving stories and aspirations into the fabric of everyday listening. Think of the iconic advertisements for local tea brands or the persuasive calls to purchase traditional sweets – they weren't just selling products; they were selling a lifestyle, a tradition, a piece of Bengali identity.

However, with the rise of mass media, globalization, and the dominance of English in commercial messaging, these authentic forms of Bengali advertising are increasingly becoming a relic of the past. Digital platforms favour brevity and visual immediacy, often at the expense of artistic depth and cultural nuance. The craftsmanship involved in hand-painted signs is being replaced by mass-produced vinyl banners, and the soulful melodies of radio jingles are drowned out by slick, often generic, digital audio ads. This erosion represents not just a loss of artistic heritage but also a disconnect from the cultural roots that once made Bengali advertising so distinctive and beloved. It begs the question: what are we sacrificing in our pursuit of modernity, and can we find ways to preserve and even revive this rich artistic legacy?
