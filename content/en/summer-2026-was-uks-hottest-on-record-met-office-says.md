The summer of 2026 will be etched in the meteorological history of the United Kingdom as the hottest ever recorded, according to definitive data released by the Met Office. The national weather service confirmed that the mercury climbed to unprecedented levels, breaking the previous record that was only set in the summer of 2025. This extraordinary heatwave was characterised by its unusual persistence, with periods of extreme temperatures stretching for weeks across much of the country. 

Analysis by the Met Office indicates that the sustained high temperatures contributed significantly to exceeding the previous record. This summer saw multiple heat alerts issued across the UK, with significant impacts on public health, infrastructure, and the natural environment. Water restrictions were implemented in several regions as reservoirs and rivers reached critically low levels. The agricultural sector also faced considerable challenges, with crops struggling under the relentless sun and increased irrigation demands. 

Scientists at the Met Office have pointed to a combination of atmospheric conditions and the ongoing influence of climate change as likely drivers behind this record-breaking summer. The prolonged absence of significant rainfall, coupled with consistently high daily and nightly temperatures, created a severe heat dome effect. While the UK has experienced warm summers in the past, the intensity and duration of the heat in 2026 were described as 'unprecedented' by meteorological experts, raising further concerns about the accelerating pace of global warming and its tangible effects on national weather patterns.
