The discovery was made by archaeologists from the Soprintendenza del Mare, the Sicilian regional authority for underwater cultural heritage. Preliminary investigations suggest the vessel, believed to be a merchant ship, sank centuries ago, carrying a cargo that is still largely intact. While the full extent of the cargo is yet to be cataloged, initial dives have revealed amphorae, pottery, and other artifacts that could offer invaluable insights into Roman trade routes and daily life during the late imperial era. 

The shipwreck's exceptional state of preservation is attributed to the seafloor conditions and the depth at which it lay undisturbed for centuries. The team has expressed optimism that further excavation will reveal even more about the ship's construction, its origin, and its final voyage. This significant find underscores Sicily's rich maritime history and its pivotal role in the Roman Empire's vast network of commerce and influence. The Italian Ministry of Culture has pledged full support for the ongoing research and preservation efforts, recognizing the immense historical and cultural value of this submerged treasure. Further dives and analysis are planned in the coming months, with hopes of unlocking more secrets from this ancient Roman vessel.
