The national association representing resident doctors announced the decision to suspend their industrial action late Sunday evening, following intensive negotiations with government representatives. Sources close to the talks indicate that the breakthrough came after the government agreed to a revised pay structure and committed to addressing concerns regarding working hours and staffing levels.  While specific details of the offer have not yet been fully disclosed, it is understood to include a phased increase in stipends and a concrete plan for recruitment of additional medical personnel to alleviate the strain on existing staff.  The union leadership stated that the new proposal "shows a genuine commitment" from the government to improve the working conditions and professional development of resident doctors, who play a crucial role in the day-to-day functioning of hospitals.  The strike would have impacted non-emergency services, potentially leading to postponed surgeries and delayed treatments, placing an additional burden on an already stretched healthcare system.  Patient advocacy groups had expressed deep concern over the impending strike, highlighting the potential negative consequences for vulnerable individuals.  With the strike now averted, hospitals can resume normal operations, and resident doctors will return to their duties, focusing on patient care. The union has emphasized that while this is a significant step forward, they will continue to monitor the implementation of the agreed-upon measures closely.
