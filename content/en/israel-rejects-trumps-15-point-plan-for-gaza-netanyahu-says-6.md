Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has publicly dismissed a 15-point peace proposal reportedly put forth by former US President Donald Trump regarding the Gaza Strip. Speaking to reporters, Netanyahu asserted that Israel's military operations in Gaza will continue unabated until the Palestinian militant group Hamas is 'genuinely' disarmed. This declaration comes amid ongoing international efforts to broker a ceasefire and find a path towards long-term stability in the region.

Netanyahu's statement directly challenges any proposals that would necessitate an Israeli withdrawal without achieving what he deems a complete dismantling of Hamas's military capabilities. He emphasized that Israel's security remains the paramount concern and that any lasting solution must ensure that Gaza no longer poses a threat to Israeli citizens. While the specifics of Trump's 15-point plan have not been fully disclosed publicly, Israeli officials have indicated that key elements, particularly those concerning the immediate cessation of hostilities and troop redeployment, are unacceptable without prior disarmament of Hamas.

This rejection underscores the deep divisions that persist regarding the future of Gaza and the methods for achieving peace. Netanyahu's administration has consistently maintained a hardline stance on Hamas, viewing its eradication as a prerequisite for any post-conflict arrangement. The Prime Minister's firm position suggests that Israel will continue its military campaign, potentially impacting future diplomatic overtures and humanitarian aid efforts. The international community is closely watching these developments, as a prolonged conflict in Gaza carries significant regional and global implications.
