The Metropolitan Police are reportedly investigating a series of large donations made to the Reform UK political party, with a particular focus on contributions originating from a company linked to the party's deputy leader, Richard Tice. Britain Means Business, a firm reportedly owned by Tice, is understood to have made two substantial donations, each amounting to £250,000, in the run-up to the recent 2024 general election. This brings the total reported donations from the company to £500,000.

Political funding regulations in the UK are stringent, requiring transparency regarding the source of donations to political parties. Investigations into such matters are typically initiated to ensure compliance with these laws and to safeguard the integrity of the electoral process. While the specific nature of the police's inquiry has not been fully disclosed, it is understood to be centered on the legitimacy and disclosure of the funds provided by Britain Means Business.

Richard Tice, a prominent businessman and co-founder of Reform UK, has been a key figure in the party's efforts to gain political traction. The party itself has positioned itself as an anti-establishment force, advocating for significant policy changes. The scrutiny of its funding sources, especially from entities closely associated with its leadership, is a critical aspect of maintaining public trust in political institutions. Further details are expected to emerge as the investigation progresses. The Metropolitan Police have declined to comment on ongoing investigations.
