Ofcom's comprehensive investigation into the risks children face online has concluded that TikTok and YouTube are 'not safe enough' to protect underage users. The report highlights a range of concerns, including exposure to harmful content, cyberbullying, and the potential for addictive usage patterns. While both platforms have implemented various safety features and content moderation policies, Ofcom's findings suggest these measures are insufficient to create a truly secure environment for children.

YouTube, owned by Google, stated that it has worked collaboratively with child safety experts to develop age-appropriate experiences and has invested heavily in AI-powered systems to detect and remove harmful content. They emphasized their commitment to continuous improvement and parental controls. However, the regulator's assessment indicates that the sheer volume of content and the sophisticated algorithms employed by these platforms present ongoing challenges in safeguarding young audiences effectively.

TikTok, the short-form video giant, expressed its disappointment with Ofcom's report, asserting that the regulator failed to acknowledge the significant investments and robust safety features they have put in place. The company pointed to its age-gating systems, content moderation teams, and tools designed to help users manage their online experience. Despite these claims, Ofcom's verdict suggests a gap remains between the companies' perceived safety efforts and the reality of the risks faced by children navigating these rapidly evolving digital spaces. The report is expected to put further pressure on tech giants to enhance their child protection measures.
