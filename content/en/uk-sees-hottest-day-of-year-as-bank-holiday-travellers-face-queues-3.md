The mercury climbed to a scorching 28.4 degrees Celsius in the village of Santon Downham, Suffolk, on Friday, marking the hottest day of 2024 so far. Forecasters at the Met Office predict this warmth to continue into Saturday, with highs of 27C expected in London and the South East. This welcome burst of summer sunshine is set to coincide with the Spring Bank Holiday, a period traditionally associated with a surge in domestic and international travel.

However, the prospect of a relaxed long weekend is being dampened by widespread travel delays. Commuters and holidaymakers faced substantial queues at the Port of Dover, with ferry operators reporting significant delays due to increased passenger numbers and checks. Similarly, Birmingham Airport experienced operational challenges, leading to extended waiting times for passengers and concerns about flight schedules. The AA has warned of potential gridlock on major routes as people embark on leisure journeys, advising drivers to allow extra time and consider alternative routes where possible.

While the public enjoys the unseasonably warm weather, with many heading to beaches and parks, authorities are also urging people to stay hydrated and take precautions against the sun. The combination of extreme heat and travel congestion presents a challenging start to the bank holiday, testing the patience of travellers and highlighting the ongoing pressures on the UK's transport infrastructure during peak periods.
