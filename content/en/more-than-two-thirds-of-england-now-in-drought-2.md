The majority of England is now officially in drought, with an announcement today confirming that the East Midlands, Lincolnshire and Northamptonshire, Kent and East Sussex, and Solent and the South Downs have all moved to drought status. This brings the total number of areas in drought to 12, covering more than two-thirds of the country. The classification follows months of below-average rainfall and exceptionally high temperatures, which have severely depleted river flows, groundwater levels, and reservoir storage.

The move to drought status by the Environment Agency and National Drought Group signals a serious and prolonged dry spell. While it does not automatically trigger new legal powers or hosepipe bans, it allows water companies to implement further restrictions if necessary to manage dwindling supplies. Public urged to use water wisely. Residents are being advised to be extremely mindful of their water consumption, with simple actions like shorter showers, turning off taps while brushing teeth, and avoiding washing cars by hand being encouraged. Gardeners are also being asked to refrain from watering lawns.

The cumulative effect of the dry weather is visible across the landscape, with parched fields and low river levels becoming commonplace. Farmers are reporting significant challenges in crop production due to the lack of water. Water companies have already implemented some hosepipe bans in various regions, and further restrictions are expected as the situation persists. The long-term outlook offers little immediate relief, with forecasts suggesting a continuation of dry and warm conditions for the foreseeable future. This situation poses a significant challenge for water management, agriculture, and ecosystems across England.
