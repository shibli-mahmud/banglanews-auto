Dermot Murnaghan, a familiar face on British television for decades, has died at the age of 68. His passing was confirmed by his family, who stated he died after a courageous battle with stage four prostate cancer. Murnaghan had a distinguished career spanning major broadcasting networks, including the BBC, ITV, and Sky News, where he became a trusted voice for audiences.

Throughout his career, Murnaghan hosted a variety of news and current affairs programmes, earning a reputation for his calm demeanour, incisive questioning, and ability to connect with viewers. He was particularly noted for his long tenure presenting Sky News' flagship evening news programmes and also had a significant stint at ITV News. At the BBC, he was a familiar face on shows like Breakfast News.

His family's statement paid tribute to his "unwavering strength and dignity" during his illness. The news of his death has prompted an outpouring of tributes from colleagues, journalists, and the public, who remember him as a consummate professional and a genuinely kind individual. His distinctive voice and presence will be deeply missed by the broadcasting world and beyond. He leaves behind a legacy of thoughtful journalism and engaging television.
