In a poignant reflection, BBC Political Editor Chris Mason has offered a vivid portrait of Ann Widdecombe, the former Conservative MP and prominent Brexit advocate, remembering her as a figure of both pugnacity and undeniable charisma. Mason, speaking in a personal capacity, noted that even those who vehemently disagreed with Widdecombe's political stances often found themselves disarmed by her personality and directness.  According to Mason, a significant number of individuals within Westminster, regardless of their political allegiances, held a deep respect for Widdecombe, finding her to be 'immensely likeable.' This sentiment, he suggests, stemmed from her unwavering commitment to her principles and her remarkable ability to articulate her views with clarity and conviction.  She never shied away from a debate, nor did she attempt to evade difficult questions. Instead, Widdecombe possessed a rare talent for confronting them head-on, delivering her responses with a characteristic blend of wit and unwavering resolve.  Her presence in parliamentary debates was often described as formidable; she could be a tenacious opponent, yet her charm and intellectual rigor prevented her from being dismissed.  Mason's recollection underscores a political landscape that valued direct engagement and conviction, qualities that Ann Widdecombe embodied with exceptional flair, leaving a lasting impression on colleagues and observers alike.
