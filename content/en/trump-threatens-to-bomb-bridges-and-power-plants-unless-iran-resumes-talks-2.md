President Donald Trump escalated his rhetoric against Iran on Saturday, declaring that the United States would "bomb your bridges and power plants" unless Tehran agrees to resume diplomatic talks. The aggressive declaration, made during a press conference, signals a significant hardening of the US stance as a series of tit-for-tat actions between the two nations continue. This latest threat arrives as the exchange of fire between American forces and Iranian-backed militias in the region has entered its fourth consecutive day, marking a dangerous acceleration of the already volatile situation.

Adding to the pressure, the United States has reportedly resumed its stringent blockade of Iranian ports, further isolating the nation economically and restricting its access to international trade. This move is intended to cripple Iran's economy and compel its leadership to reconsider its current strategic direction. The Trump administration has consistently maintained that its policy of 'maximum pressure' is designed to force Iran to negotiate a new deal that would curb its nuclear program and its regional activities. However, critics argue that such aggressive posturing risks pushing the region further into conflict and could lead to unintended and potentially devastating consequences. The Iranian government has not yet issued a formal response to Trump's latest threat, but previous escalations have often been met with defiance and further retaliatory measures, raising concerns about a potential wider confrontation.
