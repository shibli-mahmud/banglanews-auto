The educational landscape is facing a worrying trend, with an increasing number of schools closing their doors. What's particularly concerning is the disproportionate impact these closures have on pupils with special educational needs (SEN). Analysis of school closures between 2020 and 2025 indicates that almost 30% of children attending these now-closed institutions were identified as having SEN. This figure is considerably higher than the national average for SEN pupils, suggesting that schools struggling financially or facing other challenges often serve a larger cohort of students with additional needs.

This trend raises critical questions about the sustainability of support for these vulnerable students. When a school closes, the disruption to a SEN pupil's education can be immense. They often require specialized teaching methods, tailored resources, and a stable, familiar environment to thrive. Relocating these students to new schools can mean losing access to established support networks, therapists, and teachers who understand their specific requirements. Furthermore, mainstream schools may not always have the capacity or expertise to adequately cater to the diverse range of needs within the SEN spectrum.

Advocacy groups and parents are calling for urgent intervention from local authorities and the government. They argue that a more proactive approach is needed to support schools with a high concentration of SEN pupils, rather than allowing them to reach a point of closure. Potential solutions include increased funding, dedicated specialist support staff, and better collaboration between schools and local SEN services. The future of education for SEN children hinges on ensuring that such closures become a rarity, safeguarding their right to a consistent and supportive learning journey.
