Tehran has signaled that a nuclear agreement with Washington is not on the immediate horizon, despite hopeful remarks from the US Secretary of State suggesting a potential accord could be reached as early as Monday.  Speaking to reporters, Iran's Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Nasser Kanaani indicated that while diplomatic efforts are ongoing, the path to a revived Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) remains complex and requires further substantive progress. He stressed that the current situation does not indicate an imminent breakthrough, urging for realistic expectations.

The US had previously expressed a degree of optimism regarding the indirect negotiations, which have been conducted through intermediaries due to the lack of direct diplomatic ties between the two nations.  The stated possibility of an agreement by Monday had fueled speculation among international observers and regional actors. However, Iranian officials have consistently maintained that they will not compromise on their core demands, including the complete lifting of sanctions and guarantees against future US withdrawal from any agreement.  Conversely, the US has sought assurances that Iran will significantly curb its nuclear program to prevent it from reaching weapons-grade capability.  The gap in these demands, coupled with ongoing geopolitical tensions, appears to be the primary reason for the continued stalemate.  Both sides have accused the other of intransigence, making the prospect of a quick resolution unlikely.  The international community continues to watch closely, aware of the potential implications of either a renewed deal or a continued impasse for regional stability and global non-proliferation efforts.
