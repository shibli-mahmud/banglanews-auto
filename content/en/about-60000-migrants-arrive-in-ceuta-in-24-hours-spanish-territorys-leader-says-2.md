Ceuta, a Spanish city on the northern coast of Africa, experienced an unprecedented migration crisis as an estimated 60,000 individuals, primarily from sub-Saharan Africa, managed to enter the territory in a single day. The surge began early Monday morning, with many migrants reportedly swimming or using small boats to cross the maritime border from neighboring Morocco.  Spanish authorities were overwhelmed by the sheer volume of arrivals, struggling to manage the situation.

In response to the unfolding crisis, Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez characterized the event as a direct 'attack' on Spanish sovereignty and territorial integrity. He arrived in Ceuta on Tuesday to assess the situation firsthand and address the pressing concerns.  Speaking to reporters, Sánchez reiterated Spain's commitment to its borders and announced a firm policy of returning all individuals who entered Ceuta illegally back to Morocco. 'We will return all those who have entered irregularly,' he stated, emphasizing that Spain would act with 'firmness and proportionality.'

The sudden influx has placed immense strain on Ceuta's resources and infrastructure, raising humanitarian concerns for the well-being of the arriving migrants, many of whom are minors. Spanish security forces have been deployed to control the situation, and tents are being erected to house the new arrivals. The Moroccan authorities have also increased patrols along their coastline in an effort to stem the flow. This event highlights the persistent challenges of irregular migration across the Mediterranean and the complex geopolitical dynamics between Spain and Morocco.
