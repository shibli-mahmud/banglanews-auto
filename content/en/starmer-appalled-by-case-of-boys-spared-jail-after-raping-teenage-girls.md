The Prime Minister, Keir Starmer, has publicly stated he is 'appalled' by the recent court ruling that saw three teenage boys convicted of rape avoid immediate jail sentences. The decision, which has sparked widespread public outrage and condemnation, involved the boys being given community sentences and suspended jail terms after being found guilty of raping multiple teenage girls.  Mr. Starmer, speaking to reporters, described the outcome as 'wrong' and confirmed that the Director of Public Prosecutions has initiated an urgent review of the sentencing.  He emphasized the gravity of the offenses and the importance of justice for the victims. 'It is right that the sentences are being urgently reviewed,' Starmer stated, indicating that the Crown Prosecution Service will thoroughly examine the circumstances and the judge's decision. This case has ignited a fierce debate about the adequacy of current sentencing guidelines for serious sexual offenses, particularly when the perpetrators are minors.  Victims' rights groups and legal experts have voiced concerns that the sentences fail to reflect the severity of the crimes and may not provide adequate deterrence or justice for survivors. The government is under significant pressure to demonstrate its commitment to tackling sexual violence and ensuring that the justice system adequately protects and supports victims.  The outcome of the review is keenly awaited by the public and those advocating for tougher measures against perpetrators of sexual assault.
