The sentencing of Paul Quinn for the brutal rape of a woman in Salford in 2003 has ignited outrage from Andrew Malkinson, the man who was wrongly convicted and incarcerated for 17 years for the crime. Quinn received a 24-year prison sentence after pleading guilty to rape, kidnapping, and assault occasioning actual bodily harm. However, Malkinson has vehemently stated that this sentence is woefully inadequate and constitutes an 'insult' to both himself and the wider justice system.

Malkinson was released in 2020 after new evidence, including DNA, proved his innocence. He spent nearly two decades behind bars for a crime he did not commit, a period that has irrevocably impacted his life. Speaking out following Quinn's sentencing, Malkinson expressed profound disappointment, arguing that the length of Quinn's incarceration does not reflect the severity of his actions or the immense suffering he inflicted on the victim and on Malkinson himself. He highlighted the devastating consequences of his wrongful conviction, including the loss of precious years and the profound psychological toll. The disparity between the 17 years Malkinson endured unjustly and Quinn's 24-year sentence, while significant, does little to assuage the deep-seated feelings of injustice felt by Malkinson and his supporters. He called for a thorough review of how such miscarriages of justice occur and for greater accountability within the legal process.
