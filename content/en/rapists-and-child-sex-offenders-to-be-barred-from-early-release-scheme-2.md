In a significant shift driven by public outcry, the UK government has moved to exclude individuals convicted of rape and child sex offences from the early release on licence scheme. Justice Secretary Alex Chalk confirmed the policy change, stating that these serious offenders will no longer be considered for early release, regardless of their behaviour during incarceration.

The decision comes after considerable campaigning and emotional appeals, notably from Lissie Harper, the widow of PC Andrew Harper. Her husband was tragically killed in the line of duty in 2019. Harper's killers were convicted of manslaughter and are currently serving sentences, making them potentially eligible for early release schemes. Lissie Harper has consistently voiced her anger and distress, describing the continued eligibility of her husband's killers for such schemes as "disgusting."

Chalk acknowledged the public's sentiment, stating that "it is right that serious sexual offenders and those who have committed the most heinous crimes are not eligible for early release." The new policy aims to restore public confidence in the justice system and ensure that sentences reflect the gravity of the offences committed. While the exact legislative process for implementing the change is still being outlined, the announcement signals a firm commitment from the government to address the concerns raised by victims' families and the wider public. The exclusion is expected to apply to all new cases and potentially be reviewed for those currently serving sentences, though details on retrospective application are yet to be confirmed. This represents a notable tightening of early release provisions for some of the most serious crimes in England and Wales.
