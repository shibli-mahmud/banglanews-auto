David Lammy, the UK's Shadow Foreign Secretary, has publicly stated that he confronted US Senator JD Vance regarding his highly criticized remarks that attributed the death of Henry Nowak to what Vance described as a "mass invasion of migrants." Lammy, speaking to reporters, made it clear that he "told JD Vance that his comments were wrong." The Labour MP emphasized that the tragic incident involving Mr. Nowak "has got nothing to do with mass migration," directly refuting the narrative pushed by the Republican senator.

Vance, a prominent figure in the Republican party, had made the inflammatory claim during a speech, suggesting that the increasing numbers of migrants entering the United States were directly responsible for crimes like the one that resulted in Nowak's death. This framing has been widely condemned by immigration advocates and many politicians as xenophobic and an attempt to exploit a tragedy for political gain.

Lammy's intervention highlights a growing transatlantic debate over immigration rhetoric and its potential to incite division and prejudice. The Shadow Foreign Secretary's direct rebuke of Vance signals a strong stance against what he perceives as harmful and inaccurate generalizations about migrant populations. By explicitly stating that Vance's comments were "wrong," Lammy aimed to counter the misinformation and prevent the politicization of a senseless act of violence by scapegoating an entire group of people. This exchange underscores the sensitive nature of migration discussions and the responsibility of public figures to ensure their statements are fact-based and do not fuel xenophobia.
