The controversy erupted after Elon Musk took to X to voice his opinions on the handling of the investigation into the murder of Henry Nowak. Musk has posted multiple times, expressing criticism of the police's actions and the broader justice system's response to the crime. These interventions, however, have not gone unnoticed by senior political figures.

Keir Starmer, a former Director of Public Prosecutions himself, was particularly sharp in his condemnation. He stated that it is "unacceptable" for individuals, especially those with significant global influence like Musk, to use social media platforms to "undermine" ongoing police investigations. Starmer emphasized the importance of allowing law enforcement and judicial processes to operate without external pressure or influence, particularly from individuals who may not possess full knowledge of the case's complexities. He argued that such commentary risks inflaming public emotions and potentially jeopardizing the integrity of the investigation and any subsequent legal proceedings. The Labour leader called for greater responsibility from platform owners to ensure their sites are not used to spread misinformation or create undue pressure on sensitive legal matters. The comments underscore a growing concern in political circles about the impact of social media billionaires on public discourse and the administration of justice.
