A fierce backlash is brewing within political circles as an ex-MP vociferously calls for the Conservative Party to intervene and block a candidate with a deeply troubling past from contesting a seat on the new Somerset Council. Joshua Bonehill-Paine, who has previously been imprisoned for neo-Nazi activism, has reportedly been selected by the party to stand in a division for the 2027 council elections. The former MP, who has chosen to remain anonymous for now but is understood to be the target of Bonehill-Paine's past antisemitic vitriol, stated that the selection is 'utterly unacceptable' and a stain on the Conservative Party's reputation. "This individual has a documented history of hate speech and has targeted me personally with vile antisemitic abuse," the ex-MP stated. "For the party to even consider allowing him to stand, let alone select him, sends a dangerous message that such behaviour is tolerated. It is imperative that the Conservative leadership takes immediate action to disown him and ensure he cannot represent them."

Bonehill-Paine's conviction stemmed from his involvement in promoting neo-Nazi ideology, which included the dissemination of antisemitic propaganda. His selection for the Somerset Council, a newly formed unitary authority following local government restructuring, has ignited outrage among anti-racism campaigners and political opponents alike. Critics argue that allowing such a candidate to run undermines the principles of inclusivity and tolerance that should underpin public service. The Conservative Party has yet to issue a formal statement regarding the allegations and Bonehill-Paine's candidacy, leaving many to question their stance on extremism within their ranks. The situation places significant pressure on the party to uphold its commitment to combating antisemitism and ensuring that elected officials adhere to the highest ethical standards.
