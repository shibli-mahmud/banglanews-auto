In a recently published memoir, Earl Charles Spencer alleges that King Charles III, then Prince Charles, sounded "giddily elated" in the immediate aftermath of Princess Diana's tragic death in a Paris car crash in 1997. Spencer's account paints a starkly different picture of the royal family's grief than what has been publicly perceived.

According to extracts from the book, Spencer recounts a telephone conversation with Charles shortly after the news of Diana's passing broke. He describes the then-Prince of Wales's tone as one of "giddy elation," a characterisation that has sent shockwaves through royal circles and the wider public. This alleged reaction stands in stark contrast to the solemn and somber public facade presented by the monarchy during that period of national mourning.

Buckingham Palace has moved swiftly and decisively to refute these claims. A spokesperson for the Palace stated that the allegations are "categorically untrue" and "deeply hurtful." They emphasized that the Royal Family, and particularly King Charles, were experiencing profound grief at the time and were focused on supporting Diana's children, Princes William and Harry.

Earl Spencer's book, reportedly titled 'A Very Private Life', is said to delve into his complex relationship with his sister and the inner workings of the Royal Family. These latest revelations are likely to reignite public interest and debate surrounding Diana's death and the monarchy's handling of the subsequent events. The stark divergence between Spencer's claims and the Palace's response sets the stage for further scrutiny of a pivotal moment in modern royal history.
