In a move that has ignited widespread public concern and renewed scrutiny of the justice system's approach to sexual assault cases involving minors, the Attorney General's office has confirmed it will be reviewing the sentencing of three teenage boys. These individuals were not handed custodial sentences, despite their roles in the rape of two girls. The specific details of the case and the reasoning behind the initial sentences have not yet been fully disclosed, but the outcome has been met with considerable backlash from victims' rights advocates, legal experts, and members of the public who deem the sentences to be lenient and disproportionate to the gravity of the crime.

The review is expected to examine the legal framework under which the sentences were imposed, including any relevant sentencing guidelines, pre-sentencing reports, and judicial considerations. Questions are being raised about whether the current laws adequately reflect the severity of rape, particularly when committed by adolescents, and the potential impact on victims. The Attorney General's office has stated that the review aims to ensure consistency and fairness in sentencing across similar cases and to uphold public confidence in the administration of justice. The outcome of this review could have significant implications for future sentencing in juvenile sex offense cases.
