In a statement that has generated significant international attention, U.S. President Donald Trump announced on Saturday that a deal with Iran is on the cusp of being signed, with an expected date of Sunday. The President's assertion, made without providing specific details of the agreement or its signatories, suggests a potential breakthrough in long-standing tensions between Washington and Tehran. This development, if realized, could mark a significant shift in U.S. foreign policy towards Iran, following years of strained relations and a withdrawal from the previous nuclear accord.

However, the Iranian side has offered a more cautious perspective. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Tehran has publicly stated that while negotiations are ongoing and progress is being made, an exact date for any potential signing has not yet been finalized. A spokesperson clarified that the agreement "will not be tomorrow," a statement that appears to contradict President Trump's more definitive timeline. This divergence in messaging highlights the delicate nature of the ongoing diplomatic efforts and underscores the complexities inherent in reaching a mutually agreeable accord. Observers are closely watching for further clarifications from both governments, as the timing and substance of any deal remain subject to ongoing discussion and potential shifts.
