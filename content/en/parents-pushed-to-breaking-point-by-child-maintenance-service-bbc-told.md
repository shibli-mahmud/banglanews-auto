A BBC investigation has revealed the significant distress faced by parents dealing with the Child Maintenance Service (CMS). Numerous mothers have described arduous and often fruitless journeys to obtain child maintenance payments from absent fathers. Katy, a mother of two from London, shared her experience of a years-long struggle to get her ex-partner to contribute financially to their children's upbringing. 'It's exhausting, demoralizing, and feels like a constant fight,' she stated. 'The CMS promises support, but the reality is often inaction and endless bureaucracy. My children are going without because of this.'

The stories from fathers are equally concerning. Many claim to have been wrongly charged thousands of pounds by the CMS, sometimes for sums they had already paid or for periods they were not legally obligated to pay. Mark, a father of one from Manchester, expressed his frustration. 'I've been paying what I owe, but the system keeps adding charges, creating a debt I can't get rid of. I feel like I'm being punished for trying to do the right thing.'

These accounts highlight systemic issues within the CMS, including lengthy processing times, difficulties in enforcing payments, and a perceived lack of fairness in its charging mechanisms. The financial strain on these families can be immense, impacting children's welfare and parental mental health. Campaigners are calling for urgent reform to make the CMS more efficient, transparent, and supportive for all parties involved, ensuring that children receive the financial support they are legally entitled to without causing undue hardship to either parent.
