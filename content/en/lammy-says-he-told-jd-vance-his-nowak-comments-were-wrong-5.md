London – David Lammy has publicly stated that he directly informed US Senator JD Vance that his remarks attributing the death of Henry Nowak, a young migrant, to "mass invasion of migrants" were incorrect. The Shadow Foreign Secretary made these comments following a meeting with Vance, emphasizing that the tragic incident was not a consequence of increased migration.

Senator Vance had previously suggested that Nowak's death was a direct result of what he termed a "mass invasion of migrants," a sentiment that has drawn significant criticism from various quarters. Lammy, a vocal advocate for migrants' rights and a critic of anti-immigrant rhetoric, used the opportunity to push back against such claims.

"I told him directly that that was wrong," Lammy stated, referring to his conversation with Vance. He further elaborated that the circumstances surrounding Nowak's death are entirely unrelated to broader migration trends. "This has got nothing to do with mass migration," Lammy insisted, underscoring the need to avoid mischaracterizing such sensitive events for political gain.

The exchange highlights the ongoing political debate surrounding immigration, particularly in the wake of high-profile incidents involving migrants. Lammy's intervention serves to counter narratives that often seek to scapegoat migrant communities for societal problems, reinforcing the importance of evidence-based discourse on migration policy and the human cost of such tragedies.
