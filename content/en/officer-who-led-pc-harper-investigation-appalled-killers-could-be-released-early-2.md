Detective Superintendent Stuart Blaik, the senior investigating officer in the murder of PC Andrew Harper, has expressed his deep dismay over the possibility of the individuals responsible for the young officer's death being released from prison prematurely. PC Harper was tragically killed in August 2019 while responding to a burglary in Berkshire. The investigation, led by Blaik, was extensive and complex, culminating in the conviction of four men for murder and one for manslaughter.

Blaik, who has remained largely silent since the conclusion of the legal proceedings, has now broken his silence, stating he is 'appalled' by the news that the killers could be eligible for parole sooner than many anticipated. He emphasized the brutality of the crime and the devastating impact it had on PC Harper's family, friends, and the wider police force. The officer's death sent shockwaves across the nation, highlighting the dangers faced by frontline police officers.

While acknowledging the judicial process and the possibility of sentence reviews, Blaik stressed that the gravity of the offence and the sentencing should reflect the severe consequences of such violence. He articulated concerns that an early release would be a profound injustice to PC Harper's memory and a source of further anguish for his loved ones. His public statement is expected to resonate strongly with those who followed the case and underscore the emotional toll such investigations can take on senior officers.
