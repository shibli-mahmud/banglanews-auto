The Department for Education (DfE) has published updated guidance for schools and other public institutions, reinforcing the principle that facilities like toilets and changing rooms are to be segregated based on biological sex. This clarification comes after a landmark Supreme Court decision last year, which upheld the right of single-sex spaces to maintain their designation. The guidance aims to provide clarity for institutions on how to implement these policies in line with legal requirements and societal expectations.

According to the DfE document, the decision to maintain sex-segregated facilities is rooted in the need to ensure privacy, dignity, and safety for all individuals, particularly women and girls. While acknowledging the importance of inclusivity and trans rights, the guidance emphasizes that these considerations must be balanced with the existing legal framework that permits single-sex provisions. It suggests that in most circumstances, biological sex will be the determining factor for access. However, the guidance also allows for some discretion, noting that "single-sex spaces may be provided where this is a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim." This suggests that while the default remains biological sex, there might be specific, justifiable exceptions. The guidance has been met with a mix of support and criticism, with some advocating for its clarity and others expressing concerns about its implications for transgender individuals.
