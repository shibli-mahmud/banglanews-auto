Nigel Farage, the prominent leader of Reform UK, has declared his intention to enact sweeping constitutional changes if his party secures a majority in the upcoming general election. Farage articulated a clear mandate for his potential government, emphasizing the need to deliver on core pledges concerning immigration, the economy, and national sovereignty within the initial 100 days. To achieve this ambitious timeline and overcome potential legislative hurdles, Farage indicated a willingness to reform both the unelected House of Lords and the independence of the judiciary.

Speaking at a recent rally, Farage stated, "We will not be held back by an archaic second chamber that acts as a roadblock to the will of the people. If they stand in our way, we will reform them, or even replace them, to ensure progress." He further suggested that perceived judicial activism could also be a target, hinting at measures to reinforce parliamentary sovereignty over judicial pronouncements. The proposed changes have ignited debate, with critics raising concerns about the potential erosion of democratic checks and balances and the independence of the legal system. Supporters, however, argue that such decisive action is necessary to break free from what they describe as bureaucratic inertia and entrenched interests that have historically hindered significant policy shifts. Farage's firm stance signals a potential period of significant constitutional tension should Reform UK achieve electoral success, as the party signals a clear intent to prioritize its agenda above established norms.
