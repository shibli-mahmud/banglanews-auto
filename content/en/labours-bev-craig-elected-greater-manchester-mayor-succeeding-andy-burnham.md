The results of the Greater Manchester mayoral election have confirmed a clear win for Labour's Bev Craig, who will now take the reins as the region's mayor. Craig garnered a substantial majority of the votes, significantly outpacing her closest competitor, Sian Astley of Reform UK, who secured nearly half of Craig's vote count. This outcome signals strong support for Labour's vision for the city-region among its electorate.

Craig's campaign focused on key issues such as improving public transport, tackling the housing crisis, and boosting economic opportunities across Greater Manchester. Her victory is seen as a mandate to continue and expand upon the work initiated by her predecessor, Andy Burnham, who has served as mayor since the role's inception. Burnham's tenure was marked by efforts to decentralize power and improve public services, and Craig has pledged to build on this legacy.

The election saw a range of candidates from different political parties, but Craig's consistent message and extensive local government experience resonated most strongly with voters. The scale of her victory suggests a desire for continuity and a belief in her ability to address the complex challenges facing Greater Manchester, from cost of living pressures to the ongoing need for regional investment and development. As the new mayor, Bev Craig faces the considerable task of uniting the ten boroughs of Greater Manchester and driving forward a progressive agenda for the region's future.
