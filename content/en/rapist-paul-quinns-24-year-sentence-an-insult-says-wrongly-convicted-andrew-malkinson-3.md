Andrew Malkinson has spoken out against the 'utterly insulting' 24-year prison sentence given to Paul Quinn, the man finally convicted for the 2003 Salford rape for which Malkinson himself was wrongly imprisoned for 17 years. Malkinson, who was exonerated in 2020, expressed his profound disappointment and anger that Quinn, who has now been jailed for life with a minimum of 24 years, received a sentence that he believes undervalues the immense suffering of the victim and the devastating impact of his own wrongful conviction.

Speaking to reporters outside court, Malkinson stated, 'For the justice system to take 20 years to find the real rapist and then give him a sentence that I consider to be an insult to the victim and to justice itself is just not good enough.' He highlighted the long and arduous journey for both himself and the victim to reach this point of accountability, emphasizing that the delayed justice does little to heal the deep wounds inflicted by the original crime and the subsequent misjustice.

Malkinson's exoneration was a landmark moment, bringing to light systemic failures that led to his imprisonment. His fight for justice exposed flaws in the original investigation and highlighted the crucial need for robust safeguards against wrongful convictions. The sentencing of Quinn, while finally holding him accountable, has reopened painful memories and reinforced Malkinson's belief that the system designed to protect society and uphold justice remains deeply flawed. He stressed that while Quinn's conviction is a step, the length of the sentence falls short of what true justice would demand for such a heinous crime.
