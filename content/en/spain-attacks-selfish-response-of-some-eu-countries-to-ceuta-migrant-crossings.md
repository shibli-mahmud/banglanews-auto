Madrid has expressed frustration with the perceived lack of solidarity from some EU partners in addressing the humanitarian and logistical challenges posed by the mass influx of migrants into Ceuta. The Spanish government argued that the crisis demands a coordinated European response, rather than individual, protectionist measures. The dramatic increase in arrivals, reportedly numbering in the thousands over a short period, overwhelmed local resources and sparked a diplomatic row. 

In a significant development, Italy announced the temporary suspension of the Schengen agreement with Spain. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's office stated that the decision was prompted by the 'shocking' scenes witnessed in Ceuta, where a surge in irregular arrivals put immense pressure on border control and humanitarian services. Meloni has been a vocal advocate for stricter EU border policies and a more equitable distribution of responsibility for managing migration flows. Her government's move signals a growing concern within certain EU capitals about the effectiveness of current migration management strategies and a potential fracturing of the open-border principles of the Schengen Area. Spain, which has borne the brunt of this latest crisis, is now calling for immediate EU-level discussions to find sustainable solutions and reaffirm the principle of shared responsibility among member states.
