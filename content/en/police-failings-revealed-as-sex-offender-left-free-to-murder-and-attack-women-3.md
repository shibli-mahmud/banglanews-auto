An internal investigation has uncovered critical failings within the Metropolitan Police that allowed convicted sex offender Simon Levy to continue his reign of terror, culminating in the murder of Sheryl Wilkins, his second known victim. A senior officer within the Met, speaking on condition of anonymity, has conceded that Ms. Wilkins' life might have been saved. This admission follows a period of intense scrutiny on the force's handling of information regarding Levy's movements and ongoing threats to public safety.

Levy, who had previously been convicted of sexual offenses, was reportedly under police radar. However, a combination of systemic issues and alleged oversight meant that the intelligence gathered was not translated into effective action. Critics argue that this represents a grave dereliction of duty, leaving vulnerable women exposed to a known predator. The Met's internal review has highlighted a lack of robust information-sharing protocols and a failure to adequately assess the risk posed by Levy, despite his previous convictions and alleged continued offending.

Ms. Wilkins' family has expressed their profound grief and anger, demanding accountability for the police's inaction. They believe that had the police taken their concerns and the available intelligence more seriously, their daughter would still be alive. The findings of the investigation are expected to trigger significant reforms within the Metropolitan Police's approach to managing and monitoring convicted offenders, particularly those with a history of violence against women. The case has reignited public debate about police accountability and the effectiveness of safeguarding measures designed to protect the public from dangerous individuals.
