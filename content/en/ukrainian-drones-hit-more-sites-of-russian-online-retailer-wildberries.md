Ukrainian drones have reportedly carried out a series of coordinated attacks overnight, hitting several key logistics hubs operated by Wildberries, a prominent Russian online marketplace. The targeted facilities are located in the Krasnodar and Stavropol regions, strategically important areas for the company's distribution network.  While official confirmation from either the Ukrainian or Russian authorities is pending, social media channels and local reports indicate significant damage at the sites.

These attacks appear to be part of a broader strategy by Ukraine to disrupt Russia's economic capabilities and supply chains. Wildberries, along with its competitor Ozon, is a cornerstone of e-commerce in Russia, delivering a vast array of goods across the country, including to occupied territories in Ukraine. Targeting such infrastructure could aim to impede military resupply efforts or exert economic pressure on Moscow.

The Krasnodar region, bordering the occupied Crimean peninsula, and Stavropol, a key transit point, are vital for Wildberries' operations. The full extent of the damage and any potential casualties remain unclear as investigations are ongoing. This incident underscores the increasing reach of Ukrainian drone capabilities and their willingness to strike deep within Russian territory, focusing on targets that can inflict economic and logistical pain. The potential impact on consumer goods availability and the operational capacity of Wildberries is yet to be fully assessed, but the attacks signal a new phase in the economic dimension of the conflict.
