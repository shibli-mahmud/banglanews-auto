Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham has unveiled a bold proposal that could fundamentally alter the landscape of public finance in the UK. In a significant announcement, Burnham has called for regional mayors, including himself, to be granted a share of the national income tax. The stated aim is to provide local leaders with greater fiscal autonomy, enabling them to directly invest in services and infrastructure that are crucial to their communities. Burnham argues that this decentralization of power would lead to more responsive and effective governance, allowing decisions to be made closer to the people they affect.

"This is about giving power to every postcode," Burnham stated, emphasizing the potential for this policy to address regional inequalities and foster local economic growth. He believes that by controlling a portion of the tax revenue generated within their regions, mayors could be better equipped to tackle issues such as public transport, housing, and skills training with resources tailored to specific local needs. The vision is one where local taxation directly correlates with local benefit, fostering a stronger sense of accountability between elected officials and their constituents.

However, the proposal has not been without its detractors. Critics have raised concerns about the practicalities of implementing such a system, questioning the potential impact on national revenue and the fairness of distributing income tax shares across diverse regions with varying economic outputs. The lack of detailed financial modeling and a clear framework for how this shared taxation would operate has led to accusations that the plans are currently more aspirational than concrete. Questions also remain about how such a system would interact with the existing UK tax system and the potential for it to exacerbate regional disparities rather than alleviate them, depending on the allocation formula. Further scrutiny is expected as the government considers the feasibility and implications of this significant devolutionary proposal.
