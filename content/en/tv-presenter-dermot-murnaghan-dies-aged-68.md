The media world is in mourning following the death of much-loved television presenter Dermot Murnaghan, who passed away at the age of 68. His family confirmed the news, stating that he died after a brave fight with stage four prostate cancer. Murnaghan was a prominent journalist and broadcaster, building a distinguished career across major UK networks including the BBC, ITV, and Sky News.  He was widely respected for his insightful interviews, calm demeanor, and ability to navigate complex and often challenging news stories.  Born in Dublin, Murnaghan began his journalistic career in local radio before moving into television.  He became a household name presenting programmes such as BBC's 'Newsnight' and later fronting Sky News' flagship evening bulletin. His later years saw him become a familiar face on ITV's 'Good Morning Britain' and 'GMB', where his affable personality and sharp questioning were a regular feature for millions of viewers.  His candour about his illness in recent months resonated with many, highlighting the importance of awareness and early detection of prostate cancer.  Colleagues and viewers alike have paid tribute to his professionalism, warmth, and significant contribution to broadcast journalism. He leaves behind a legacy of credible and engaging television news presentation.
