British Shadow Foreign Secretary David Lammy has sharply rebuked US Senator JD Vance for attributing the death of a constituent, Henry Nowak, to what Vance described as a "mass invasion of migrants." Lammy disclosed that he directly challenged Vance on the assertion, making it unequivocally clear that the Senator's narrative was inaccurate and unfounded.

According to Lammy, the tragic incident involving Mr. Nowak "has got nothing to do with mass migration." He emphasized that Vance's comments were not only factually incorrect but also potentially harmful, playing into divisive rhetoric. The Labour MP made the remarks during a press conference, highlighting the importance of basing public discourse on facts rather than inflammatory speculation.

Lammy's forceful rejection of Vance's claims underscores a broader political debate concerning immigration policies and their perceived impact on public safety and social cohesion. Vance, a prominent conservative voice, has frequently employed strong rhetoric regarding border security and immigration levels. However, Lammy's intervention suggests a bipartisan, or at least cross-national, effort to counter what he views as misinformation and xenophobic sentiment being propagated by some political figures. The exchange highlights the growing international scrutiny of such narratives and the potential for significant diplomatic friction when political rhetoric crosses national borders.
