Dr. Jason Arday, a former lecturer at Cambridge University and a prominent figure in anti-racism scholarship, has died at the age of 37. His passing was confirmed by police after emergency services found him unresponsive at an address in Battersea, south London, on Friday afternoon. The Metropolitan Police stated that the death is being treated as unexplained, and an investigation is underway.

Arday's career had recently been overshadowed by allegations of plagiarism. An investigation by The Times newspaper revealed that significant portions of his doctoral thesis, submitted to the University of Edinburgh, appeared to have been lifted from existing sources without proper attribution. These claims led to considerable controversy and scrutiny, impacting his academic standing and public image. Arday had previously held positions at the University of Glasgow and Durham University before joining Cambridge University as a lecturer in sociology and a fellow at the Centre for Research in Arts, Social Sciences and Humanities.

His contributions to discussions on race, inequality, and higher education were widely recognised, and he was seen as a rising star in his field. The news of his death has sent shockwaves through academic circles, with many expressing sadness and disbelief. Details surrounding the cause of death have not yet been released, and authorities are appealing for information from anyone who may have seen or heard anything relevant on the day of his discovery. The Metropolitan Police have confirmed that a post-mortem examination will be conducted to establish the cause of death.
