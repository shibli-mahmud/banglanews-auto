Tourists in France have described the terrifying experience of their summer holidays being abruptly cut short by escalating wildfires. Families on what were meant to be relaxing breaks found themselves in a desperate race against time to escape their holiday homes and campsites as flames rapidly encroached.

The BBC has spoken to several individuals who were vacationing in regions particularly affected by the blazes, including the south and southwest of France. Many expressed shock and disbelief as smoke billowed and the scale of the danger became apparent.  "We were told to pack quickly, that we had to leave immediately," recounted Sarah Jenkins, who was on holiday with her two children in the Gironde region. "The air was thick with smoke, and you could hear the sirens everywhere. It was truly frightening."

Others described chaotic scenes at evacuation centers, with many struggling to secure essential belongings or even understand the rapidly changing instructions. "We just grabbed our passports and our dog," said Mark Peterson, who had been camping with his wife. "Everything else was left behind. We didn't know if we'd ever get back to our tent."

Firefighters have been working tirelessly to combat the infernos, which have been exacerbated by dry conditions and high temperatures. The disruption to tourism has been significant, with many holiday parks and campsites forced to close temporarily, leaving visitors stranded or seeking emergency accommodation. The experiences shared paint a stark picture of how quickly a dream holiday can transform into a terrifying ordeal, highlighting the vulnerability of tourist areas to extreme weather events.
