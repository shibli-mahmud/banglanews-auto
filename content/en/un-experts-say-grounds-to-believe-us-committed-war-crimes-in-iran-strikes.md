A panel of United Nations experts has announced that they have found sufficient evidence to suggest the United States may have violated international humanitarian law during recent strikes in Iran. The experts, who operate under a mandate from the UN Human Rights Council, stated that there are "grounds to believe" that the attacks, which reportedly targeted civilian infrastructure, could constitute war crimes. 

The specific incidents under scrutiny are believed to have occurred in February, with reports of strikes on a primary school in the city of Minab and a sports complex in Lamerd. Both locations are described as civilian sites, and the attacks are alleged to have resulted in civilian casualties. The United States government has not officially confirmed or denied carrying out these specific strikes. However, the UN experts have emphasized the importance of a thorough and independent investigation into the matter.

Under international law, intentionally directing attacks against the civilian population or civilian objects is prohibited and can amount to war crimes. The experts' statement does not constitute a formal legal judgment but rather an assessment based on available information and credible reports. They have called for accountability and for all parties to respect their obligations under international humanitarian law. The findings are expected to put further pressure on the US to provide clarity on its actions and to address any potential wrongdoing. This development underscores the ongoing scrutiny of military actions in conflict zones and the UN's role in upholding international legal standards.
