Prime Minister Burnham has stated he is prepared to openly criticize US President Donald Trump if he believes it is necessary for the protection of Britain's national interests. Speaking in an exclusive interview with the BBC, Burnham described his initial conversation with the US President as 'good,' but stressed the paramount importance of advocating for the UK's position on the global stage.

"You have to defend your own national interest," Burnham asserted, outlining a pragmatic approach to diplomacy. He suggested that while maintaining cordial relations with allies is crucial, this should not come at the expense of jeopardizing domestic priorities or undermining Britain's sovereignty. This stance signals a departure from a more deferential diplomatic posture, indicating a willingness to engage in robust debate and potentially public confrontation if fundamental British interests are perceived to be at risk.

The Prime Minister's remarks come at a time of heightened global uncertainty and evolving international alliances. His emphasis on 'defending our own' is likely to resonate with a domestic audience concerned about the implications of shifting geopolitical landscapes on the UK. While not explicitly detailing the circumstances under which he would issue a public rebuke, Burnham's comments suggest a clear red line: any action or rhetoric from the US that demonstrably harms Britain's economic, security, or political standing would be met with firm opposition. This resolute attitude underscores his commitment to a proactive and assertive foreign policy, prioritizing national well-being above all else.
