In a dramatic eleventh-hour announcement, U.S. President Donald Trump revealed that he had called off planned retaliatory strikes against Iran. The U.S. president's message, delivered via Twitter, indicated that the decision was made after he "immediately" and "proportionately" responded to Iran's downing of a U.S. drone. Trump stated that he had been informed that 150 Iranians were likely to be killed in the strikes, and that this was "not a proportionate" response. He further elaborated that he "stopped it at the last minute." The U.S. president's message came amid widespread media reports suggesting that the Trump administration was preparing for new, more intense strikes on Iran over the weekend in response to recent provocations. These provocations included attacks on oil tankers in the Gulf of Oman and the aforementioned drone incident. The sudden reversal has taken many by surprise, including some within his own administration, and has led to considerable speculation about the future trajectory of U.S.-Iran relations. While the immediate military threat appears to have receded, the underlying tensions remain high, and the possibility of future confrontations lingers. Trump's emphasis on a "rapid" deal suggests a potential opening for diplomatic engagement, though the specifics of such an agreement remain entirely unclear at this juncture. International observers are closely watching for further developments, hoping for a de-escalation of the crisis.
