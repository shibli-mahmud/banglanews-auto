Andy Burnham, the current Mayor of Greater Manchester, is frequently cited as a potential successor to Sir Keir Starmer. For him to become Labour leader, a leadership contest would need to be triggered. This typically occurs if a significant number of Labour MPs formally declare a lack of confidence in the current leader.  Historically, such challenges require a substantial bloc of parliamentary support, often numbering in the dozens, to meet the threshold set by the party's rules for initiating a formal vote.

If a contest is called, Burnham would need to secure nominations from at least 10% of the parliamentary Labour Party (PLP) to be officially on the ballot paper. This is a crucial hurdle, requiring him to build considerable support among his fellow MPs, many of whom are currently aligned with Starmer. Beyond the PLP, he would then need to win the support of the wider party membership and affiliated trade unions through a series of hustings and a ballot.

Burnham's appeal lies in his perceived ability to connect with traditional Labour voters, particularly in the North of England, an area where the party has suffered significant losses. His mayoral record in Greater Manchester, focusing on public services and regional devolution, offers a tangible vision of his policy priorities. However, navigating the internal politics of the Labour Party, which can be fiercely factional, would be a significant challenge.

Should he successfully navigate a leadership contest, the path to becoming Prime Minister would then depend on Labour winning a general election. This would involve presenting a compelling alternative to the current Conservative government, addressing the key concerns of the electorate, and potentially winning back voters lost in recent years. Burnham's potential leadership would likely be characterized by a focus on regional inequalities and a robust defence of public services.
