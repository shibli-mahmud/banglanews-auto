Nigel Farage, the leader of the Reform UK party, has offered a new explanation for remarks made during a meeting with potential donors, which were captured by an undercover reporter. Speaking to the BBC's Laura Kuenssberg, Farage claimed he "wasn't listening" to the specifics of the conversation, implying a lack of full engagement with the financial proposals put forward.

During the interview, which touched upon his contentious role and the ongoing scrutiny of his political activities, Farage also disclosed the significant personal toll his career is taking. He revealed that his family members are "begging" him to leave his job, suggesting a profound impact on his personal life and relationships. This admission comes at a time when Farage's political future and the direction of Reform UK are under intense examination.

The undercover footage, released by the Bureau of Investigative Journalism, reportedly showed Farage discussing financial matters with individuals purporting to represent an organisation with potential interests in British policy. Farage's defence that he was not paying close attention is likely to face further questioning from both the media and political opponents. The revelations add another layer to the ongoing debate surrounding political funding and transparency in the UK, particularly concerning the influence of foreign money in domestic politics. Farage's comments, while aiming to mitigate damage, may also raise further questions about his awareness and involvement in such discussions.
