India's economic trajectory over the past few decades has been a remarkable success story, often lauded for its impressive GDP growth rates. However, a closer examination reveals a pattern of development that is heavily skewed towards certain high-profile sectors, particularly information technology and services, and to a lesser extent, manufacturing. While these sectors have created significant wealth and global recognition, they have not always translated into widespread, inclusive growth that benefits the majority of the population.  The benefits of this 'top-heavy' boom have largely accrued to a skilled, urbanized workforce, leaving behind large segments of the rural population and those engaged in traditional agriculture and unorganized sectors. This has led to widening income inequality and a concentration of economic power.  For Bangladesh, currently experiencing its own robust economic expansion, India's experience serves as a critical case study. As Bangladesh aims for higher income status, it is imperative to avoid the pitfalls of uneven development. The focus needs to be not just on aggregate growth but on fostering a diversified economic base that generates employment across various skill levels and regions.  Investing in broad-based education and skill development, supporting small and medium enterprises (SMEs), and strengthening the agricultural sector are crucial.  Bangladesh must strive for an economic model that lifts all boats, ensuring that the fruits of growth are shared equitably, preventing the emergence of a similar 'top-heavy' boom that could exacerbate social and economic disparities.
