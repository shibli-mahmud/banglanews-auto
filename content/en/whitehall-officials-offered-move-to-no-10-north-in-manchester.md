Whitehall officials willing to relocate from London to Manchester have been informed they will be provided with relocation assistance, the BBC has learned. The move is part of a broader government strategy to establish a significant presence in the North of England, with reports suggesting a new office, dubbed 'No 10 North', is being set up in the city.

This initiative is understood to be a key component of the government's levelling-up agenda, designed to spread opportunities and government decision-making beyond the capital. By offering financial incentives and support for those prepared to move, ministers hope to attract experienced civil servants to the new Manchester base.

The establishment of 'No 10 North' signifies a commitment to decentralising power and fostering a more geographically diverse civil service. While specific details regarding the number of positions or the exact nature of the relocation packages remain undisclosed, the BBC's understanding indicates a concerted effort to build a substantial government footprint in Manchester.

This move is likely to be met with a mixed reaction. Supporters will see it as a positive step towards reducing regional inequalities and strengthening public sector presence in the North. However, concerns may arise regarding the practicalities of such a relocation, the potential impact on families, and whether it truly represents a significant shift in power or primarily a symbolic gesture. The success of this endeavour will ultimately depend on the effectiveness of the support offered and the genuine integration of the new office into the wider government machinery.
