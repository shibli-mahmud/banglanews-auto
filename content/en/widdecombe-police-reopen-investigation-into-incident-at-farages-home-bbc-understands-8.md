Sources have indicated to the BBC that the Metropolitan Police's Counter Terrorism Command has reopened an investigation into an incident at Nigel Farage's property. The probe, which is understood to be examining a potential attempted burglary, is being carried out by the same unit that is also looking into the circumstances surrounding the recent death of the prominent Brexit figure.  While details of the original incident from last year remain limited, the decision to revisit it by a specialist counter-terror unit suggests a potential escalation in the perceived threat or the discovery of new, significant evidence.  The re-examination by the Met's Counter Terrorism Command underscores the seriousness with which authorities are treating the matter.  Mr. Farage, a former leader of the UK Independence Party (UKIP) and a Member of the European Parliament, was a highly visible and often controversial political figure in Britain. The nature of the alleged attempted burglary and its connection, if any, to his political activities or the subsequent investigation into his death are yet to be clarified.  A spokesperson for the Metropolitan Police declined to comment on live investigations but confirmed that the unit operates on referrals and intelligence.  This reopening of the case, coming after Mr. Farage's death, is likely to attract significant public and media attention, given his profile and the sensitive nature of the investigation.
