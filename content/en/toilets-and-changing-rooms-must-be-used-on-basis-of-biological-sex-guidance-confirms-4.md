The Department for Education (DfE) published updated guidance on Thursday, reinforcing the principle that access to single-sex spaces should align with an individual's biological sex at birth. This clarification comes after a landmark Supreme Court decision last year, which affirmed the legality of providing single-sex facilities. The guidance is intended to provide clarity for schools and other public institutions, addressing concerns raised by some about the implications of gender self-identification on the use of these spaces.

According to the DfE's updated advice, schools have a legal obligation to provide single-sex toilets and changing rooms where it is reasonably practical. This means that while transgender individuals may be permitted to use facilities aligning with their gender identity, this should not supersede the provision and maintenance of sex-specific spaces. The guidance emphasizes that decisions regarding facility access must be balanced, considering the needs and privacy of all users, particularly women and girls. It also suggests that where separate facilities are not feasible, measures such as single-occupancy cubicles or communal changing areas with individual stalls can be implemented to ensure privacy. The aim is to uphold the principle of biological sex in these sensitive areas while also seeking to be inclusive where possible, though the primary emphasis remains on biological sex.
