Prime Minister Andy Burnham has declared that local councils will soon receive significantly expanded powers to regulate and potentially limit the number of vape and betting shops opening in their communities. Speaking on the challenges facing Britain's high streets, Burnham described how decades of economic shifts and the rise of online retail had 'hollowed out' town centres, leaving them struggling to retain their vibrancy and appeal.

The government's proposed measures aim to address the perceived over-saturation of these specific types of businesses, which critics argue contribute to social problems and detract from the unique character of local areas. While the exact legislative details are yet to be fully disclosed, the intention is to give councils more control over planning applications and change-of-use permissions, enabling them to refuse new licenses for vape and betting premises more readily if they are deemed detrimental to the local environment.

Burnham emphasized that this initiative is part of a broader strategy to revitalize town centres and make them more appealing for families and diverse retail offerings. He stated, "We have heard the concerns from communities across the country. Our high streets should be vibrant hubs for everyone, not dominated by a narrow range of businesses that can, in some cases, contribute to social harm." The move has been welcomed by many local government leaders and community groups who have long campaigned for greater control over the commercial landscape of their towns. The government hopes these new powers will empower councils to foster more diverse and beneficial business environments, contributing to the long-term health and prosperity of Britain's urban centres.
