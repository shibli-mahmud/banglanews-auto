The political landscape is increasingly dominated by discussions about the Prime Minister's potential departure. Recent reports from BBC journalists Henry Zeffman and Nick Eardley indicate a discernible shift in the mood within government circles, moving from robust support to a growing undercurrent of anticipation, and in some quarters, expectation, of an impending resignation. This change is not being attributed to a single event, but rather a confluence of factors, including sustained public pressure, internal party divisions, and a perceived weakening of the Prime Minister's authority.  Sources close to the government suggest that while public pronouncements may remain steadfast, private conversations are increasingly focused on contingency planning and the leadership vacuum that could ensue.  The narrative is evolving from one of weathering storms to one of preparing for a potential leadership transition.  This burgeoning mood within the government signifies a critical juncture, where the long-term viability of the Prime Minister's tenure is being openly questioned by those closest to the corridors of power. The shift suggests that even loyalists are beginning to reassess the political calculus, potentially paving the way for a resignation announcement sooner rather than later.
