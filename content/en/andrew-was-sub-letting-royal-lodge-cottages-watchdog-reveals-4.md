The National Audit Office (NAO), tasked with scrutinizing public expenditure, has concluded a thorough review of how royal properties are managed and utilized. Their findings, detailed in a recently published report, highlight that Prince Andrew, the Duke of York, was engaging in sub-letting arrangements for several cottages located within the grounds of Royal Lodge, his Windsor residence. These arrangements, according to the NAO, were not made public and appear to fall outside the standard protocols for managing royal estates.

The watchdog's investigation delved into the financial implications and transparency surrounding these sub-lettings. While the report does not explicitly accuse any wrongdoing, it emphasizes the need for clearer guidelines and greater accountability in the management of properties funded through the Sovereign Grant, which ultimately derives from public funds. The cottages in question are understood to be part of the Royal Lodge estate, which is maintained by the Crown Estate, but used by the Duke of York.

Sources close to the NAO indicated that the sub-letting arrangements were discovered during a broader audit of royal household finances and property management. The report aims to ensure that all assets are managed in the best interests of the public and that there is no perceived or actual misuse of royal residences or their associated amenities. The revelations are likely to reignite discussions about the transparency and financial oversight of the royal family's considerable property portfolio.
