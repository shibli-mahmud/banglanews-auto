A bombshell report has emerged detailing serious allegations against David Sullivan, a prominent figure in English football and media. Multiple women have accused the West Ham United co-owner of abusing his considerable power and influence within his media empire to prey on them for sex. The women, who have spoken out anonymously for fear of reprisal, claim that Sullivan made it clear that sexual encounters were a condition for them to secure coverage or prominent placement in the newspapers he owned.

These accusations paint a disturbing picture of a powerful individual allegedly exploiting his position in the media industry for personal gratification. The women recount instances where they were allegedly told directly by Sullivan that sexual favours were necessary to achieve their goals, whether it was securing an interview, a feature article, or even just a chance to be seen in his publications. The allegations suggest a systematic pattern of behaviour where professional opportunities were leveraged as a means to coerce women into sexual relationships.

Sullivan, who co-owns West Ham with David Gold and Daniel Kretinsky, is a well-known businessman with extensive interests in media, including a significant stake in the Daily Mirror and Sunday Mirror through his company Express Newspapers. The accusations, if proven, could have significant ramifications not only for his reputation and business dealings but also for the reputation of the football club he co-owns. Representatives for David Sullivan have yet to issue a formal response to these serious allegations.
