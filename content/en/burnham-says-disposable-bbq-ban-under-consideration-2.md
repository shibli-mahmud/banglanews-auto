Prime Minister Boris Johnson has indicated that a ban on disposable barbecues is being actively considered as the country grapples with severe wildfire risks. Speaking amid soaring temperatures and widespread dry conditions, Johnson implored the public to exercise extreme caution and to avoid using single-use grills "at this moment in time." The government is reportedly reviewing all available options to mitigate the dangers posed by the current extreme weather conditions, which have already led to numerous blazes across the UK.  The Metropolitan Police and other emergency services have been stretched thin responding to a surge in fires, many of which are believed to be linked to careless use of barbecues and other ignition sources.  Environmental groups and fire chiefs have long been calling for restrictions on these easily discarded items, citing their potential to ignite dry grass and vegetation, particularly in parks and outdoor spaces.  While specific details of any proposed ban have not yet been released, the Prime Minister's comments signal a significant shift in the government's stance, moving from strong advice to potential legislative action. The move comes as the UK faces unprecedented heatwaves and the very real threat of further environmental damage and public safety hazards.  The consideration of a ban reflects the growing urgency to address the escalating wildfire threat and protect vital green spaces. Further announcements are expected as the situation develops.
