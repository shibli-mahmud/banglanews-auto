Downing Street has confirmed that the Prime Minister does not believe it is appropriate for him to comment on the specific security provisions for Prince Harry and Meghan Markle. When asked about concerns raised regarding the couple's safety upon their return to the UK, a spokesperson for the Prime Minister stated that these arrangements are a "private matter" for the individuals concerned and their security providers.

This stance comes amidst ongoing discussions and reports about the nature of their security, particularly following their step back as senior royals and their relocation to the United States. The Duke and Duchess have previously expressed concerns about their personal safety and the adequacy of security measures in place. The government has, in the past, been involved in discussions regarding their security, but the current position emphasizes a shift towards a private responsibility.

The Prime Minister's office also conveyed well wishes to the Duke and Duchess of Sussex as they prepare for their upcoming visit. The couple is scheduled to attend several events, including the One Young World summit in Manchester and the Invictus Games. Their return to Britain has generated considerable public interest, and the focus on their private security arrangements highlights the complex relationship between the Royal Family, their public profile, and personal safety considerations. The government's decision to classify these arrangements as private underscores a broader principle of personal responsibility for security outside of official royal duties.
