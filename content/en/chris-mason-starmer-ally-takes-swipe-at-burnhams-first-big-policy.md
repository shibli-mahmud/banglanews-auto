A prominent figure within the Labour party, Darren Jones, has delivered a significant swipe at Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham's signature policy initiative. Jones, who previously served as Sir Keir Starmer's right-hand man in the Treasury brief and has maintained a close working relationship with the Labour leader, has publicly questioned the financial viability of Burnham's proposal to slash VAT on domestic fuel bills. 

Speaking to reporters, Jones articulated his concerns, stating unequivocally that Burnham's plan is "unfunded." This direct challenge from within the party's ranks is likely to cause discomfort for Burnham, who has been championing the VAT cut as a key measure to alleviate the cost of living crisis for households in his region and across the country. The Greater Manchester Mayor has argued that such a move would provide immediate relief to struggling families and stimulate the economy by putting more disposable income into people's pockets. 

However, Jones's intervention raises serious questions about the practical implementation and the potential fiscal consequences of the proposal. By labeling the policy as "unfunded," he implies that there is no clear plan for how the significant reduction in tax revenue would be compensated for, potentially leading to increased borrowing or cuts to other public services. This criticism from a figure so closely associated with Sir Keir Starmer, who himself has been cautious about committing to unfunded spending promises, suggests a potential divergence in policy approaches within the Labour party or a stark reminder of the fiscal responsibilities that any government must consider. The debate highlights the ongoing tension between offering immediate cost-of-living relief and maintaining sound public finances, a balancing act that will likely define much of Labour's policy messaging heading into future elections.
