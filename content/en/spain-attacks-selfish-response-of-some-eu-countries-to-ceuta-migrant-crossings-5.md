The situation in Ceuta has become a flashpoint for broader EU debates on migration and border management. Thousands of migrants, primarily from sub-Saharan Africa, managed to enter the Spanish territory over a single weekend in May, overwhelming local resources and prompting a strong reaction from the Spanish government. In the wake of the influx, Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni characterized the scenes in Ceuta as 'shocking' and announced the suspension of the Schengen agreement, which normally allows for free movement within participating countries. This decision has been met with considerable dismay in Spain, with officials viewing it as a retrograde step and a failure of European solidarity.

Prime Minister Sánchez stated that while Spain is 'making an enormous effort' to manage the situation, some EU partners are exhibiting a 'selfish' attitude. He emphasized that the challenges posed by irregular migration are a shared European problem, not solely Spain's burden. The Spanish government has called for greater cooperation and a unified approach to tackling the root causes of migration and strengthening external borders. Critics of Italy's decision argue that it undermines the principles of free movement and cooperation that are foundational to the EU. The incident highlights the persistent divisions within the bloc regarding migration policies and the equitable distribution of responsibility in managing external frontiers and asylum processes. Spain is now seeking to rally support from other EU member states for a more cohesive and compassionate response.
