Former US President Donald Trump has declared that any potential nuclear energy agreement with Saudi Arabia will be directly tied to the Kingdom's recognition of Israel and its adherence to the Abraham Accords. Speaking to reporters, Trump, who was instrumental in brokering the normalization agreements between Israel and several Arab nations, underscored the importance of this diplomatic step. The proposed deal, which aims to assist Saudi Arabia in developing civilian nuclear energy capabilities, now faces a significant political hurdle as outlined by the former president.

Trump's remarks suggest that the strategic partnership, which involves sharing sensitive nuclear technology and expertise, is not merely a transactional energy agreement but a move deeply embedded within broader regional normalization efforts. The Abraham Accords, signed in 2020, marked a significant departure from decades of Arab policy towards Israel, establishing diplomatic relations and fostering economic and cultural ties. By making Saudi Arabia's participation in these accords a prerequisite, Trump is signaling his desire to see a wider embrace of normalization across the Middle East.

While the specifics of the proposed nuclear deal remain under discussion, the implication from Trump's statement is clear: Saudi Arabia's ambition for advanced nuclear technology is inextricably linked to its willingness to establish formal ties with Israel. This stance positions the United States, under Trump's influence, as a key arbiter not only of nuclear cooperation but also of regional diplomatic progress. The future of the Saudi nuclear program, therefore, appears to be heavily influenced by the evolving geopolitical landscape of the Middle East and the continued pursuit of normalization among its key players.
