The BBC's report, based on extensive interviews with survivors and analysis of official documents, shines a spotlight on the systematic nature of the alleged abuses. Detainees recounted being subjected to beatings, mock executions, electric shocks, and prolonged periods of solitary confinement. Many described witnessing fellow prisoners being tortured to death. The report names several individuals, including prison commanders and guards, whose alleged actions contributed to a climate of fear and brutality.

Survivors detailed how these detention centers, often repurposed schools or administrative buildings, were used to interrogate and punish Ukrainians suspected of collaborating with the resistance or simply of holding pro-Ukrainian sentiments. The psychological impact on victims has been profound, with many struggling to cope with the trauma long after their release. The article highlights the bravery of those who have spoken out, often at great personal risk, to ensure that those responsible face accountability. The international community is being urged to act on this evidence, with calls for war crime investigations and the prosecution of those implicated in these heinous acts. The BBC's investigation serves as a critical piece of evidence in the ongoing efforts to document and address alleged atrocities committed during the conflict.
