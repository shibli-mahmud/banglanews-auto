An unarmed RC-135 Rivet Joint reconnaissance aircraft belonging to the Royal Air Force (RAF) was intercepted multiple times by Russian military jets in international airspace over the Black Sea. The Ministry of Defence (MoD) described the actions of the Russian pilots as "unnecessarily provocative" and "dangerous," citing manoeuvres that posed a risk to the safety of the RAF crew and the aircraft. The incidents, which are understood to have occurred over several days, involved the Russian aircraft flying in close proximity to the unarmed surveillance plane as it conducted what the MoD described as "routine international flights."

The Rivet Joint is a highly sophisticated intelligence-gathering aircraft, equipped to monitor electronic signals. Its presence in international airspace is not uncommon and is part of ongoing efforts to gather information in strategically important regions. However, the nature of the intercepts has drawn strong criticism from the UK government.

A spokesperson for the MoD stated, "We condemn these a'ttacks and urge Russia to refrain from any action that could escalate tensions or compromise air safety." The specific details of the intercepts, including the type of Russian jets involved and the precise proximity achieved, have not been fully disclosed, but the language used by the MoD suggests a serious disregard for established international aviation protocols. The Black Sea region remains a flashpoint, with frequent military activity from both Russia and NATO member states, making such encounters particularly sensitive.
