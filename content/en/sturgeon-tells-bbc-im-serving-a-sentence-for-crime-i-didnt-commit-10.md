In a candid and at times visibly emotional interview with the BBC's Laura Kuenssberg, former Scottish First Minister Nicola Sturgeon spoke about the profound personal impact of the ongoing police investigation into the SNP's finances.  Sturgeon, who stepped down as First Minister and SNP leader in February 2023, stated that she feels she is "serving a sentence for a crime I didn't commit."  The remarks come as the Scottish National Party faces intense scrutiny over its funding and accounts, with police investigations leading to the arrest and subsequent release on bail of Sturgeon's husband, Peter Murrell, and the party's former treasurer, Colin Beattie.

During the interview, as the details of the investigation and its implications were discussed, Sturgeon struggled to hold back tears on several occasions. She conveyed a sense of frustration and deep personal hurt, suggesting that the prolonged nature of the investigation and the cloud of suspicion hanging over her and the party have taken a significant personal toll.  "It's incredibly difficult," she admitted, her voice breaking, when asked about the personal impact.  "You are being investigated, and it feels like you're being punished for something that you know, deep down, you haven't done."

The former leader maintained her innocence throughout the exchange, emphasizing that she has cooperated fully with the authorities.  However, she acknowledged the damaging effect the scandal is having on the SNP's public image and her own legacy.  The interview offered a rare glimpse into the emotional strain faced by Sturgeon as she navigates the fallout from the investigation, underscoring the personal cost of such prolonged public scrutiny.
