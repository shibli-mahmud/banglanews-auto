The typically peaceful streets of Belfast were plunged into chaos on Tuesday night, as residents watched in horror while their homes were engulfed in flames. The unrest, which began as a protest following a stabbing incident in the north of the city, quickly spiralled into widespread disorder, with petrol bombs and other projectiles being hurled at police and civilian properties. The scenes that unfolded have left an indelible scar on the community, with many left questioning the safety and stability of their neighbourhoods.

One resident, who asked to remain anonymous, described the terrifying experience of being forced to evacuate their home. "I'll never get over watching my home burn," they said, their voice trembling with emotion. "We grabbed what we could and ran. We lost everything. It's just… devastating." The sentiment was echoed by many others, who spoke of the fear and helplessness they felt as the violence intensified around them.

The events have sparked widespread condemnation, with political leaders calling for calm and urging an end to the violence. However, for those directly affected, the immediate concern is rebuilding their lives and coming to terms with the trauma they have endured. The long-term implications of this night of unrest are yet to be fully understood, but it is clear that the resilience of Belfast's communities will be tested in the days and weeks to come.
