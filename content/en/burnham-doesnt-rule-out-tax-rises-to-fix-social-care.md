Andy Burnham, the Mayor of Greater Manchester, has openly acknowledged that significant, long-term investment is urgently needed to overhaul England's struggling social care system. Speaking after a series of cross-party talks aimed at accelerating a review of the sector, Burnham stated that "all options are on the table," explicitly including the potential for tax rises to secure sustainable funding.  This position marks a significant shift, as social care has long been a politically sensitive issue, with successive governments hesitant to impose additional burdens on taxpayers.

The current social care system is widely regarded as being in crisis, with local authorities facing immense financial pressure, leading to reduced services, staff shortages, and a growing number of people unable to access the support they need.  The COVID-19 pandemic further exacerbated existing problems, highlighting the critical role of social care in supporting vulnerable individuals and the wider healthcare system.

Burnham's initiative seeks to move beyond short-term fixes and develop a sustainable, long-term funding model. His cross-party approach is designed to build consensus and ensure that any proposed solutions have broad political backing, making them more resilient to future political changes.  While the exact form of any potential tax increase remains undefined, the Mayor's willingness to consider it signals the severity of the challenges and a commitment to finding a robust solution. The ongoing review is expected to present concrete proposals in the coming months, with a focus on both funding and structural reform.
