India's economic narrative in recent years has been one of impressive headline figures. However, a closer examination reveals a growing disparity, with the benefits of this boom largely accruing to the top echelons of society. While corporate profits soar and the stock market reaches new heights, a substantial portion of the population remains largely untouched by this prosperity. This 'top-heavy' growth, driven by sectors that disproportionately favor capital and highly skilled labor, has led to widening income inequality. The benefits of economic expansion are not trickling down effectively to the lower and middle classes, who continue to grapple with stagnant wages, rising costs of living, and limited access to quality education and healthcare.

This situation presents a stark warning for Bangladesh. As the nation strives to achieve upper-middle-income status and beyond, it must learn from India's experience and prioritize an inclusive growth strategy. A focus solely on aggregate GDP growth risks exacerbating existing inequalities and creating a fragile economic structure. Bangladesh needs to ensure that its development initiatives are designed to uplift all segments of society, not just a select few. This includes investing in sectors that create widespread employment opportunities, strengthening social safety nets, and implementing progressive tax policies. Furthermore, policies that promote small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and foster skill development for the broader workforce are crucial. The ultimate measure of economic success should not just be the size of the pie, but how equitably it is shared. Ignoring this fundamental principle, as India's current trajectory suggests, could lead to social unrest and undermine long-term economic stability.
