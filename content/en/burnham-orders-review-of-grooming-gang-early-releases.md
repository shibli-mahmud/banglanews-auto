Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham has launched a critical review into the early release mechanisms for individuals convicted of serious grooming offences, particularly those involving child sexual exploitation. The decision comes in response to mounting concerns that some sex offenders might be released from prison prematurely, posing a potential risk to vulnerable individuals and communities.  Mayor Burnham stated that public safety is paramount and that the review will examine current sentencing guidelines, parole board decisions, and rehabilitation programmes for convicted groomers.

The announcement follows pressure from within the Conservative party, with calls for a crackdown on early releases for serious offenders.  While details of specific cases that prompted the review have not been widely disclosed, the underlying sentiment is a desire to strengthen safeguards against reoffending.  The review will involve collaboration with relevant agencies, including the Prison Service, the Probation Service, and child protection charities.  The objective is to identify any systemic weaknesses that could allow for early release and to propose robust measures to prevent it.  Furthermore, the review is expected to explore how effectively offenders are being monitored and supported upon their release to minimise the likelihood of them re-engaging in criminal behaviour.  The findings and recommendations from this review are anticipated to have implications for national policy on offender management and public protection strategies across the UK, aiming to restore public confidence in the justice system's ability to protect children.
