In a surprising turn of events, U.S. President Donald Trump revealed on Saturday that he had ordered the cancellation of planned retaliatory strikes against Iran. The strikes were reportedly in response to Iran's downing of a U.S. drone earlier in the week. Trump stated that the decision to halt the military action was made after "the room came together" and that "Iran is saying they want to talk." He further elaborated that "several of the (other) countries asked me, 'Don't go too fast, let's get the perimeters of a deal made, and you can deal with it afterwards.'"

Trump indicated that the proposed deal was not yet finalized but that "perimeters" for an agreement had been established. He suggested that the "real, and greatly appreciated, respect" shown by Iran in requesting a delay was a key factor in his decision. The President implied that the U.S. response would be different in the absence of this dialogue. This abrupt shift in policy follows days of escalating tensions in the Persian Gulf and signals a potential de-escalation of the conflict, at least in the immediate term. However, the specifics of any potential deal remain unclear, and the situation continues to be closely monitored by international observers.
