In a dramatic turn of events, thousands of migrants, primarily from sub-Saharan Africa, managed to enter the Spanish territory of Ceuta over the past few days, bypassing heavily fortified borders. This unprecedented surge has overwhelmed local authorities and prompted Spain to request an urgent meeting with European Union counterparts.  The situation has become a focal point for the long-standing challenges the EU faces in managing irregular migration. Spain has been vocal in its criticism, suggesting that some member states are not doing enough to control their external borders, which ultimately impacts countries like Spain that are direct gateways to Europe.  Concerns are mounting regarding humanitarian conditions for the migrants and the security implications for the enclave.  The emergency meeting is expected to focus on immediate containment measures, potential burden-sharing arrangements, and a re-evaluation of the EU's broader migration and asylum strategies.  Diplomatic tensions are high as leaders grapple with finding a unified and effective response to this unfolding crisis, which underscores the precariousness of EU external border management and the urgent need for solidarity among member nations.
