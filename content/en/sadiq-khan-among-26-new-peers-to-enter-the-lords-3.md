London Mayor Sadiq Khan is among 26 new life peers who will be entering the House of Lords, according to an announcement from Downing Street. The list of appointments, released on Friday, includes a diverse range of individuals recognized for their contributions to public life, arts, and public service. 

Khan, a member of the Labour Party, has served as the Mayor of London since 2016. His elevation to the Lords is a significant political development, though it is expected that he will continue in his role as Mayor. The exact timing of his formal entry into the upper house of Parliament has not yet been specified. 

Joining Khan in the new cohort of peers are several other notable figures. Broadcaster and television presenter June Sarpong, known for her work on shows like 'Loose Women' and her advocacy for diversity, has also been appointed. Christina McAnea, the former General Secretary of UNISON, one of the UK's largest trade unions, is another high-profile appointee, reflecting a continued recognition of union leadership in public life. 

The other appointees represent a broad spectrum of society, from former politicians and business leaders to academics and cultural figures. The appointments, made by King Charles III on the advice of the Prime Minister, Rishi Sunak, are part of the regular process of creating life peers, which grants members the title of Baron or Baroness and the right to sit and vote in the House of Lords. The House of Lords serves as the second chamber of the UK Parliament, scrutinizing legislation and holding the government to account. The addition of these 26 individuals will bring new perspectives and expertise to the legislative process.
