The Spanish government has voiced its deep disappointment with the reactions of some EU partners to the escalating migrant situation in the Spanish North African enclave of Ceuta.  Madrid alleges that a number of member states have adopted a 'selfish' approach, failing to offer adequate support and instead focusing on their own immediate concerns.  This criticism comes in the wake of Italy's temporary suspension of the Schengen arrangement with Spain, a move that Spanish officials have interpreted as a sign of isolation rather than solidarity. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni of Italy described the scenes in Ceuta as 'shocking,' implicitly placing blame on Spain for the situation.  However, Spain argues that this is a shared European challenge, requiring a united front and collaborative solutions, not unilateral actions that undermine the principle of free movement and shared responsibility within the Schengen Area.  The influx of thousands of Moroccan migrants into Ceuta over a short period has placed immense strain on the territory's resources and infrastructure, prompting Spain to call for greater EU-level coordination and assistance.  The Spanish Foreign Ministry has emphasized that the crisis in Ceuta is not merely an internal Spanish issue but a complex challenge with significant implications for the entire bloc, necessitating a robust and unified European response.  The government is urging a reassessment of border management strategies and a more equitable distribution of responsibilities among member states to effectively address such humanitarian and security concerns.
