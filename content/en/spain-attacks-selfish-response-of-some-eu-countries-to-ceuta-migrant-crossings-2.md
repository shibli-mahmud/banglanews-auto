Spain's government has launched a scathing critique against several European Union countries, accusing them of adopting a 'selfish' and uncooperative stance in the face of the escalating migrant crisis in Ceuta. The situation intensified this week when thousands of migrants, primarily from sub-Saharan Africa, managed to enter the Spanish enclave bordering Morocco. The influx led to overcrowded conditions and strained resources, prompting a strong Spanish response including border reinforcement and deportations.

Key among the criticized nations is Italy, whose Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni described the scenes in Ceuta as 'shocking' and announced a temporary suspension of the Schengen arrangement with Spain. This move, intended to bolster border controls, has been interpreted by Madrid as a betrayal of EU solidarity principles. Spanish officials argue that while the situation in Ceuta is undoubtedly challenging, it is a symptom of broader migratory pressures affecting the entire continent, not just Spain. They emphasize that shared responsibility and coordinated solutions are paramount to managing such humanitarian challenges effectively.

The Spanish foreign ministry has called for an urgent meeting of EU interior ministers to discuss the crisis and devise a common strategy. "We cannot allow a situation where some countries prioritize their national interests at the expense of the collective European responsibility," a Spanish diplomat stated. The government insists that isolating Spain or imposing unilateral measures like border suspensions is counterproductive and undermines the fundamental tenets of the Schengen agreement, which facilitates free movement within the EU. The ongoing diplomatic tensions highlight the deep divisions within the EU regarding migration policy and border management, posing a significant challenge to bloc-wide cooperation.
