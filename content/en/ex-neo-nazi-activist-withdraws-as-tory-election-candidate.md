Joshua Bonehill-Paine, who has a history of white supremacist and neo-Nazi activism, has officially withdrawn his application to be a Conservative Party candidate for the upcoming general election. The decision comes after significant public scrutiny and criticism directed at the party for considering a candidate with such a controversial past.  Sources close to the matter, including comments attributed to Business Secretary Kemi Badenoch, suggest that Bonehill-Paine will not be standing but will instead transition to an advisory role.  His purported expertise in "understanding extremist groups" will be leveraged to assist the Conservative Party in developing its approach to tackling radicalisation and far-right extremism within the UK.  This move has been met with a mixed reaction. While some within the party may see it as a pragmatic way to gain insight into extremist ideologies, civil liberties groups and anti-racism campaigners have expressed serious concerns. They argue that associating with or seeking advice from individuals with a history of promoting hate speech is inappropriate and could inadvertently legitimize such views.  The Conservative Party has not officially commented on the exact nature or scope of Bonehill-Paine's potential advisory role, nor has it detailed how his input will be incorporated into policy. The withdrawal itself is a significant concession, but the proposed advisory position raises further questions about the party's vetting processes and its commitment to inclusive politics.
