Scottish authorities have escalated their response to a fast-moving wildfire in the Cairngorms National Park, officially designating it a 'major incident'. The blaze, which ignited yesterday, has rapidly consumed large swathes of moorland and woodland, sending plumes of smoke across the picturesque landscape. The primary concern for emergency services is the encroaching threat to the village of Nethy Bridge, where some residents have already begun evacuating their properties as a precautionary measure. Fire crews are working tirelessly to contain the wildfire, battling challenging terrain and unpredictable wind conditions. The Scottish Fire and Rescue Service has deployed multiple appliances and specialist teams to the scene, with aerial support also being utilized. Local police have established a cordon around the affected area and are advising the public to avoid the vicinity to allow emergency responders unimpeded access. The cause of the wildfire is currently under investigation, though dry conditions and high temperatures have been cited as significant contributing factors to its rapid spread. The incident underscores the growing risk of wildfires in Scotland during periods of extreme weather, prompting calls for increased vigilance and fire prevention measures. Further updates on the containment efforts and any potential impact on residents will be provided as the situation develops.
