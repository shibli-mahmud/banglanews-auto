Nigel Farage, the former leader of the UK Independence Party (UKIP) and a prominent figure in the Brexit movement, has resigned his seat as the Member of Parliament for Clacton. The resignation, confirmed on Tuesday, has immediately paved the way for a by-election in the Essex constituency. Farage, however, has not retreated from the political arena, promptly announcing his intention to stand as a candidate in the forthcoming by-election, aiming to secure a return to Parliament. 

His departure comes at a time when he is reportedly facing a significant tax dispute. Details surrounding the exact nature and scale of the financial issue remain under scrutiny, but it is understood to be a key factor in his decision to resign. The controversy has cast a shadow over his political future and has become the central focus of the impending electoral battle.

In a remarkable turn of events, the major political parties have opted out of contesting the Clacton by-election. The Labour Party, the ruling Conservative Party, and the Liberal Democrats have all confirmed they will not be putting forward their own candidates. Additionally, the nascent political group Restore Britain, which advocates for a more moderate approach to post-Brexit Britain, has also declared its intention to boycott the election. This unified stance by the opposition parties means Farage will likely face a significantly less competitive race to retain his seat, effectively a de facto endorsement of his candidacy by the established political forces, albeit through their abstention. The implications of this orchestrated boycott on the dynamics of the by-election and on parliamentary politics are expected to be significant.
