Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has firmly stated that Israel will not withdraw its forces from the Gaza Strip until the militant group Hamas has been 'genuinely' disarmed. This declaration comes in response to reports of a 15-point peace plan, purportedly put forward by former U.S. President Donald Trump, aimed at resolving the protracted conflict. While details of the proposed plan remain largely undisclosed to the public, Netanyahu's unequivocal stance suggests significant reservations from the Israeli leadership regarding its implications for national security.

The Prime Minister's office emphasized that Israel's primary objective remains the dismantling of Hamas's military capabilities and governance in Gaza, a goal that they believe cannot be achieved without a continued military presence. The sentiment within the Israeli government appears to be that any premature withdrawal or a plan that does not guarantee the complete disarmament of Hamas would jeopardize Israel's security interests and fail to prevent future attacks. 

Netanyahu's remarks highlight the complex geopolitical landscape surrounding the Gaza conflict, with international actors attempting to mediate a path towards de-escalation and long-term stability. However, Israel's current position, as articulated by its leader, underscores the deep-seated security concerns that continue to dictate its strategic decisions in the region. The rejection of the Trump-backed plan, if confirmed, suggests that any viable resolution will need to address Israel's stringent security demands to gain its full approval.
