In a candid and at times emotional interview, Nicola Sturgeon addressed the seismic events that have rocked the Scottish National Party, including the guilty plea of her husband, Peter Murrell, to embezzling £400,000. Speaking to Laura Kuenssberg for the BBC, Sturgeon described the situation as "utterly devastating" and "heartbreaking," emphasizing that she only learned of the details of the charges against Murrell "in the public domain."  She maintained that she had no prior knowledge of his alleged actions and expressed her shock and disappointment.

The former First Minister also delved into the reasons behind her surprise resignation from the top political office earlier this year. While acknowledging the intense scrutiny and pressure that comes with leading the SNP, Sturgeon suggested that the "brutal" nature of modern political life had taken its toll. She alluded to the relentless attacks and the constant need for public performance as factors that contributed to her decision to step down, stating she felt she had become a "lightning rod" for criticism.

Kuenssberg pressed Sturgeon on the ongoing investigations into the SNP's finances, particularly the questions surrounding the £600,000 raised for independence campaigning. Sturgeon reiterated her commitment to cooperating fully with any inquiries, stressing the importance of transparency and accountability for the party. She expressed hope that the investigations would bring clarity and allow the SNP to move forward. The interview offered a rare glimpse into Sturgeon's personal reflections on her tenure and the challenges she faced, providing a complex and layered perspective on a tumultuous period for Scottish politics.
