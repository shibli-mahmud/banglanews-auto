In a significant policy announcement, Andy Burnham, the Mayor of Greater Manchester, has advocated for a radical shift in fiscal devolution, proposing that regional mayors should receive a direct share of national income tax revenue. Burnham argues that this would provide a crucial and sustainable funding stream for local authorities, enabling them to invest in vital public services, infrastructure, and economic development initiatives tailored to the specific needs of their regions.  He stated that the move is designed to grant genuine power and accountability to 'every postcode,' allowing local leaders to make decisions that directly benefit their communities without being solely reliant on central government grants, which can often be unpredictable and subject to national political priorities.  The vision is to create a more dynamic and responsive system of governance, where the fruits of local economic activity are reinvested locally, stimulating further growth and prosperity.

However, the proposal has been met with a degree of caution and criticism. Opposition parties and some economic analysts have raised concerns about the practicalities of implementing such a system. Key questions revolve around how the share of income tax would be calculated and distributed, and what safeguards would be in place to ensure fiscal responsibility at the regional level. Critics also point to the potential for this to undermine national fiscal planning and create disparities between regions.  The government has acknowledged the ambition of the proposal but has yet to commit to its implementation, emphasizing the need for further detail and consultation.  The debate is likely to intensify as the implications of such a significant transfer of fiscal power are thoroughly examined.
