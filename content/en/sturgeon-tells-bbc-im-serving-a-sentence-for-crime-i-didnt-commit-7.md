In a rare and deeply personal interview with the BBC's Laura Kuenssberg, Nicola Sturgeon, the former leader of the Scottish National Party (SNP), has articulated her distress over the ongoing embezzlement investigation that has cast a shadow over her party and her own legacy.  Sturgeon, who stepped down as First Minister in February 2023, spoke with palpable emotion about the strain of the prolonged inquiry, stating, 'I feel like I'm serving a sentence for a crime that I didn't commit.'

The interview, broadcast on Sunday, saw Kuenssberg pressing Sturgeon on the financial irregularities that have plagued the SNP, a situation that has seen both Sturgeon and her husband, Peter Murrell, a former chief executive of the SNP, questioned by Police Scotland. While Sturgeon maintained her innocence and stated her full cooperation with the authorities, the weight of the situation was evident.  At several points during the discussion, her voice wavered, and she appeared to be on the verge of tears as she grappled with the accusations and the public scrutiny.

Sturgeon defended her tenure and the integrity of the SNP under her leadership, emphasizing that the party had always operated with transparency. However, she acknowledged the damaging nature of the allegations and the impact they were having on public trust. The former First Minister did not shy away from the personal toll the investigation has taken, describing it as a 'very difficult period' for her and her family.  The interview offered a stark glimpse into the personal anguish of a political figure facing intense pressure amid a widening investigation, leaving many to ponder the eventual outcome and its implications for Scottish politics.
