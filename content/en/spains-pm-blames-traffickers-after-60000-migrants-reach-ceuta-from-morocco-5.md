Spain's Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has strongly condemned human trafficking organizations, accusing them of exploiting the situation after an unprecedented surge of migrants crossed into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta from neighboring Morocco. The influx, which began earlier this week, saw an estimated 60,000 individuals, many of them minors, enter the territory, overwhelming local resources and sparking a significant humanitarian and security challenge.  Sánchez stated that the Spanish government was working with Moroccan authorities to manage the situation and reiterated that the vast majority of those who entered had been returned to Morocco by Friday evening.  The crisis, however, has not been without its diplomatic repercussions. Italy, citing the strain on border controls and the potential for further uncontrolled movement, announced it was suspending its participation in the Schengen agreement on free movement with Spain.  This move by Rome underscores the broader European concerns about managing external borders and the impact of irregular migration. Spanish officials have expressed frustration, emphasizing that the responsibility lies with the trafficking networks who facilitated the dangerous journey.  The situation in Ceuta remains tense, with ongoing efforts to assess the number of individuals still present and to address the humanitarian needs of those affected by the mass migration.
