Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of Israel has publicly dismissed a 15-point peace proposal put forth by former U.S. President Donald Trump regarding the Gaza Strip. Netanyahu asserted that Israel's military will not withdraw from Gaza until the militant group Hamas is 'genuinely' disarmed, a condition that appears to be non-negotiable for the current Israeli government. This firm stance underscores Israel's paramount concern with its long-term security and the elimination of perceived threats from Hamas.

The details of Trump's plan have not been fully disclosed, but reports suggest it aimed to outline a path towards a potential ceasefire and future governance in Gaza. However, Netanyahu's rejection implies that the plan, whatever its specifics, did not align with Israel's strategic priorities. The Prime Minister has consistently emphasized the necessity of dismantling Hamas's military capabilities as a prerequisite for any lasting peace or Israeli withdrawal. He reiterated that the ongoing military operation in Gaza is essential to achieve this objective and prevent future attacks on Israeli citizens.

This development highlights the significant challenges in achieving a diplomatic resolution to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, particularly concerning Gaza. While international actors often seek de-escalation and humanitarian solutions, Israel prioritizes its security interests and the eradication of groups it deems terrorist organizations. Netanyahu's statement suggests that any plan for Gaza's future must first address the disarmament of Hamas to Israel's satisfaction, a demand that is likely to complicate future negotiations and diplomatic efforts.
