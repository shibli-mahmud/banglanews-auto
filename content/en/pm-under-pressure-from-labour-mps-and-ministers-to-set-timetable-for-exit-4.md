Prime Minister Rishi Sunak is reportedly facing mounting pressure from within his own party and from the opposition to articulate a definitive timeline for his departure from Downing Street. Sources close to the matter have indicated to the BBC that a significant contingent of Labour MPs, alongside some ministers, are actively advocating for the Prime Minister to set out a clear exit strategy. This internal and external push for clarity comes at a critical juncture for the government, amidst ongoing economic challenges and public scrutiny.

The Transport Secretary is said to be among those who have advised Labour leader Keir Starmer on the potential benefits of pushing for such a timetable. The rationale behind this strategy is believed to be rooted in the desire to inject a sense of predictability and stability into the political landscape.  By forcing the Prime Minister to commit to a date, Labour hopes to gain a strategic advantage and begin to frame the narrative around a future government.  However, the specific reasons for the pressure and the potential implications for the Conservative party remain subjects of intense speculation.  Whether this concerted effort will result in a public announcement from the Prime Minister remains to be seen, but the calls for a defined exit plan are becoming increasingly audible.
