In a dramatic escalation of the ongoing conflict, Israeli airstrikes pounded the southern Lebanese city of Tyre and its surrounding areas early Tuesday, despite a direct warning from Iran on Monday that it might resume hostilities if attacks on its key regional ally, Hezbollah, did not cease. The Iranian Foreign Ministry had issued a stern statement declaring that "the patience of the resistance axis is running out" and that "they are watching carefully." 

Witnesses in Tyre reported hearing multiple explosions and seeing plumes of smoke rising from the city. Preliminary reports indicate that several locations, suspected of being Hezbollah infrastructure or operational bases, were targeted. The scale of the damage and any potential casualties are still being assessed, but the predawn timing suggests an effort to maximize disruption and minimize potential civilian exposure, though this remains unconfirmed. 

The Israeli military has not immediately commented on the specific targets or the extent of the strikes in Tyre. However, its ongoing operations in Lebanon have been framed as a response to persistent rocket fire and cross-border incursions by Hezbollah. The timing of the Tyre strikes, coming mere hours after Iran's pointed warning, signals a defiant stance from Israel and raises serious concerns about a broader regional conflagration. International observers are closely monitoring the situation, with fears that the tit-for-tat exchanges could engulf the wider Middle East in conflict.
