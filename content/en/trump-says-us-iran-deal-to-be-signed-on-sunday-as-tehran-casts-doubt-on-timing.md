President Donald Trump has declared that a deal with Iran is slated for signing on Sunday, a statement that has been met with cautious optimism and significant skepticism. The US president's pronouncements, often delivered via social media, signal a potential breakthrough in protracted negotiations that have aimed to curb Iran's nuclear program and ease sanctions. However, the Iranian side has moved to temper expectations regarding the immediate finalization of any agreement.  Foreign ministry officials in Tehran have indicated that while a deal is indeed in the works, a definitive date for its signing has not been set.  A spokesperson for the Iranian foreign ministry explicitly stated that the agreement 'will not be tomorrow,' implying that the timeline Trump presented might be premature or inaccurate. This divergence in public statements highlights the delicate nature of the ongoing diplomatic efforts and the potential for misunderstandings or strategic posturing between the two nations.  Analysts suggest that while both sides appear committed to reaching an accord, the specifics of the implementation and the final ratification process are still subject to considerable negotiation. The exact terms of the proposed deal remain largely undisclosed, adding to the speculation and uncertainty surrounding its imminent signing.  The international community will be closely watching for further developments, hoping for a stable resolution that can de-escalate regional tensions and prevent Iran from acquiring nuclear weapons.
