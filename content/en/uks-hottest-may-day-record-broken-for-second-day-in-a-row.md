The United Kingdom experienced a sweltering heatwave that saw its hottest May day record broken for the second successive day on Thursday. Provisional figures released by meteorological authorities confirmed that temperatures soared to a remarkable 35.1C in Kew Gardens, located in south-west London. This extraordinary reading surpassed the previous day's record, which itself had broken a long-standing benchmark for the month of May.

The heatwave, which has gripped much of the country, has prompted health warnings and advice for people to stay hydrated and avoid strenuous activity during the peak heat of the day. The Met Office, the UK's national weather service, has been monitoring the situation closely, with forecasters indicating that the unusually high temperatures are a result of a persistent area of high pressure drawing warm air from continental Europe.

This prolonged period of intense heat is a significant meteorological event, with the previous record for the hottest May day standing at 32.8C, recorded in the UK in 1944. The breaking of this record for two consecutive days highlights the unusual nature of the current weather patterns. While the extreme heat offers a taste of summer for many, it also raises concerns about potential impacts on public health, infrastructure, and the environment. Authorities are urging vigilance and preparedness as the nation grapples with this exceptional spell of warm weather.
