The police probe, which commenced in 2019, initially focused on the handling of donations intended for the Scottish independence campaign. As the investigation has unfolded, it has unearthed a complex web of financial dealings and internal party processes that have come under intense public and political examination. The prolonged nature of the inquiry has undoubtedly fueled speculation and concern, creating a climate of uncertainty that has impacted the SNP's public image and internal morale.

Key questions persist regarding the alleged misuse of funds, the adequacy of internal financial oversight mechanisms within the SNP, and the timeline of knowledge held by senior party figures. The lack of definitive answers has allowed the controversy to fester, becoming a persistent point of contention in Scottish politics. Opposition parties have seized upon the allegations to question the SNP's integrity and competence, further intensifying the pressure on the ruling party.

The controversy has not only raised questions about financial probity but also about the broader principles of accountability and transparency within political organisations. The slow pace of the investigation has undoubtedly contributed to the 'toxic cloud' described, leaving many to wonder when, or if, a clear resolution will be reached. The ramifications extend beyond the immediate legal proceedings, impacting the SNP's ability to effectively campaign and govern, and potentially influencing voter perception ahead of future elections.
