Nicola Sturgeon, the former leader of the Scottish National Party (SNP) and First Minister, has voiced her deep personal anguish over the ongoing police investigation into the party's finances. In a candid BBC interview, Sturgeon stated, 'I feel as though I'm serving a sentence for a crime I didn't commit.' Her voice reportedly trembled at points, and she struggled to hold back tears as she discussed the impact of the embezzlement scandal, which has cast a long shadow over her legacy and the SNP's reputation.

The investigation, which has seen the arrest and questioning of several senior SNP figures, including Sturgeon's husband and former party chief executive, Peter Murrell, has plunged the party into turmoil. Sturgeon, who stepped down as First Minister in February 2023, has been steadfast in her denials of any wrongdoing, but the prolonged nature of the inquiry has clearly taken a significant emotional toll. She described the process as 'incredibly difficult' and 'frustrating,' emphasizing her commitment to cooperating fully with the authorities while also asserting her innocence.

The former First Minister's remarks highlight the profound personal cost of such investigations, even for those who maintain their innocence. The interview, which aimed to provide clarity and address public concerns, instead underscored the raw emotion and evident strain on Sturgeon. The ongoing scrutiny and the protracted legal proceedings continue to dominate headlines, leaving the SNP grappling with its internal challenges and the wider public's perception of its integrity. Sturgeon's words suggest a growing sense of injustice and weariness as she navigates this difficult chapter.
