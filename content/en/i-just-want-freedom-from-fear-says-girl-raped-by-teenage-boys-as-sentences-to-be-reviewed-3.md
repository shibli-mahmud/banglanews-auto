The victim, speaking anonymously to BBC Newsnight, described the persistent and debilitating impact of the attack, revealing she is still experiencing severe flashbacks and lives in constant fear. 'It’s like a shadow that follows me everywhere,' she shared, her voice trembling. 'I can't sleep properly. Every little noise makes me jump. I just want to feel safe again, to not have this dread hanging over me all the time.'

The sentencing of the teenage perpetrators has drawn criticism, prompting a review by the Attorney General's office. While the specifics of the sentences remain sensitive due to the ages of those involved and ongoing legal processes, the victim's courageous decision to speak publicly highlights the societal imperative to ensure justice is not only served but also perceived as commensurate with the harm inflicted. Her words serve as a stark reminder of the long-lasting psychological scars that victims of sexual violence carry, and the urgent need for robust support systems and appropriate legal consequences.

The review of the sentences is expected to consider whether they adequately reflect the severity of the crime and the devastating impact on the survivor. The victim's plea for 'freedom from fear' underscores the broader societal challenge of addressing sexual violence and supporting survivors on their journey to recovery and reclaiming their lives from the grip of trauma.
