A new law is set to dramatically increase the closure period for businesses found to be operating illegally, with mini-marts and other smaller retail outlets facing potential shutdowns of up to 12 months. This significant toughening of penalties, which previously capped closures at six months for establishments in England and Wales, has been directly influenced by investigative journalism that exposed ongoing non-compliance with trading standards and licensing regulations. The BBC's reporting brought to light instances where businesses repeatedly violated laws concerning product sourcing, hygiene standards, and trading hours, with the existing penalties deemed insufficient to deter persistent offenders.

Local authorities will now have enhanced powers to enforce these stricter closure orders. The change aims to provide a more effective deterrent against illegal trading practices that can undermine legitimate businesses, pose public health risks, and contribute to the illicit economy. Officials hope the extended closure periods will send a clear message that such violations will no longer be met with minor inconveniences but with substantial business disruption. The move is expected to be welcomed by law-abiding businesses and consumer protection groups, who have long argued for more robust enforcement mechanisms to ensure a level playing field and safeguard public interest. The legislation is currently undergoing parliamentary review, with full implementation anticipated in the coming months.
