The air in Sano Barkhu hangs heavy with the scent of mud and despair. Where homes once stood, rows of flattened debris now stretch as far as the eye can see.  The vibrant colours of everyday life have been washed away, replaced by a monotonous palette of grey and brown.  BBC correspondents on the ground have witnessed firsthand the heartbreaking aftermath, speaking with survivors who have lost everything.

Locals recount tales of a sudden, terrifying rush of water, the roar of the floods drowning out desperate cries.  Children's toys, furniture, and the remnants of everyday life are scattered amongst the mud and uprooted trees, a poignant reminder of the lives that were irrevocably altered in a matter of hours.  The floods, exacerbated by heavy monsoon rains and the overflowing of local rivers, have not only destroyed homes but also decimated farmland, the lifeline for many of these rural communities.  Essential infrastructure, including roads and bridges, has also been severely damaged, hindering rescue efforts and the delivery of much-needed aid.

The BBC's report highlights the immense challenge of rebuilding from scratch. Survivors are grappling with the immediate need for shelter, food, and clean water, while the long-term prospect of restoring their village and their livelihoods looms large. The once-familiar landscape of Sano Barkhu is now a barren testament to nature's fury, a village lost to the relentless force of the water, leaving behind a community struggling to find hope amidst the ruins.
