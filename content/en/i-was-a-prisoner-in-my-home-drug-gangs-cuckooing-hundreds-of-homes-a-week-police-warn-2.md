UK police forces are raising serious concerns about the escalating trend of 'cuckooing', a predatory tactic employed by drug gangs to exploit vulnerable individuals and seize control of their homes. This practice, where criminals essentially displace the legitimate resident, often involves targeting individuals with addiction issues, mental health problems, or those living in isolation. Gangs gain access to a property, sometimes by offering drugs or money, and then use it as a base for their operations, including drug dealing and storage. The original occupant is often threatened, coerced, or intimidated into leaving or tolerating the unwelcome presence, effectively becoming a prisoner in their own home.  Authorities report that hundreds, and possibly thousands, of such instances are occurring weekly across the nation.  Detective Chief Inspector [Name], a leading officer in tackling county lines crime, stated, 'We are seeing a significant increase in cuckooing. These gangs are ruthless and will exploit anyone they perceive as weak. The impact on victims is devastating, leading to extreme fear, isolation, and a complete loss of control over their personal space.'  Police are urging the public to be vigilant for signs of cuckooing, such as unusual comings and goings at a neighbour's property, signs of drug use, or if a vulnerable individual appears to be under duress or being controlled by others. Initiatives are being ramped up to support victims and disrupt the networks responsible for this insidious crime.
