Holidaymakers embarking on journeys to the continent via the Port of Dover are being urged to prepare for substantial delays as the port anticipates significant congestion. The primary driver for these warnings is the recent full implementation of the European Union's new Entry/Exit System (EES). This sophisticated digital border control system requires travellers from non-EU countries, including UK citizens, to register their passport details, fingerprints, and facial images upon entry into the Schengen Area.

The EES aims to enhance security and speed up border crossings in the long run. However, during its initial rollout and subsequent busy periods, it is expected to significantly slow down processing times at border checkpoints. For the Port of Dover, a vital gateway to France and mainland Europe, this translates directly into potential for lengthy queues and extended waiting times for both passengers and freight. Ferry operators have echoed these concerns, advising travellers to allow considerably more time for check-in and pre-departure procedures than they might be accustomed to.

This half-term holiday represents the first significant test for the EES during a period of peak travel since its complete activation. While the system's long-term benefits are touted, the immediate impact is likely to be felt most acutely by travellers during this busy holiday exodus. Passengers are advised to ensure they have all necessary documentation readily available and to consult their ferry operator's specific guidance regarding pre-travel preparations to mitigate potential disruptions.
