The controversial digital ID project, which had been a flagship initiative of the previous administration, is now officially on hold indefinitely. Sources close to the new government suggest that the substantial financial and administrative investment required for the digital ID system would be better utilized in addressing the pressing economic challenges facing households across the nation.  The rising cost of energy, food, and housing has become the paramount concern, and Burnham's government aims to demonstrate a proactive and responsive approach.

This policy U-turn is expected to be met with a mix of reactions. While proponents of the digital ID envisioned it as a tool for streamlining services and enhancing security, critics had raised concerns about privacy, data security, and the potential for exclusion.  The decision to abandon it in favor of cost of living measures suggests a government keen on demonstrating tangible benefits to the electorate in the short term.  The spokesperson elaborated that consultations will be undertaken to understand the most effective ways to alleviate financial pressures on families, with potential measures including targeted support packages, energy bill relief, and initiatives to combat inflation.  The focus on the immediate needs of the population marks a departure from the more ambitious, long-term digital infrastructure projects, signaling a pragmatic and people-centric agenda from the outset of Burnham's premiership. Further details on the specific cost of living initiatives are anticipated in the coming weeks.
