In a development that casts a new light on the handling of Prince Andrew's affairs, it has emerged that the Royal Household received a significant cache of emails belonging to the Duke of York six years ago, specifically in 2020. These emails reportedly contain thousands of messages and delve into his financial dealings, a subject that has drawn considerable scrutiny in recent years. The timing of this delivery is particularly noteworthy, arriving at a period when Prince Andrew's association with convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein and subsequent allegations of sexual assault were gaining intense public attention.

The nature of the "envoy" emails is not explicitly detailed, but their transfer to the Palace suggests a potential connection to his activities and interactions during his time as a special representative for trade and investment. This role saw him travel extensively and engage with various international figures and businesses, raising questions about the extent of financial entanglements and potential conflicts of interest.

Sources close to the matter indicate that the emails were handed over as part of an effort to provide a comprehensive overview of Prince Andrew's past activities. However, the six-year delay between their original creation or relevance and their delivery to the Palace raises concerns about transparency and the effectiveness of internal oversight mechanisms within the monarchy. The extent to which these emails were reviewed, or if they informed any decisions regarding Prince Andrew's public duties or private life, remains unclear. This revelation is likely to fuel further debate about accountability and the management of sensitive information within the highest echelons of the British establishment.
