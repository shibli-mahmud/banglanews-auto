In a rare and deeply personal interview, Nicola Sturgeon, the former leader of Scotland, conveyed a profound sense of personal injustice as she discussed the ongoing investigation into the finances of the Scottish National Party (SNP). Speaking to the BBC's Laura Kuenssberg, Sturgeon stated, "I am serving a sentence for a crime I didn't commit." Her voice reportedly trembled at times, and she struggled to maintain her composure as she reflected on the impact of the scandal, which has seen her home and party headquarters searched by police.  The former First Minister, who stepped down earlier this year, maintained her innocence and expressed frustration with the protracted nature of the inquiry. She emphasized that she has cooperated fully with the authorities and that the lack of a clear resolution has been personally devastating.  Sturgeon highlighted the public perception and the relentless media scrutiny, which she feels has already amounted to a form of punishment, regardless of the investigation's ultimate outcome.  The interview touched upon the immense pressure of her leadership and the toll the recent events have taken on her and her family.  Kuenssberg pressed Sturgeon on specific details of the investigation, but Sturgeon repeatedly deferred to the ongoing police inquiry, stating it would be inappropriate to comment on specifics.  However, her impassioned defence and visible emotion underscored the personal anguish she is experiencing, painting a stark picture of a political figure feeling trapped by circumstances beyond her control.
