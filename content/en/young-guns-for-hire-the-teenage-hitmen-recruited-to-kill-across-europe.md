The shadowy world of organized crime has found a new, alarming recruitment pool: teenagers.  Across Europe, law enforcement agencies are grappling with a disturbing surge in juvenile involvement in high-level criminal activities, most notably as contract killers.  The Foxtrot Network, a sophisticated and ruthless criminal syndicate, has been identified as a key player in this disturbing phenomenon.  Intelligence suggests this organization is responsible for a grim tally of around 35 murders throughout the continent, a figure that is likely to be an underestimate.

What makes the Foxtrot Network particularly insidious is its adoption of 'violence-as-a-service' tactics. This means they offer lethal services to clients, carrying out assassinations and other violent acts for a price. Crucially, their preferred operatives for these high-risk missions are often teenagers. These young individuals, some as young as 15 or 16, are recruited, groomed, and then deployed for contract killings. The motivations for their recruitment are varied, ranging from financial desperation and social exclusion to the allure of power and belonging within a criminal gang.

The use of minors as hitmen presents a complex challenge for authorities. Teenagers are often less experienced, making them potentially more susceptible to manipulation and coercion. However, they can also be perceived as less likely to attract immediate suspicion from law enforcement. Their youth can be a double-edged sword for the criminals, offering a degree of deniability and a seemingly disposable workforce. The increasing sophistication of these networks, coupled with their willingness to exploit vulnerable young people, highlights the evolving nature of organized crime and poses a significant threat to public safety across Europe.
