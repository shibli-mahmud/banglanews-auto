The mercury soared across swathes of southern England on Wednesday afternoon, culminating in a new all-time high for June at 36.1 degrees Celsius (97F) recorded in Coningsby, Lincolnshire. This remarkable temperature surpassed the previous record of 35.6C set in 1976.  The intense heatwave, fuelled by hot air originating from continental Europe, has prompted health warnings and advice for people to stay hydrated and avoid strenuous activity.  Hospitals are on high alert, and the public is being urged to check on vulnerable individuals, including the elderly and those with pre-existing health conditions, who are most at risk from the extreme conditions.

Forecasters warned that the heat is likely to persist for several days, with the possibility of further record-breaking temperatures.  The Met Office has issued amber extreme heat warnings for large parts of England and Wales, indicating a high likelihood of impacts on health, infrastructure, and the environment.  Concerns are also rising about the strain on emergency services and the potential for wildfires.  Residents are being advised to limit travel and to be extra vigilant, particularly during the hottest parts of the day.  The unusual warmth for June has also had an impact on wildlife, with reports of animals struggling to find water sources.  This record-breaking heat serves as a stark reminder of the escalating impacts of climate change.
