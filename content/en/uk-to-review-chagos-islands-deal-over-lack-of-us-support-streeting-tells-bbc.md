The United Kingdom is set to re-examine its agreement over the Chagos Islands, a move prompted by recent indications of waning support from key allies. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting confirmed the review in a BBC interview, revealing that the deal was described as "terrible" by former US President Donald Trump during a conversation with Andy Burnham, who was then the Mayor of Greater Manchester. This public acknowledgement of a review signals a significant departure from the UK's previous unwavering stance on the territorial dispute.

The Chagos Archipelago, which the UK administers but Mauritius claims sovereignty over, has been a point of contention for decades. The agreement in question likely relates to the long-term lease of a key US military base on Diego Garcia, the largest island in the archipelago. The base has been crucial for US military operations in the Indian Ocean and beyond. However, the complex geopolitical landscape, coupled with shifting political tides in the United States, appears to have prompted a reassessment by the British government.

Streeting's confirmation suggests that the UK is sensitive to the implications of diminished US backing. The review will likely scrutinise the legal, strategic, and diplomatic ramifications of the existing deal. It also raises questions about the UK's ability to maintain its position on the islands without robust international endorsement. The outcome of this review could have far-reaching consequences for UK-Mauritius relations and the broader geopolitical dynamics in the Indian Ocean region. Further details on the scope and timeline of the review are expected to be released in the coming weeks.
