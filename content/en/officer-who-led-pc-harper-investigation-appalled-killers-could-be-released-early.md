Former Detective Superintendent Stuart Blaik, the lead investigator in the tragic case of PC Andrew Harper, has publicly voiced his "appalled" feelings regarding the potential early release of the men convicted of his murder. PC Harper was killed in August 2019 while responding to a burglary call in Berkshire. He was dragged to his death by a getaway car. 

Four men were convicted in connection with his death. Henry Long, the driver of the car, pleaded guilty to manslaughter and was sentenced to 16 years. His accomplices, Albert Bowers and Thomas Huggins, were convicted of murder and received life sentences with a minimum term of 13 years. A fourth man, Jessie Smith, was convicted of murder and sentenced to 16 years. 

Mr. Blaik, who led the extensive investigation that culminated in the convictions, has now broken his silence. He stated that the thought of these individuals being released from prison after serving only a portion of their sentences is "difficult to comprehend" and deeply upsetting. He highlighted the immense trauma experienced by PC Harper's family and the impact of his death on the wider police community.

"To think that the people responsible for taking the life of a brave police officer… could be back on the streets so soon is frankly appalling," Mr. Blaik stated. He emphasized the thoroughness of the investigation and the justice he believed was served at trial. The prospect of early release, which can occur under certain legal provisions after a portion of a sentence has been served, has understandably reignited the pain and anger felt by those close to PC Harper and his colleagues.
