Prime Minister Rishi Sunak stated today that he is 'confident' that measures can be put in place to halt the early release of PC Andrew Harper's killers. The Prime Minister's comments come in the wake of public and political outcry after it emerged that Henry Long, Albert McCarthy, and Jessie Cole, who were convicted of manslaughter in the death of the heroic PC Harper in 2019, could be eligible for parole. PC Harper was dragged to his death by a getaway car as he attempted to stop a quad bike theft. The three men were sentenced to 16 and 18 years respectively. The Parole Board has been reviewing their cases, leading to fears that they could be released years before the end of their sentences.

In response to these concerns, the Prime Minister announced that the Justice Secretary, Alex Chalk, will present a comprehensive plan in September. This plan is reportedly aimed at preventing further early releases of offenders convicted of the most serious crimes. While the specifics of the plan are yet to be revealed, it is understood to focus on tightening the rules and processes surrounding parole decisions for individuals whose crimes have caused significant public outrage. Mr. Sunak emphasized the government's commitment to justice for victims and to ensuring that the sentences handed down by the courts are served. He assured the public that all necessary steps would be taken to address the current situation and to prevent similar occurrences in the future, reinforcing the seriousness with which the government views such cases.
