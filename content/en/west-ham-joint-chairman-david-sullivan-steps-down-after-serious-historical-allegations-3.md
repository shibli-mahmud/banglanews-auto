David Sullivan, a prominent figure in West Ham United's ownership for over a decade, has resigned from his positions as joint chairman and director of the club. The decision was announced with immediate effect after Sullivan was informed about the imminent publication of "serious historic allegations." While the exact nature of these allegations has not yet been disclosed, their severity has prompted this swift departure from the club's leadership.

Sullivan, along with his co-owner David Gold, acquired a controlling stake in West Ham in 2010. During their tenure, the club moved from its historic Boleyn Ground to the London Stadium and has experienced periods of both on-field success and fan discontent. Sullivan has been a consistent presence in the club's day-to-day operations and strategic decision-making.

The club has stated that it will not be making any further comment at this stage, citing the sensitive nature of the allegations. The timing of Sullivan's resignation is significant, coming at a crucial point in the football calendar. The implications for the club's future leadership and ownership structure remain uncertain. Fans and stakeholders will be keenly awaiting further details surrounding the allegations and the subsequent steps the club will take to address the situation.
