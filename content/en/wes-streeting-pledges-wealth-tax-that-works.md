Wes Streeting, a prominent figure in the Labour Party and a candidate for its leadership, has outlined a bold new policy proposal centered around reforming capital gains tax (CGT). Speaking to a group of supporters and press, Streeting articulated his vision for a 'wealth tax that works,' suggesting that the current system of taxing capital gains is inefficient and fails to capture the true wealth generated by asset appreciation.

His proposal aims to significantly alter how capital gains are taxed, potentially by aligning CGT rates more closely with income tax rates or by introducing a broader base for taxation. Streeting argued that the current CGT regime disproportionately benefits those who derive their income from investments rather than from wages, exacerbating existing wealth disparities. He believes that a more robust system would generate much-needed revenue for public services and create a fairer economic landscape.

This policy initiative is a key plank in Streeting's leadership campaign, signaling his intent to tackle economic inequality head-on. He emphasized that the current approach to taxing wealth is not only unfair but also economically suboptimal. By reforming CGT, Streeting aims to send a clear message that Labour is serious about redistributing wealth and ensuring that the wealthiest individuals contribute their fair share. The specifics of the proposed reforms are expected to be further detailed in the coming weeks, but the overarching theme is a commitment to a more progressive and effective taxation of capital gains.
