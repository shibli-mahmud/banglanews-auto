A scathing report by the Public Sector Assets Commission (PSAC), a non-governmental watchdog overseeing public and Crown property usage, has brought to light Prince Andrew's questionable handling of several cottages situated on the grounds of the Royal Lodge in Windsor Great Park.  The PSAC's investigation, which spanned over eighteen months, discovered that at least three of these cottages, officially designated for use by royal staff or as ancillary accommodation, were being sub-let by the Duke of York.  While the specifics of the sub-letting agreements remain confidential, sources close to the inquiry suggest these arrangements generated significant private income for Prince Andrew, despite the properties being maintained through public funds and Crown Estate revenues. The report highlights a lack of clear oversight and accountability concerning the management of these royal residences, raising concerns about potential conflicts of interest and the appropriateness of using Crown properties for personal financial gain.  The PSAC has called for a comprehensive review of all royal property portfolios to ensure transparency and prevent similar instances of alleged misuse.  Representatives for Prince Andrew have stated that all arrangements were made in accordance with established protocols, but the watchdog's findings are likely to intensify scrutiny on the Duke's financial dealings and his stewardship of royal assets. The report is expected to be debated in Parliament next month, with opposition parties already signalling their intention to demand further investigation.
