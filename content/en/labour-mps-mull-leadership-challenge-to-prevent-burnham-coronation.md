Speculation is mounting within the Labour Party over the potential for a leadership challenge, should Sir Keir Starmer decide to step down. Several senior MPs are reportedly considering throwing their hats into the ring, not necessarily out of a desire to win, but to ensure that any successor's policy agenda is thoroughly debated and tested. The primary concern appears to be preventing what some perceive as an impending 'coronation' of Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham.

Sources within the party suggest that a number of influential backbenchers and former frontbenchers are actively discussing the possibility of launching bids. Their motivation is reportedly rooted in a desire to foster a more democratic and transparent leadership selection process. They believe that a contested race, even if the eventual winner is not one of the challengers, would be beneficial for the party, allowing for a wider range of ideas and policy proposals to be aired and scrutinised by the membership. This, they argue, is crucial for the party's renewal and its ability to effectively challenge the current government.

While Andy Burnham has not formally declared his intentions, his name is frequently mentioned as a frontrunner in any hypothetical leadership contest. His strong performance as Mayor of Greater Manchester has garnered him significant support and a public profile that many believe positions him well for a national leadership role. However, the rumoured intentions of other senior figures signal a potential resistance to an uncontested ascension, highlighting a desire for a more vigorous internal debate on the future direction of the Labour Party. The coming weeks are expected to provide more clarity on whether these nascent plans will materialise into formal challenges.
