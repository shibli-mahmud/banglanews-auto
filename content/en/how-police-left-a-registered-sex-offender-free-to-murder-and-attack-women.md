The Metropolitan Police has acknowledged critical errors in its handling of registered sex offender Simon Levy, admitting that these failures directly contributed to him being free to murder and attack women. A senior officer has conceded that the second victim, Sheryl Wilkins, could potentially have been saved if the police had acted more effectively on intelligence and concerns raised about Levy.  The admission follows an extensive internal review that has uncovered a series of missed opportunities and systemic weaknesses in how the force managed Levy, despite his history of serious sexual offenses.

Levy was already a registered sex offender when he committed the murders. The review highlighted issues with information sharing, risk assessment, and proactive intervention, suggesting that a more robust and coordinated approach might have prevented the tragic loss of life.  The force is now facing intense scrutiny over its safeguarding procedures and the adequacy of its resources dedicated to monitoring and managing individuals deemed a high risk to the public.  This case is expected to trigger a broader re-evaluation of police practices concerning sex offenders and the procedures in place to protect potential victims. The findings underscore a deep-seated need for improved accountability and operational effectiveness within the Metropolitan Police to ensure such devastating failures are not repeated.
