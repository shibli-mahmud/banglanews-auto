In a move hailed as a significant victory for justice, Prime Minister Rishi Sunak has announced a sweeping reform of the early release scheme in England and Wales. The change, directly influenced by the brutal killing of West Mercia Police Constable Andrew Harper in 2019, will now explicitly exclude individuals convicted of manslaughter from being considered for early release from prison.  Previously, some offenders sentenced for manslaughter could be eligible for release under the Sentence Act, a loophole that caused immense pain to PC Harper's family and the wider policing community.

Andy Burnham, speaking on BBC Breakfast, confirmed the government's intention to amend the legislation. "Anyone convicted of manslaughter will be excluded from the early release scheme in the Sentencing Act," Burnham stated. This assurance comes after years of campaigning by PC Harper's widow, Lissie Harper, and various police advocacy groups who argued that the existing system did not adequately reflect the severity of crimes like the one that led to the young officer's death.

The decision signifies a strong commitment from the government to listen to the concerns of victims' families and to ensure that sentences for serious offences are served more fully. The Police Federation has expressed its gratitude, emphasizing that this change will provide greater reassurance to officers on the front line, knowing that those who inflict the ultimate harm will face the full extent of their sentences. The revised policy aims to restore public confidence in the justice system and ensure that the pain of victims' families is more closely considered in sentencing and release decisions.
