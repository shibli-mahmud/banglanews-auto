Spain has launched a sharp critique against what it terms a 'selfish' and uncooperative response from some European Union countries regarding the recent influx of migrants to its North African enclave of Ceuta. The Spanish government expressed dismay at the perceived lack of solidarity, particularly following Italy's decision to temporarily suspend the Schengen area agreement in response to the unfolding events. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni had described the scenes witnessed in Ceuta as 'shocking,' a sentiment that, while understandable to some, has been met with frustration by Spanish officials who believe it highlights a broader European failure to manage migration effectively and humanely.

Ceuta, a Spanish city bordering Morocco, has been experiencing a significant surge in arrivals as thousands of migrants, many from sub-Saharan Africa, managed to cross into the territory. This unprecedented situation has strained resources and brought the humanitarian crisis to the forefront. Spain's Foreign Minister, José Manuel Albares, has emphasized the need for a united European approach, arguing that unilateral actions like Schengen suspensions are counterproductive and do not address the root causes of migration or the shared responsibility of all member states. He called for greater cooperation in managing external borders and supporting countries on the front lines of migration flows. The Spanish government maintains that the issue requires a coordinated EU strategy, rather than individual nations prioritizing their immediate concerns by closing borders or withdrawing from cooperative agreements. The ongoing diplomatic friction underscores the deep divisions within the EU on how to best handle migration challenges and the ethical considerations involved.
