Peter Murrell, once a pivotal figure within the SNP, appeared in court today where he entered a guilty plea to charges of embezzlement. Prosecutors allege that Murrell misappropriated around £400,000 of party funds over a period of time, a sum that has sent shockwaves through Scotland's political establishment. The investigation, which has been ongoing for several years, has cast a shadow over the SNP, a party that has dominated Scottish politics for over a decade.

Murrell's resignation as chief executive in February 2023 followed an investigation by Police Scotland into the party's finances. His arrest and subsequent charges have been a closely watched affair, particularly given his close relationship with Nicola Sturgeon, who led the SNP and the Scottish Government for nine years before her own surprise resignation in the same month.

The embezzlement charges reportedly centre on funds intended for campaigning and party operations. The scale of the alleged misappropriation has led to widespread concern among party members and the wider public about the transparency and integrity of the SNP's financial management.  Legal proceedings are expected to continue, with further court appearances anticipated to determine the full extent of the alleged crimes and to consider sentencing. The SNP has stated its full cooperation with the authorities throughout the investigation.
