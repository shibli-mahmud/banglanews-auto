A clandestine operation with alleged Russian state backing has been linked to a series of arson attacks across the United Kingdom, including a fire at the office of Prime Minister Rishi Sunak's constituency in Northallerton. The BBC's Panorama program, working with investigative partners, has obtained evidence pointing to Russian intelligence services directing individuals to execute these acts of vandalism. 

The investigation details how the plot aimed not only to create fear and disrupt communities but also to exacerbate societal divisions. Central to this strategy was the creation and amplification of fictitious far-right and Muslim extremist groups on social media platforms. These fabricated entities were allegedly used to spread inflammatory content, inciting hatred and distrust between different communities. The intention, according to the findings, was to portray the UK as a nation deeply fractured and susceptible to external manipulation.

Several individuals have been arrested in connection with these incidents, with at least three reportedly having connections to Russia. One suspect, identified as Dmitry Petrov, is alleged to have been in direct contact with Russian intelligence officers who provided him with instructions and funds to carry out the attacks. The evidence, including intercepted communications and financial records, suggests a sophisticated and deliberate campaign to destabilize the UK. The UK government has yet to issue a formal statement on the BBC's findings, but the revelations are expected to significantly escalate diplomatic tensions between London and Moscow.
