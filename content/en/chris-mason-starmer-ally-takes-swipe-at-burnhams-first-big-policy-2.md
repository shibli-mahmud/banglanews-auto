A significant policy initiative championed by Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham has come under fire from within his own party, drawing criticism from a prominent ally of Labour leader Sir Keir Starmer. Darren Jones, who served as Starmer's former chief of staff and remains a close advisor, has publicly stated that Burnham's ambitious plan to cut Value Added Tax (VAT) for businesses in the region is currently unfunded.

Burnham has been vocal about his desire to implement a VAT cut as a measure to stimulate local economies and support struggling businesses. The proposal, which has been a cornerstone of his recent policy pronouncements, aims to empower businesses by reducing their overhead costs and encouraging investment. However, Jones's intervention raises serious questions about the practicalities and financial backing of such a significant fiscal measure.

Speaking to reporters, Jones emphasized the importance of fiscal responsibility and ensuring that any new policy is underpinned by robust financial planning. He suggested that while the intent behind Burnham's VAT cut might be commendable, the current proposals lack a clear funding mechanism, potentially leaving a significant fiscal gap. This criticism from a figure so closely aligned with the party's top leadership underscores a broader internal debate on economic policy and how to best present Labour's fiscal platform to the electorate.

While Burnham's office has yet to issue a detailed response, the criticism from Jones is likely to spark further discussion and scrutiny of the feasibility of his policy. It also highlights the delicate balance Labour seeks to strike between offering bold, popular policies and demonstrating a credible commitment to sound public finances, especially as the party gears up for future elections.
