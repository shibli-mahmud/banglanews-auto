In a candid conversation, Nicola Sturgeon addressed the difficult period following her resignation and the subsequent legal proceedings involving her husband. She expressed deep personal regret and sadness regarding the situation, emphasizing her commitment to cooperating fully with any investigations. When questioned about the accusations against Peter Murrell, Sturgeon maintained a tone of sorrow and disappointment, stating that the discoveries were "devastating" for her and for the party.

Kuenssberg pressed Sturgeon on the timing of her departure from the First Minister's office, probing whether she had any foreknowledge of the issues that were beginning to surface. Sturgeon firmly denied any prior knowledge of financial impropriety, asserting that her decision to step down was solely based on her belief that a new leader was needed to reinvigorate the party and its independence campaign.

The interview also delved into the broader impact of the scandal on the SNP's standing. Sturgeon acknowledged that trust within the party and with the public had been eroded. She stressed the importance of transparency and accountability moving forward, suggesting that the SNP needed to undertake a period of introspection and reform to regain its footing. While not directly commenting on the ongoing police investigation, Sturgeon reiterated her belief in the justice system and her hope for a swift resolution.

Reflecting on her time as First Minister, Sturgeon defended her record, highlighting achievements in areas like education and renewable energy. However, the shadow of the current investigations loomed large, with much of the discussion centering on the personal and political fallout. The interview provided a rare glimpse into Sturgeon's perspective during a deeply challenging chapter, offering her first public reflections since the legal developments unfolded.
