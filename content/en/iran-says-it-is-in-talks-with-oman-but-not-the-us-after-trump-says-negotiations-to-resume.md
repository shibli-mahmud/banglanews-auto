Iran has revealed that it is currently engaged in discussions with Oman, a neighboring Gulf state known for its neutral diplomatic stance. This announcement comes on the heels of pronouncements by former US President Donald Trump, who suggested that negotiations with Iran were on the verge of restarting. Tehran's foreign ministry, however, has explicitly denied any ongoing talks or impending discussions with the United States, framing the Omani engagement as the sole diplomatic avenue being pursued at this time. 

This direct contradiction has ignited a strong response from Donald Trump. The former president issued a scathing condemnation of Iran's leadership, accusing them of dishonesty and double-dealing. Trump, who previously pursued a policy of "maximum pressure" against Iran following the US withdrawal from the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) nuclear deal, expressed his frustration at what he perceived as a deceptive maneuver. His remarks suggest a continued skepticism towards the Iranian regime's intentions and a potential reluctance to re-engage in diplomatic processes if trust is perceived to be broken. 

The Omani mediation is a recurring theme in regional diplomacy, with Muscat often playing a crucial role in facilitating communication between adversaries. The nature and scope of these discussions with Oman remain undisclosed, but their confirmation by Iran serves as a clear signal that the Islamic Republic is not currently pursuing direct engagement with Washington, despite Trump's claims. This development underscores the complex and often opaque nature of Iran's foreign policy and its intricate relationship with both regional powers and former international interlocutors.
