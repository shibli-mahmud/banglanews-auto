US officials have confirmed to CBS News that an uncrewed vessel was instrumental in plucking two crew members from the water following the crash of a US Army Apache helicopter. The incident occurred on Monday in waters adjacent to the Strait of Hormuz, a vital chokepoint for global oil transit and a region of significant geopolitical tension. While details surrounding the cause of the crash remain under investigation, the swift and effective rescue operation, facilitated by the autonomous sea drone, has been praised. The US Navy routinely operates in the Persian Gulf and surrounding waters to ensure freedom of navigation and deter hostile actions. The involvement of a sea drone in this particular rescue underscores the Pentagon's ongoing investment in and deployment of advanced unmanned technologies across various branches of the armed forces. These systems are increasingly being utilized for surveillance, logistics, and, as demonstrated here, critical search and rescue missions. The successful retrieval of the crew members not only averted a potential tragedy but also showcased the growing capabilities of unmanned maritime systems in supporting traditional military operations in challenging environments.
