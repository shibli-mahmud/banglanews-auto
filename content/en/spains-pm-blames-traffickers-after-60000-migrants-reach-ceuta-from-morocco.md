The Spanish territory of Ceuta, nestled on the North African coast, has been engulfed by an unprecedented migration crisis, with an estimated 60,000 individuals, predominantly from Morocco, crossing its borders in a matter of days. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has unequivocally labeled the situation an "attack" on Spain's sovereignty and territorial integrity. He further stated that all migrants who entered the country illegally would be returned to Morocco, signaling a firm stance on border control.

The sheer volume of arrivals has overwhelmed local resources and facilities in Ceuta, a city of around 85,000 people. Images and reports have depicted scenes of chaos, with migrants, many of them young men and some families, swimming across shallow waters and scaling border fences. The rapid influx has sparked concerns about humanitarian conditions and the logistical challenges of managing such a large number of arrivals.

Spain has accused Morocco of facilitating or at least not preventing the crossings, a charge that Rabat has largely denied. The prime minister's strong rhetoric suggests a deepening diplomatic rift between the two nations, which have historically navigated complex relations regarding migration and territorial disputes. The Spanish government is working to manage the immediate crisis while also seeking to de-escalate tensions and find a long-term solution to stem such large-scale movements of people.
