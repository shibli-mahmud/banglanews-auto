President Donald Trump has declared that he has halted planned military strikes against Iran, emphasizing that the actions were not a forgone conclusion and would only be enacted if a diplomatic resolution was achieved with "rapid" progress. The announcement came after reports surfaced of imminent U.S. retaliation for Iran's downing of an American drone over the Strait of Hormuz.

In a series of tweets, Trump stated that "major new sanctions" would be imposed on Iran, signaling a shift towards economic pressure rather than immediate military engagement. He asserted that the decision to halt the strikes was made after he was "informed the Iranians were ready to make a deal," a claim not immediately corroborated by Iranian officials. Trump also suggested that "senior” Iranian officials had reached out, requesting a postponement of any U.S. action.

"We were cocked & loaded to strike last night to make a proportional counter, and when I heard that, 10 minutes prior I stopped it," Trump tweeted. He added that the "perimeters" of a deal had been agreed upon, but did not provide further details on the nature or terms of this alleged agreement. The move signals a complex diplomatic game at play, with the Trump administration seeking to de-escalate tensions while simultaneously exerting maximum pressure on Tehran. The international community remains watchful, as the situation in the Persian Gulf continues to be a critical flashpoint.
