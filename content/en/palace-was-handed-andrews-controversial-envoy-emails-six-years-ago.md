The Royal Household received a substantial trove of emails belonging to Prince Andrew in 2020, containing sensitive information related to his financial affairs and his controversial role as an envoy. These emails, reportedly numbering in the thousands, were handed over to the Palace six years ago, according to recent reports. The nature of their content, particularly concerning his past business dealings and association with figures like Jeffrey Epstein, has led to significant scrutiny. 

The disclosure of these emails at such a late stage, following the extensive public backlash and legal proceedings surrounding Prince Andrew's alleged involvement in sexual abuse scandals, has ignited fresh debate. Critics are questioning why the Palace did not act more decisively or transparently when these documents were first received. The timing of the handover in 2020, well before the full extent of the allegations became a dominant public narrative, suggests a potential for earlier intervention or investigation by the Royal Household. 

Sources close to the matter suggest that the emails provide a detailed, albeit potentially damaging, insight into Prince Andrew's activities during his tenure as a UK trade envoy. This period was marked by numerous overseas trips and business engagements, some of which have since come under a cloud of suspicion. The Royal Household has, in the past, maintained a policy of not commenting on private matters, but the sheer volume and sensitive nature of these emails make the current silence increasingly conspicuous. The revelation is likely to reignite calls for greater accountability and a clearer understanding of the Palace's internal procedures for handling such sensitive information.
