Provisional figures released by meteorologists confirm that yesterday's scorching temperatures surpassed the previous day's record, solidifying this May as the hottest on record for the UK. The mercury climbed to a staggering 35.1 degrees Celsius (95.2 degrees Fahrenheit) in Kew Gardens, a suburban district of London, late yesterday afternoon. This remarkable heatwave has blanketed much of the country, with numerous locations reporting temperatures well into the high 20s and low 30s Celsius.

Health authorities are urging the public to take precautions against the extreme heat. They are advising people to stay hydrated, seek shade during the hottest parts of the day, and check on vulnerable individuals, such as the elderly and those with pre-existing health conditions. The intense sunshine and high temperatures also pose a risk of sunburn and heatstroke.

Environmental agencies are monitoring the situation closely, particularly concerning the strain on natural resources. Water companies are reporting increased demand, and there are worries about the impact on crops and wildlife. Fire services have also issued warnings about the increased risk of wildfires due to the dry conditions. While many are enjoying the unseasonably warm weather, the sustained and extreme nature of this heatwave has raised questions about climate change and its potential to deliver more frequent and intense weather events. Further analysis is expected to determine the long-term implications of this unprecedented May heat.
