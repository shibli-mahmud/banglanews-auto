TEL AVIV – Israeli forces have seized a heavily fortified castle deep within Lebanese territory, a move described by Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu as a "decisive shift" in the protracted conflict with Hezbollah. The fortress, whose specific identity has not yet been officially disclosed by Israeli authorities, is reported to be of significant strategic importance, offering commanding views and defensive capabilities.  Netanyahu stated in a televised address that this operation marks a new phase in Israel's efforts to dismantle Hezbollah's military infrastructure and secure its northern border.

The expanded ground offensive, which has seen Israeli troops penetrate further into southern Lebanon than in previous skirmishes, has raised alarm bells across the international community. Several European nations, including France and Germany, have publicly voiced their disquiet over the escalating hostilities. These governments are calling for de-escalation and a renewed focus on diplomatic solutions to prevent a wider regional conflagration. Concerns are mounting regarding the humanitarian impact on civilians caught in the crossfire and the potential for further displacement.  UN officials are reportedly in urgent discussions to assess the situation and explore avenues for humanitarian aid and ceasefire negotiations. The seizure of the castle is expected to intensify calls for restraint from all parties involved, as the risk of a broader conflict continues to loom.
