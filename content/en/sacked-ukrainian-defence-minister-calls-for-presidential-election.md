Former Ukrainian Defence Minister Oleksii Reznikov, who was recently replaced in a high-profile cabinet reshuffle, has broken his silence to advocate for the staging of presidential elections in Ukraine. Speaking to journalists, Reznikov acknowledged the current reality of martial law, which has been in effect since the full-scale Russian invasion in February 2022. Under this legal framework, national elections are constitutionally prohibited from being held to ensure stability and focus on the war effort. However, Reznikov argued that postponing elections indefinitely could undermine democratic principles and the nation's long-term vision. He suggested that mechanisms could be explored to conduct elections safely, even under the challenging circumstances of war, potentially through secure voting methods for citizens both within Ukraine and abroad. His call is likely to ignite a fierce debate within Ukrainian political circles and among the public. While proponents might see it as a vital step towards democratic continuity and accountability, opponents will likely emphasize the paramount importance of national security and the logistical and ethical complexities of holding elections during active hostilities. The president's office and the Verkhovna Rada (parliament) have yet to officially respond to Reznikov's assertion, but his comments are poised to become a significant talking point in the ongoing national discourse.
