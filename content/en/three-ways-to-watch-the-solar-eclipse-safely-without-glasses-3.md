The upcoming solar eclipse is a highly anticipated astronomical event, but the rush to purchase certified eclipse glasses has left many unprepared. Fortunately, the American Astronomical Society and NASA recommend several indirect viewing techniques that do not require special eyewear. These methods allow you to observe the sun's silhouette projected onto another surface, effectively shielding your eyes from direct exposure.

One of the simplest and most effective methods is using a pinhole projector. This can be made with two pieces of stiff paper or cardboard. Punch a small, clean hole in the center of one piece. Holding the pierced paper above your shoulder, let sunlight pass through the hole onto the second piece of paper held below. The second piece acts as a screen, displaying a projected, smaller image of the eclipsed sun. The smaller the pinhole, the sharper the projection.

Another readily available option is to use a colander. Hold the colander at a height, allowing sunlight to filter through its many small holes. As the light passes through, it will create multiple, small projections of the sun on the ground or a piece of paper held beneath it. This method is particularly useful for viewing the eclipse with a group, as it allows multiple people to observe simultaneously.

For those who have access to a telescope or binoculars (that are NOT intended for direct solar viewing), you can also create an indirect viewing system. Attach a piece of white cardboard to the eyepiece end of the telescope or binoculars. Point the instrument towards the sun (without looking through the eyepiece!) and project the magnified image of the sun onto the cardboard screen. This offers a larger, more detailed view of the eclipse. Remember, under no circumstances should you look directly at the sun through any optical instrument without a certified solar filter specifically designed for that purpose. By employing these safe, indirect viewing techniques, everyone can partake in the awe-inspiring experience of a solar eclipse.
