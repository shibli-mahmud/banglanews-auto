A profound sense of grief has settled over the Royal Navy following the confirmation of the identities of the three servicemen lost in a devastating helicopter crash. Lieutenant Commander Chris Gayson, aged 42, Lieutenant Lily-Mae Fisher, 31, and Petty Officer Owen Green, 24, were all members of the crew of the advanced Wildcat helicopter that went down during a routine operation off the coast of Crete earlier this week.  The incident occurred while the helicopter was operating from HMS Glasgow, a state-of-the-art Type 26 frigate.

Details surrounding the cause of the crash remain under investigation, with authorities working diligently to piece together the events that led to this tragic loss. The Ministry of Defence has expressed its deepest condolences to the families and loved ones of the deceased, emphasizing the immense contribution each of them made to the Royal Navy. Lieutenant Commander Gayson was a highly respected officer with a distinguished career, while Lieutenant Fisher and Petty Officer Green were recognized for their dedication and promising futures within the service.

Colleagues have described the fallen trio as exemplary individuals, deeply committed to their service and highly valued members of their respective teams. The Royal Navy community is united in mourning, with flags flying at half-mast at naval bases across the country. Support services have been made available to the bereaved families and to the ship's company of HMS Glasgow, who are undoubtedly deeply affected by the loss of their shipmates. The investigation into the crash is expected to be thorough, with the aim of understanding the circumstances and preventing similar tragedies in the future.
