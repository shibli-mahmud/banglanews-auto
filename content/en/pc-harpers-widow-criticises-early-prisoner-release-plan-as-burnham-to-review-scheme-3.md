Lissie Harper has launched a scathing attack on the government's potential early release scheme for prisoners, describing it as "deplorable." Her strong condemnation comes in the wake of reports suggesting that two individuals convicted of the murder of her husband, PC Andrew Harper, might be considered for release significantly ahead of their full sentences. PC Harper tragically died in August 2019 after being dragged behind a car while attempting to apprehend thieves. 

The news has understandably reignited immense pain and anger for Lissie Harper, who has become a prominent advocate for victims' rights. She stated that the idea of those responsible for her husband's brutal death being freed early is "unacceptable" and sends a "terrible message" about the value placed on the lives of those who serve and protect the public. She further questioned the justice of such a system if perpetrators can avoid serving the full term for heinous crimes.

In response to the public outcry and the specific concerns raised by Lissie Harper, Justice Secretary Alex Chalk has announced an urgent review of the early release policies. The Ministry of Justice has stated that the review will examine the current processes to ensure they are robust and that public safety remains paramount. While the government insists that no decisions have been made regarding the early release of any specific high-profile offenders, the announcement of a review offers a glimmer of reassurance to victims' families like the Harpers, though their fundamental opposition to the policy remains unwavering.
