Passengers faced considerable disruption on Saturday as a band of severe thunderstorms moved across the south of England, leading to widespread flight delays at two of the UK's busiest airports. Heathrow and Gatwick reported a combined total of over 600 flights delayed, causing frustration and uncertainty for thousands of travellers. The volatile weather, characterised by heavy rain, lightning, and strong winds, necessitated the temporary grounding of aircraft and impacted air traffic control operations.  While some delays were a direct consequence of immediate storm activity, others stemmed from the knock-on effects as aircraft and crews were repositioned.  Aviation authorities and airport operators worked to mitigate the impact, but the sheer intensity of the weather system made swift resolution challenging.  Travellers were advised to check their flight status with their respective airlines before heading to the airport.  Airlines have apologised for the inconvenience and are working to resume normal operations as soon as weather conditions permit.  The Met Office had issued yellow weather warnings for large parts of southern England, indicating the potential for disruptive thunderstorms, hail, and flooding in affected areas.  The delays highlight the vulnerability of air travel to severe weather events, particularly during peak travel periods.
