Travel plans were thrown into disarray on Friday as a band of intense thunderstorms swept across southern England, significantly impacting operations at London Heathrow and Gatwick airports. More than 900 flights experienced substantial delays, with some flights being cancelled altogether. Airports confirmed that the inclement weather, characterized by heavy rain, lightning, and strong winds, led to temporary ground stops and reduced runway capacity as a safety precaution.

Heathrow Airport, the UK's largest international hub, reported a significant number of its scheduled flights were affected. Gatwick, another major gateway, also experienced considerable disruption. Airlines scrambled to reschedule services and communicate updates to affected passengers, leading to long queues at customer service desks and increased pressure on airport staff.

Passengers expressed frustration and disappointment as they faced uncertain wait times and the possibility of missed connections or onward travel plans. Many took to social media to share their experiences of the lengthy delays and the challenging weather conditions visible from the terminals. The Met Office had issued yellow weather warnings for thunderstorms across several regions, advising of potential flash flooding, power outages, and travel disruption.

While some flights managed to depart and arrive despite the conditions, the overall impact has been a significant disruption to air travel. Airports have advised passengers to check their flight status with their respective airlines before travelling and to allow extra time for their journey.
