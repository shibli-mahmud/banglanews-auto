U.S. Special Envoy for Iran, Vance, stated that the United States and Iran are 'very close' to reaching an agreement regarding the extension of an existing ceasefire, although he cautioned that 'we are not there yet.' This optimistic assessment from a senior U.S. diplomat follows earlier reports from BBC sources suggesting that a preliminary framework for the ceasefire extension had been agreed upon by officials. However, the finalization of this deal hinges on the explicit approval of both U.S. President Donald Trump and the leadership of Iran.

The potential agreement comes at a critical juncture, as regional tensions have been simmering. While details of the proposed extension remain scarce, sources close to the negotiations indicate that the discussions have been complex and have involved multiple sensitive points. The success of this initiative would represent a significant diplomatic achievement, offering a temporary reprieve from the ongoing hostilities and potentially opening avenues for broader dialogue.

However, the caveat that the deal is 'not there yet' underscores the inherent fragility of such negotiations. The final decision-making power rests with the highest echelons of both governments, and unforeseen political considerations or domestic pressures could still derail the process. International observers are closely monitoring the situation, hopeful for a de-escalation but aware of the long and arduous path to lasting peace. The coming days will be crucial in determining whether this 'very close' proximity translates into a concrete agreement or remains an unfulfilled aspiration.
