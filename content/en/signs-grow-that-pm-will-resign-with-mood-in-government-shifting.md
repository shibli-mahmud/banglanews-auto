A discernible change in the atmosphere within government corridors has fueled growing speculation that the Prime Minister is on the verge of resignation. While no official announcement has been made, a confluence of recent political developments and a palpable shift in the mood among key figures suggest a significant turning point. Sources close to the administration report a growing sense of unease and a pragmatic reassessment of the current political landscape.

This shift is not attributed to a single event, but rather a cumulative effect of ongoing pressures and challenges that appear to have eroded support and confidence in the Prime Minister's leadership. Conversations among MPs and ministers, as reported by the BBC's political correspondents Henry Zeffman and Nick Eardley, indicate a growing acceptance, if not anticipation, of a change at the top. The previously unwavering loyalty, or at least public pronouncements of it, seem to be giving way to a more candid acknowledgment of the difficult position the Prime Minister finds himself in.

Journalists covering Westminster have noted a distinct change in the tenor of discussions, moving from attempts to shore up support to more discreet inquiries about future leadership. The strategic calculus within the party appears to be adapting, with a focus shifting towards navigating a potential transition rather than reinforcing the status quo. This evolving sentiment, observed closely by political analysts, points towards a government grappling with the implications of a leadership change, signaling that the question is no longer *if* but *when* the Prime Minister might step down.
