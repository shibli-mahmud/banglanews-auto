Business Secretary Kemi Badenoch has fiercely criticised Labour leader Keir Starmer, asserting that he has left a 'mess' concerning defence spending for the party's shadow chancellor, Andy Burnham. The Conservative frontbencher claimed that Labour's defence investment plan is missing an estimated £5 billion, a deficit she argues calls into question the opposition's genuine commitment to bolstering the UK's national security capabilities. 

Speaking at a Conservative event, Badenoch stated, "Keir Starmer has left Andy Burnham with a £5 billion hole in Labour's defence plans. This isn't just a matter of policy; it's about competence and honesty regarding our national security." She further accused Labour of making grand pronouncements on defence without providing a credible financial roadmap, suggesting that their pledges are "unfunded and unrealistic."

The Business Secretary emphasised the government's dedication to increasing defence spending, pointing to the recent commitment to raise it to 2.5% of GDP. "We are putting our money where our mouth is," Badenoch declared, contrasting this with what she portrayed as Labour's vague and potentially detrimental approach. She implied that Starmer's leadership has resulted in a confused and underfunded approach to defence, leaving his colleagues with the unenviable task of rectifying the situation, should they ever come to power. The accusation adds another layer to the ongoing political sparring over economic policy and national security ahead of a potential general election.
