Former U.S. President Donald Trump has made a significant claim regarding Iran, stating that a deal with the nation is "largely negotiated." During a rally, Trump detailed that the potential agreement includes provisions for the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil transit. The former president did not provide specific details about the parties involved in these negotiations or the timeline for any potential agreement. His remarks come amid ongoing, albeit often indirect, communications between the U.S. and Iran concerning various regional and international issues.

Concurrently, Iranian officials have also signaled a degree of progress in their dialogue with international partners. However, they have been careful to clarify the scope of these discussions. Tehran's foreign ministry indicated that while a "framework" for certain talks is being developed, the paramount concern of Iran's nuclear program is not part of this initial stage. This suggests a deliberate strategy by Iran to separate different negotiation tracks, potentially prioritizing economic sanctions relief or other concessions before directly addressing its nuclear activities. The contrasting statements from Washington and Tehran highlight the complex and delicate nature of the diplomatic efforts surrounding Iran, with differing interpretations and priorities likely to shape future engagements.
