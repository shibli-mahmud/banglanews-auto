Bangladesh, a low-lying delta nation, stands at the forefront of the global climate crisis. Its susceptibility to rising sea levels, extreme weather events, and water scarcity necessitates a radical re-evaluation of its energy policy. The continued dependence on imported fossil fuels, particularly coal and natural gas, not only drains valuable foreign exchange reserves but also contributes significantly to greenhouse gas emissions, further intensifying the climate threat.

The economic rationale for embracing renewables is equally compelling. The declining costs of solar and wind power present an unprecedented opportunity for Bangladesh to achieve energy security and affordability. Investing in domestic renewable resources can create a plethora of green jobs, foster technological innovation, and reduce the volatility associated with fluctuating global energy prices. Furthermore, decentralised renewable energy solutions, like solar home systems, have already proven instrumental in electrifying rural areas, improving livelihoods, and boosting local economies.

The environmental benefits are profound. A large-scale transition to renewables would dramatically cut air pollution, which is a major public health concern in urban centers. It would also help Bangladesh meet its climate commitments under the Paris Agreement, showcasing its leadership on the global stage and potentially unlocking access to international climate finance. The urgency is clear: a strategic and accelerated pivot to renewable energy is no longer a matter of choice, but a critical pathway to securing a resilient, prosperous, and sustainable future for Bangladesh.
