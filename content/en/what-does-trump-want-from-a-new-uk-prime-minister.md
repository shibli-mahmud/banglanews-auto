Donald Trump's approach to international relations is often characterized by a pragmatic, and at times, self-interested outlook. During his presidency, the UK found itself navigating a complex relationship with the United States, often viewed through the lens of personal diplomacy rather than established geopolitical alliances. While there were initial indications that Donald Trump might find common ground with the then-Shadow Brexit Secretary, Keir Starmer, this perceived potential for a friendly understanding proved fleeting. The escalation of tensions and the outbreak of conflict in Iran dramatically reshaped the regional and global political landscape, forcing a recalibration of foreign policy stances. This shift directly impacted the nascent possibilities of a close working relationship between Trump and Starmer, highlighting how external crises can quickly derail personal or ideological alignments.

Looking ahead to a potential future UK prime minister, Trump's priorities are likely to remain centered on perceived benefits for the United States. Key areas would probably include securing favourable trade deals, advocating for policies that align with his "America First" agenda, and fostering a perception of strong Anglo-American cooperation, particularly in areas where it serves his strategic interests. The specific individual leading the UK will matter less than their willingness to align with Trump's objectives. Any prime minister seeking to cultivate a positive relationship with a potential future Trump administration would need to demonstrate a clear understanding of these priorities and a readiness to engage in negotiations that prioritize reciprocal advantages, however defined by the former president. The past has shown that personal rapport is secondary to perceived national interest in Trump's foreign policy calculus.
