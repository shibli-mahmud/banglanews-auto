A large wildfire in Scotland's Cairngorms National Park has been declared a 'major incident' by emergency services, prompting the full evacuation of the village of Nethy Bridge. The decision was made as a significant change in wind direction dramatically increased the risk of the blaze spreading rapidly. Fire crews have been battling the flames for several days, with the rugged terrain and dry conditions creating a challenging environment. The Scottish Fire and Rescue Service (SFRS) is coordinating efforts with other agencies, including Police Scotland and the Cairngorms National Park Authority. 

Helicopters have been deployed to assist in water bombing operations, attempting to suppress the fire from the air. However, the unpredictable nature of the wind poses a constant threat, making it difficult to contain the spread. The wildfire, which is affecting a vast area of moorland and forestry, has already consumed a significant portion of the landscape, raising concerns about wildlife habitats and natural beauty. Local authorities are working to provide temporary accommodation for the displaced residents of Nethy Bridge, ensuring their safety and well-being. The immediate priority remains to bring the wildfire under control and prevent further escalation, with the fluctuating weather conditions being a primary concern for all involved in the response. Further updates are expected as the situation develops.
