Andy Burnham, the influential Mayor of Greater Manchester, has articulated a bold vision for the future of British governance, proposing a significant decentralisation of power. His 'No 10 North' initiative, detailed in a recent announcement, is designed to fundamentally rebalance the nation's political and economic landscape, which he argues has become overly centralised around London. Burnham, a prominent figure in the Labour party and often discussed as a potential contender for its leadership, believes this move is crucial for driving 'good growth in every postcode' across Britain.

The core of the 'No 10 North' plan involves establishing a more powerful and visible presence for national government functions outside of Westminster. While specific details are still emerging, the concept suggests a relocation or significant expansion of key ministerial departments and civil service roles to northern England. This, Burnham argues, would not only bring decision-making closer to communities but also stimulate local economies through job creation and investment in the regions.

He contends that the current concentration of power in the capital stifles the potential of other parts of the country, leading to disparities in opportunity and economic prosperity. By decentralising, Burnham envisions a more equitable distribution of resources and a renewed sense of local agency. This approach, he suggests, is essential for tackling regional inequalities and fostering a truly 'United Kingdom' in practice, not just in name. The plan aims to empower local leaders and communities, giving them a greater say in shaping their own destinies and driving economic prosperity from the ground up.
