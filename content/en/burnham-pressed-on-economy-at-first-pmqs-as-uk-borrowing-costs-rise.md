Andy Burnham, the Mayor of Greater Manchester, found himself at the centre of a political storm during his inaugural appearance at Prime Minister's Questions, a rare platform for a regional leader. The focus of the debate quickly turned to the precarious state of the UK economy, particularly in light of escalating borrowing costs that are placing significant strain on public finances.  Burnham, who has been a vocal advocate for greater devolution and fiscal responsibility, was pressed by opposition MPs on his plans to address economic challenges within his region and contribute to national recovery.  In his defence, Burnham pointed to proactive measures he has already taken, emphasizing that his administration has implemented tax cuts designed to alleviate pressure on businesses and households.  Specifically, he cited the reduction of Value Added Tax (VAT) on energy bills, a move intended to ease the burden of rising utility costs for consumers. Furthermore, Burnham highlighted the cut to business rates for the hospitality sector, aiming to support an industry that has been particularly hard-hit in recent years. He argued that these initiatives demonstrated a commitment to fiscal prudence and targeted support, even as the broader economic landscape remains challenging. The exchange underscored the complex interplay between regional governance and national economic policy, with the spotlight firmly on how leaders like Burnham intend to navigate the current headwinds. The rising cost of government borrowing, a key indicator of market confidence, adds another layer of urgency to these discussions, with implications for future investment and public services.
