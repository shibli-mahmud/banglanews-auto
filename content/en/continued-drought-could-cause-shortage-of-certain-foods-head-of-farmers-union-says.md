Tom Bradshaw, President of the NFU, has voiced significant concerns that the continuing dry spell across the United Kingdom could lead to a critical shortage of certain staple foods. He emphasized the urgent need for government intervention to mitigate the risks posed by prolonged drought and other extreme weather events to the UK's food production capabilities.

Bradshaw highlighted that current weather patterns are placing immense pressure on agricultural land, impacting crop yields and livestock. He stated, "The prolonged dry weather is beginning to have a serious impact on our farms, and if it continues, we could see shortages of certain key food items on our shelves." He called for a comprehensive strategy that includes investment in water infrastructure, support for farming practices that enhance drought resilience, and the development of contingency plans to address potential supply chain disruptions.

The NFU president's warning comes as the UK experiences one of its driest periods in recent years, with reservoirs at low levels and agricultural areas suffering from lack of rainfall. Farmers are already reporting increased costs for irrigation and feed, further straining their businesses. Bradshaw stressed that food security is a national security issue and that proactive government action is essential to ensure the resilience of the British food system. He added that collaboration between the government, agricultural sector, and water management authorities is paramount to averting a crisis that could affect every household in the country.
