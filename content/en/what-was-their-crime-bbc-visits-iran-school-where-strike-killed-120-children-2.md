The scars are still visible, both on the building and in the eyes of those who survived. Nearly five months after an attack claimed the lives of 120 children at a school in Minab, Iran, the community is still grappling with immense loss. A BBC journalist, visiting the site, spoke with a teacher who witnessed the unfolding tragedy and a mother whose daughter was among the victims.

The teacher, her voice trembling, described the chaos and terror that ensued. "We heard the explosions, then screaming. It was… unimaginable. We tried to get the children to safety, but it was too late for so many," she recounted, her gaze fixed on the now-repaired but still haunting school walls. She spoke of the innocence lost, the bright futures extinguished in an instant. "What was their crime? They were just children, wanting to learn, wanting to play."

For parents, the pain is an all-consuming force. A mother, clutching a faded photograph of her smiling daughter, shared her unending sorrow. "Every day is a reminder," she whispered, tears welling up. "Her room is still as she left it. I can't bring myself to change anything. It feels like she's just gone for a moment, but I know she's not coming back." She questioned the rationale behind the attack, her voice laced with anguish and anger. "Why them? Why us? We just want justice, we want to understand why our children had to pay such a terrible price."

The visit underscores the deep emotional wounds that persist in Minab. The memory of the 120 children is a constant presence, a painful reminder of a tragedy that has irrevocably altered the lives of countless families and a community still searching for answers and solace.
