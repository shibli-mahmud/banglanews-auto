Fifa President Gianni Infantino has confirmed that the world football governing body will abandon its ambitious investment plan, which aimed to raise billions by selling stakes in its flagship tournaments. The proposal, first reported in March, sought to engage external investors in securing long-term financial stability for the organization. However, the plan quickly drew sharp criticism from national football federations, fan groups, and commercial partners who feared it would lead to a dilution of control over the sport's most lucrative events and potentially compromise their integrity.

In a statement released by Fifa, Infantino acknowledged that the "project has created divisions" and that the governing body "listened to the concerns raised." He emphasized that Fifa's priority is to unite the football community and that the "feedback received during consultations was valuable." The decision to withdraw the plan signals a significant retreat for Infantino's administration, which had been keen to explore new revenue streams to fund development programs and expand its global reach. While the exact details of the proposed investment structure were never fully disclosed, speculation suggested that minority stakes in future World Cup hosting rights and commercial deals were on the table. The outcry underscores the sensitivity surrounding the commercialization of major sporting events and the importance of maintaining a consensus among key stakeholders before embarking on such transformative financial strategies.
