As the UK experienced unprecedented high temperatures, the allure of cooling off in rivers, lakes, and the sea proved fatal for nine people. Charities dedicated to water safety have expressed deep concern, highlighting that many of these incidents could have been prevented with greater caution and awareness. The Royal Life Saving Society (RLSS UK) and the National Water Safety Forum (NWSF) are at the forefront of the campaign, urging the public to be acutely aware of the dangers associated with open water, especially during warm weather. 

Experts point to several contributing factors, including the temptation to swim in unsupervised or unfamiliar locations, underestimating the risks of cold water shock even on hot days, and the influence of alcohol. The sudden influx of people seeking respite from the heat in open water bodies has overwhelmed local rescue services in some areas. "We are deeply saddened by these preventable tragedies," stated an RLSS UK spokesperson. "Our message is clear: if you are going to be near water, please know the risks and take precautions. Don't swim alone, stay sober, and be aware of your surroundings."

The charities are advocating for increased signage at known danger spots and are working with local authorities to improve safety infrastructure. However, they stress that personal responsibility remains paramount. Families and individuals are being urged to educate themselves and their children about the dangers and to avoid taking unnecessary risks. The ongoing warm spell means the threat is still present, and the safety warning remains in effect until further notice.
