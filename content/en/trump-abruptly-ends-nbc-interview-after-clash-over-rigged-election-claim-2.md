The interview, conducted at the White House and aired as part of a primetime special, saw Trump facing pointed questions regarding his ongoing efforts to undermine confidence in the electoral process. Welker directly confronted the president with evidence and facts that contradicted his claims of a stolen election, citing numerous court cases and official reviews that found no evidence of significant fraud. Trump, visibly agitated, repeatedly attempted to pivot the conversation to other topics, including his past accomplishments and criticisms of the media.

As Welker continued to press for concrete evidence to support his allegations, the president's demeanor grew increasingly defensive. The exchange became particularly heated when Welker questioned his calls for his supporters to "protest" and his rhetoric surrounding election integrity. Trump accused Welker of being "dishonest" and "biased," echoing his frequent criticisms of mainstream media coverage. At one point, he declared the interview was over, refusing to answer further questions and abruptly leaving the set. The interview's abrupt conclusion highlighted the deep divisions and the continuing challenges surrounding the narrative of the 2020 election, with Trump remaining steadfast in his unsubstantiated claims despite overwhelming evidence to the contrary.
