Robert Bush, a funeral director, is facing sentencing after a court heard damning evidence of how dozens of families were subjected to unimaginable distress. The prosecution detailed a pattern of deeply concerning practices at his funeral home, where bodies were allegedly left unburied for extended periods, causing immense suffering and betraying the sacred trust placed in him by grieving relatives. 

The court was told that the funeral parlour was likened to a 'horror scene', painting a grim picture of the conditions under which the deceased were kept. Families who had entrusted Bush with the dignified care of their loved ones instead faced delays, lack of communication, and ultimately, the devastating discovery of their deceased relatives being unburied. The scale of the alleged breaches of trust was described as 'industrial', suggesting a systemic failure rather than isolated incidents.

During the sentencing hearing, legal representatives for the affected families presented their cases, emphasizing the profound emotional and psychological toll their discoveries have had. The details emerging from court proceedings highlight a gross dereliction of duty and a profound disrespect for the deceased and their grieving families. The legal process now aims to deliver justice for those who were so grievously wronged during a vulnerable time in their lives. The full extent of the impact on the community and the number of families affected is still being assessed as the sentencing continues.
