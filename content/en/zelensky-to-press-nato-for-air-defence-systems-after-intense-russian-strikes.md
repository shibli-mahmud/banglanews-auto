President Volodymyr Zelensky of Ukraine is preparing to make a compelling case to NATO leaders at an upcoming summit in Turkey, demanding a significant increase in air defense systems and interceptor missiles. The plea comes in the wake of a relentless barrage of Russian strikes that have targeted Ukrainian cities and critical infrastructure in recent weeks. These attacks, often involving a combination of drones and missiles, have underscored Ukraine's vulnerability and the urgent need for more robust aerial defenses to protect its population and vital assets.

Sources close to the Ukrainian government indicate that Zelensky intends to highlight the escalating threat posed by Russian aerial bombardments and the critical shortage of Western-supplied air defense munitions. He is expected to argue that current provisions are insufficient to counter the sheer volume and sophistication of the attacks. The Ukrainian president's agenda at the NATO meeting will reportedly focus on securing concrete commitments for additional Patriot missile systems and other advanced air defense platforms, alongside a substantial increase in the supply of interceptor missiles. He will likely emphasize that these systems are not merely defensive tools but essential components for safeguarding civilian lives and maintaining the nation's ability to function amidst ongoing hostilities. The summit presents a critical opportunity for Ukraine to rally international support and secure the necessary military aid to bolster its air sovereignty.
