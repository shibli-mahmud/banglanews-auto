Detectives from the Metropolitan Police's counter-terrorism command, who are already investigating the circumstances surrounding the death of former Member of Parliament Nigel Farage, have reportedly reopened an inquiry into an incident described as an 'attempted burglary' at his home. This development, understood by the BBC, suggests a broader scope to the ongoing investigation beyond the initial assumptions.

The incident, which is believed to have taken place sometime last year, has now come under renewed scrutiny by the specialized unit. While details surrounding the exact nature of the attempted burglary remain scarce, the fact that counter-terrorism officers are involved indicates a serious concern about the potential motives or implications of the event. It raises questions about whether there was any connection between this incident and subsequent events.

Sources close to the investigation have indicated that this reopened inquiry is not being treated in isolation, and its findings are being integrated into the wider probe into Mr. Farage's death. The complexity of the investigation has been underscored by this new focus, highlighting the meticulous approach being taken by the police to ensure all possible avenues are explored. The Metropolitan Police have declined to comment on specifics, citing the ongoing nature of the investigation. However, the involvement of the counter-terrorism command in both the death and the prior alleged burglary signals a high level of concern.
