In a deeply personal and often poignant interview with the BBC's Laura Kuenssberg, Nicola Sturgeon, Scotland's former First Minister, has broken her silence on the SNP embezzlement scandal, declaring she feels she is 'serving a sentence for a crime I didn't commit.' The emotional exchange, which saw Sturgeon visibly struggling to contain her tears at times, offered a rare glimpse into the immense personal pressure she is under.

Sturgeon was pressed on the ongoing police investigation into the disappearance of over £600,000 in donations meant for the SNP's independence campaign. While maintaining her steadfast denial of any wrongdoing, she admitted the situation had been 'incredibly difficult' for her, her family, and the party. She spoke of the 'intense scrutiny' and the 'painful experience' of being questioned by the police, emphasizing that she has cooperated fully with all inquiries.

The former leader articulated a sense of frustration and injustice, suggesting that the perception of guilt had already been established for many, regardless of the investigation's outcome. 'I haven't been charged with anything,' Sturgeon stated firmly, yet acknowledged the weight of public opinion and the sustained media attention. The interview underscored the significant personal cost of such investigations, even for those who maintain their innocence throughout the process. Sturgeon's words conveyed a profound sense of being caught in a protracted and emotionally draining ordeal, highlighting the devastating impact of such scandals on individuals and political parties alike.
