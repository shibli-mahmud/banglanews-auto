The Met Office has elevated its warning for England and Wales, signalling a period of potentially dangerous high temperatures. The amber alert, in effect from midnight until 11:59 PM on Thursday, advises that "very hot, thundery showers" are possible, but the main concern is widespread high temperatures.  This is the second amber warning of the week, highlighting the persistent nature of the heat.

Experts are urging the public to take precautions, including staying hydrated, avoiding strenuous activity during the hottest parts of the day, and checking on vulnerable individuals.  The sustained high temperatures pose a risk of heat exhaustion, heatstroke, and can exacerbate existing health conditions.  The prolonged period of heat is also expected to strain essential services and infrastructure, with potential for power outages and disruption to transport.

This latest heatwave follows a period of record-breaking temperatures earlier this month, which saw the mercury climb above 40C for the first time in the UK. The country's infrastructure, particularly its railway lines and road surfaces, has shown vulnerability to such extreme heat.  Officials are closely monitoring the situation and advising people to plan their journeys accordingly and to be mindful of the risks associated with prolonged exposure to the sun.

The forecast suggests that while the peak of the heat is expected on Thursday, the warm conditions are likely to persist into the weekend, albeit with a slight reduction in intensity in some regions.
