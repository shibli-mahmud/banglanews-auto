The head of the Police Federation of England and Wales is reportedly considering legal action to challenge the imminent early release of two of PC Andrew Harper's killers. Henry Long, 22, and Albert Bowers, 19, are among the inmates set to benefit from the government's new 'release on license' scheme, which is scheduled to commence in October. This controversial policy allows certain prisoners to be released from jail up to 18 months before the end of their sentence, provided they have been on a license since two-thirds of their custodial term began. PC Harper, 28, was tragically killed in August 2019 when he was dragged to his death by a getaway car as he attempted to apprehend a group of quad bike thieves in Berkshire. Long pleaded guilty to manslaughter, while Bowers was convicted of murder. The decision to potentially release them under the new scheme has sparked outrage and disbelief among the policing community and PC Harper's family. The Police Federation, representing rank-and-file officers, is understood to be examining all available legal options to contest this decision, emphasizing the profound impact such releases can have on public safety and the justice system. A spokesperson for the Ministry of Justice stated that decisions regarding license are made by the Parole Board, an independent body, and that public safety remains their top priority.
