FIFA President Gianni Infantino has strongly refuted accusations that UEFA, the European football governing body, made a severance payment to an individual with whom he allegedly had a relationship. The allegations, detailed in a recent Daily Telegraph investigation, state that this payment was made while Infantino was serving as UEFA's general secretary.  According to the report, the sum was intended to end the affair and silence the individual involved.

Infantino's office issued a statement denouncing the claims as "baseless and false." He asserted that the investigation was based on "unsubstantiated rumours and anonymous sources." The FIFA chief maintained that he had always conducted himself with integrity and professionalism throughout his career at UEFA. The report further suggests that the payment was authorized by senior UEFA officials at the time, raising questions about the organization's internal governance and oversight during Infantino's leadership.  While the specific amount of the alleged payment has not been publicly disclosed, the investigation implies it was significant.  UEFA has yet to issue an official statement regarding the matter. The allegations have cast a shadow over Infantino's leadership, drawing scrutiny to his past dealings within European football's administrative structures.  The FIFA president's strong denial indicates a potential legal or public relations battle to counter these serious claims.
