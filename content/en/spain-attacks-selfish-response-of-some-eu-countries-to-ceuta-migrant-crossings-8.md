Spain's government has launched a pointed attack on several fellow European Union countries, accusing them of a 'selfish' and inadequate response to the escalating migrant crisis in its territory of Ceuta. The Spanish authorities are reportedly dismayed by a perceived lack of solidarity and tangible support from some EU partners, even as the humanitarian situation on the ground intensified.  The criticism comes in the wake of Italy's decision to temporarily suspend the Schengen arrangement with Spain, a move that Spain views as counterproductive and indicative of a broader unwillingness to share the burden of managing irregular migration. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni had previously described the scenes unfolding in Ceuta, where thousands of migrants, including many minors, crossed into the Spanish enclave, as 'shocking'.  While acknowledging the gravity of the situation, Spain argues that such reactions do not contribute to a unified European approach to migration challenges. Spanish officials have emphasized the need for collective responsibility and burden-sharing within the EU, stressing that the pressures faced by frontline states like Spain require coordinated action and mutual assistance, rather than isolated measures or a retreat from established agreements. The government is reportedly seeking to rally greater EU support for border management and integration policies, advocating for a more cohesive and humane response to the complex issue of migration. The diplomatic friction highlights the ongoing divisions within the EU on how to effectively address migration flows and support member states bearing the brunt of these challenges.
