Lissie Harper has delivered a scathing critique of a reported government plan that could allow for the early release of certain prisoners, a move she described as 'deplorable.' Her impassioned response was triggered by news suggesting that two of the individuals convicted of the brutal murder of her husband, PC Andrew Harper in August 2019, might be eligible for early release.  The incident, which saw PC Harper killed while responding to a burglary, sent shockwaves across the nation and highlighted the dangers faced by frontline police officers.  Lissie Harper's public stance underscores the profound impact the crime has had on her and her family, and her opposition to any measure that could see those responsible for her husband's death freed prematurely.  In response to the growing concerns and the public outcry, Justice Secretary Robert Buckland has announced that he will be reviewing the prisoner release scheme in question.  He has committed to examining the criteria and processes involved in early release decisions to ensure public safety and justice are upheld.  The review is expected to address the specific circumstances that have led to these reports and to reassure the public and victims' families that the system is robust and fair.  The controversy has reignited debate about sentencing, parole, and the overall justice system's approach to serious offenders, particularly those who have committed heinous crimes against law enforcement.
