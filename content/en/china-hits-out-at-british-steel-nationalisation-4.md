The Chinese Ministry of Commerce issued a stern statement today, criticising the UK government's move to nationalise British Steel. The ministry argued that such state intervention in a private enterprise undermines the principles of fair competition and could lead to protectionist measures that disadvantage foreign companies. "We are seriously concerned about the UK government's decision to take British Steel into public hands," read the statement. "This action appears to deviate from market-oriented principles and could create an uneven playing field for international businesses operating in the steel sector." Beijing's reaction comes after the UK government announced its intention to nationalise the struggling steel giant, citing the need to safeguard "a vital national capability." The government has assured that this is a temporary measure aimed at stabilising the company and preserving jobs, with the ultimate goal of finding a sustainable, long-term solution. However, China views this as a protectionist stance that could impact global trade dynamics. The Chinese ministry further urged the UK to adhere to World Trade Organization (WTO) rules and ensure that its industrial policies do not lead to discriminatory practices against foreign investors or producers. The nationalisation of British Steel, a key employer in several regions, has been a complex issue, with the government seeking to prevent its collapse and protect its strategic importance. Yet, the strong reaction from China highlights the potential geopolitical ramifications of such significant industrial interventions.
