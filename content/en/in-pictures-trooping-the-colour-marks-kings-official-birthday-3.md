The annual Trooping the Colour ceremony, a cornerstone of the British summer calendar, took place on Saturday, celebrating His Majesty King Charles III's official birthday. Amidst a sea of Union Jack flags and enthusiastic cheers, the King and Queen, Camilla, embarked on a majestic procession down The Mall, heading towards Horseguards Parade. The King, dressed in his military uniform, rode a bay horse, while the Queen travelled in a carriage. The route was lined with thousands of spectators, eager to witness the pageantry and offer their felicitations to the monarch.

The Household Division, comprising the King's Troop Royal Horse Artillery and the First Battalion Welsh Guards, showcased their impeccable drill and horsemanship. The highlight of the day was the King's inspection of the troops, a ritual steeped in tradition that dates back centuries. The flypast by the Royal Air Force, a breathtaking aerial display featuring iconic aircraft, provided a spectacular finale to the ceremonial part of the event. Despite the King's ongoing cancer treatment, his presence at the helm of this significant national occasion underscored his resilience and commitment to his duties. The ceremony is not only a military tradition but also a vibrant symbol of national pride and continuity, offering a moment of collective celebration for the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth.
