Labour leader Sir Keir Starmer has publicly defended his stance on defence spending and his decision to remain in post, following the resignation of his Defence Secretary. Starmer described the recently made financial decisions as "hard-edged," implying they were difficult but essential choices made in the best interests of the party and the country.  This assertion comes just a day after the dramatic departure of his Defence Secretary, who reportedly quit over disagreements concerning the allocation of funds to the armed forces.  Starmer stressed that leadership in challenging times requires making tough calls, and he feels a profound "duty" to continue guiding the party through these complex issues. He suggested that the budgetary constraints are a reality that must be confronted pragmatically, and the decisions taken reflect a clear-eyed assessment of national security needs and available resources.  The Labour leader appears determined to present a united front and signal stability, despite the internal friction. He aims to reassure the public and his party that he is steadfast in his leadership and committed to navigating the fiscal realities of defence without compromising on core principles or future capabilities. The situation highlights the delicate balancing act faced by opposition parties in formulating credible defence policies while contending with limited resources and internal dissent.
