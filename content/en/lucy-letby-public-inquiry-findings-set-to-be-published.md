The highly anticipated findings of the independent public inquiry into the case of Lucy Letby, Britain's most prolific serial killer of children, are expected to be published imminently. Chaired by the Rt Hon Dame Nicola Davies, the Thirlwall Inquiry has spent months meticulously examining the events leading up to and following the atrocities committed by Letby at the Countess of Chester Hospital and the Liverpool Women's Hospital. The core focus of the investigation has been to understand how concerns raised by medical staff about Letby's actions were addressed – or indeed, if they were addressed adequately at all.  Crucially, the inquiry seeks to answer whether the lives of the seven babies Letby murdered, and the six she attempted to murder, could have been saved if swift and decisive action had been taken by the hospital management and relevant authorities.  The investigation has heard extensive evidence from healthcare professionals, hospital executives, and the families of the victims, painting a harrowing picture of systemic failures and a breakdown in communication and trust.  The publication of these findings is expected to offer profound insights into the systemic issues that allowed Letby to continue her killing spree for an extended period, and to propose crucial recommendations aimed at preventing such tragedies from ever occurring again.  The families of the victims have long called for accountability and for lessons to be learned from this horrific case, and the inquiry's report is seen as a vital step towards achieving that.
