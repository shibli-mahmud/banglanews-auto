A senior local politician has voiced strong criticism regarding the police's handling of a recent protest in Dover, alleging a significant delay in their response. Cllr Trevor Shonk, leader of Dover District Council, described the protest as "exceptionally well organised" and suggested that its effectiveness in disrupting local services was due to a lack of preparedness among authorities. Speaking to reporters, Cllr Shonk stated, "It's quite clear that this particular demonstration caught several agencies on the hoof. While we appreciate the right to protest, the response from law enforcement was notably slow, allowing the situation to escalate beyond what might have been necessary."

The exact nature of the protest has not been fully detailed, but it is understood to have significantly impacted traffic flow and local businesses in the port town. Cllr Shonk emphasized that the council had requested greater visibility and a more proactive approach from the police in anticipation of such events. "We had raised concerns beforehand about the potential for disruption and had asked for assurances that adequate resources would be deployed to manage any demonstrations swiftly and efficiently," he added. "The delay in their arrival and subsequent management of the situation has left us questioning the effectiveness of current contingency plans."  The Kent Police force has yet to issue a formal statement in response to Cllr Shonk's remarks, but sources within the force suggest they are reviewing the operational deployment during the incident. The council leader is now calling for a full inquiry into the policing of the event and for clearer protocols to be established to ensure a more robust response to future protests in the region.
