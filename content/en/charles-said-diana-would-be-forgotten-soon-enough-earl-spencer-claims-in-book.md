In a revelation set to stir further debate surrounding the Royal Family, Earl Spencer claims in his forthcoming book that King Charles III expressed a sentiment that his sister, Princess Diana, would be 'forgotten soon enough'. The memoir, details of which have been previewed by The Mail on Sunday, reportedly recounts an alleged conversation from the early days following Diana's tragic death in 1997.

According to Spencer's account, the remark was made in the context of the overwhelming public outpouring of grief that followed the Princess of Wales's demise. The Earl himself has often been a vocal figure in discussing the complexities of his family's relationship with the public and the media, particularly concerning his sister's legacy.

Buckingham Palace has issued a carefully worded response to the allegation, stating, "Grief can colour memory and it is understandable that Lord Spencer may recall events differently." This statement, while not directly refuting the claim, offers a counterpoint, suggesting that the passage of time and the intense emotions of the period may have influenced Spencer's recollection. The palace's intervention underscores the sensitive nature of the topic and the enduring public fascination with Princess Diana and her family.

Earl Spencer's book is expected to delve into various personal anecdotes and reflections on his life and his prominent family. This particular claim, however, is likely to attract the most significant attention, potentially reigniting discussions about the private thoughts and feelings of senior royals during a period of profound national mourning and upheaval. The memoir promises to offer a unique, albeit potentially contentious, perspective on a pivotal moment in modern royal history.
