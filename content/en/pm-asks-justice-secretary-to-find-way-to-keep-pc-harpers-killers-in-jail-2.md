Prime Minister Rishi Sunak has reportedly instructed Justice Secretary Alex Chalk to find a way to keep the killers of PC Andrew Harper behind bars, according to BBC sources. PC Harper was tragically killed in August 2019 while responding to a burglary in Berkshire. The individuals convicted of his manslaughter, Henry Long and Albertamate, were sentenced to 19 and 18 years respectively. However, their sentences were reduced on appeal, sparking outrage and a desire from the government to ensure they serve their full time.

The legal framework surrounding sentence reductions and early release schemes is complex, and the government is understood to be examining existing legislation and potentially exploring new measures to address this specific case. Andy Burnham, a key figure in advocating for justice for PC Harper, has expressed confidence that a solution can be found to exclude the killers from any early release programs. This sentiment suggests that legal experts are actively working on options to override or circumvent the current parole board decisions. The focus is on preventing the convicted individuals from benefiting from schemes that might allow for their premature freedom, ensuring they remain incarcerated for the duration deemed appropriate by the initial sentencing or through further legal intervention. The government's determination reflects a strong public and political will to honor the memory of PC Harper and deliver a sense of justice to his family and colleagues.
