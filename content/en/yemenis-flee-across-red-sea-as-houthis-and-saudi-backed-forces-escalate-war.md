The brutal civil war in Yemen has driven thousands to seek refuge, with an increasing number undertaking the perilous Red Sea crossing to reach Djibouti. For many, the journey is a last resort, a desperate gamble for survival as their homeland becomes increasingly uninhabitable due to relentless fighting between Houthi rebels and Saudi-backed government forces. The BBC's Frank Gardner reports on the human toll of this escalating conflict, speaking to individuals and families who have braved the treacherous waters.

These refugees often depart under the cover of darkness, crammed into overcrowded, unseaworthy vessels. The voyage itself is fraught with danger, from the risk of capsizing in rough seas to the constant threat of being intercepted by military patrols. Upon arrival in Djibouti, exhausted and often traumatized, they face the daunting prospect of starting their lives anew in a foreign land. Many have lost everything – homes, livelihoods, and loved ones – in the chaos of the war. Their stories are a stark reminder of the catastrophic humanitarian crisis unfolding in Yemen, a conflict that continues to devastate civilian populations and displace millions. The international community grapples with finding a sustainable peace, while ordinary Yemenis continue to pay the ultimate price.
