The United Kingdom experienced a historic heatwave on Wednesday, with the village of Coningsby in Lincolnshire officially recording a scorching 36.1°C (97°F) in the early afternoon. This unprecedented temperature has shattered the previous June record of 35.6°C, set in 1976. The heatwave has gripped much of southern England, with many areas exceeding 30°C for extended periods.

The Met Office issued amber extreme heat warnings, advising the public to be aware of the potential health risks, including heat exhaustion, dehydration, and sunstroke, particularly for vulnerable groups such as the elderly and young children. Emergency services are on alert, and healthcare providers are preparing for an increase in heat-related illnesses. The extreme temperatures also pose risks to infrastructure, with potential for buckling train tracks and disruptions to power supplies.

While the heat is expected to ease slightly in some areas by the end of the week, forecasters warn that above-average temperatures are likely to persist. This record-breaking heat is being linked by scientists to the broader trend of climate change, with experts emphasizing the increasing frequency and intensity of such extreme weather events. Authorities are urging residents to stay hydrated, seek shade where possible, and check on neighbours who may be more susceptible to the heat. Public transport services are advising passengers to carry water and allow extra time for their journeys due to potential delays caused by the heat.
