India's economic narrative in recent years has been one of impressive expansion, often lauded as a 'boom.' This growth, however, has been notably 'top-heavy,' with the services sector, particularly IT and financial services, playing a disproportionately large role. Coupled with a robust performance in certain manufacturing sub-sectors, this has led to headline GDP figures that mask underlying structural imbalances. The benefits of this growth have not been evenly distributed, leading to a widening chasm between the wealthy elite and a significant portion of the population still struggling with poverty and limited opportunities.

For Bangladesh, a nation striving to transition from a Least Developed Country (LDC) status to a developing one, India's experience offers invaluable lessons. Dhaka's economic strategy has historically relied heavily on the ready-made garment (RMG) industry, a sector that has been a powerful engine of export earnings and employment. However, mirroring India's situation, a continued over-dependence on a single dominant sector carries inherent risks. Diversification into new manufacturing industries, such as pharmaceuticals, light engineering, and electronics, is crucial to create a more resilient and broad-based economy.

Furthermore, India's struggle with equitable growth underscores the importance of inclusive development policies. As Bangladesh aims for higher economic growth, it must ensure that the dividends are shared across all segments of society. Investing in education, healthcare, and social safety nets, while simultaneously fostering an environment conducive to entrepreneurship and small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), will be vital to prevent the emergence of significant inequality. The goal should not just be a larger economy, but a more prosperous and equitable one for all its citizens. Ignoring these lessons from its larger neighbor could lead Bangladesh down a similar path of uneven progress.
