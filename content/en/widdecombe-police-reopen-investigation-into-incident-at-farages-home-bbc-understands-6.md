Westminster, London – In a significant development, the Metropolitan Police's counter-terrorism command has reportedly reopened an investigation into an incident at the home of prominent politician Nigel Farage. The BBC has learned that officers are now examining an alleged attempted burglary that took place at the former Member of Parliament's residence last year. While the details surrounding the initial incident remain scarce, the reopening of this inquiry by a specialized unit, typically focused on national security threats, has raised questions about the nature of the events.

Sources close to the investigation suggest that the re-examination is part of a broader review by the counter-terrorism unit, which was initially involved in looking into other matters related to Mr. Farage. It is understood that the attempted burglary, previously investigated by local police, is now being scrutinised by the specialist unit as potentially linked to wider concerns. The timing of this renewed focus, coming as police continue their work on other aspects of Mr. Farage's affairs, underscores the gravity with which the authorities are treating the matter. Further information is expected as the investigation progresses, with the Metropolitan Police yet to issue a formal statement on the specifics of the reopened inquiry.
