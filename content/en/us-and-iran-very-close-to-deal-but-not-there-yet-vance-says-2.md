The United States and Iran are reportedly on the cusp of a significant diplomatic breakthrough, with a senior US official stating they are "very close" to reaching a deal. While the exact nature of the proposed agreement remains under wraps, early indications suggest it pertains to the extension of a ceasefire.  This development follows intense negotiations, with US officials having previously informed the BBC that the "framework" of such an agreement had been reached.  However, they cautioned that this framework is still contingent on the final approval of both President Trump and Iran's supreme leadership.

Speaking on condition of anonymity, the officials described the progress as substantial, signaling a potential de-escalation of tensions between the two nations. The details of the proposed ceasefire extension are crucial, especially in light of previous periods of heightened friction.  The cautious optimism surrounding the potential deal is tempered by the acknowledgement that "not there yet" – a phrase emphasizing the remaining hurdles and the need for definitive endorsement from the highest levels of both governments.  The coming days will be critical in determining whether this fragile progress can be translated into a finalized agreement that could usher in a period of renewed calm.
