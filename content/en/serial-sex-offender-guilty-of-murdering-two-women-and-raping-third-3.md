A jury has convicted Simon Levy, 54, of Tottenham, of the murders of Sarah Davies and Jane Peterson, and the rape of another woman. The devastating outcome for the victims and their families is amplified by the revelation that Levy should never have been free to commit these heinous crimes.  An investigation following his arrest unearthed a catalogue of errors within the justice system. These included a failure by police to adequately investigate previous allegations of sexual assault against Levy, allowing him to evade capture and continued scrutiny.  Furthermore, court proceedings related to earlier offenses were reportedly mishandled, resulting in delays and a lack of appropriate safeguarding measures. Levy had a history of violence and sexual offenses, yet a complex web of administrative oversights and judicial missteps meant he was not subject to the necessary restrictions.  The Crown Prosecution Service has acknowledged that there were 'significant failings' in the handling of Levy's case prior to the murders.  Victims' support groups have voiced outrage, demanding a thorough independent inquiry into how such a dangerous individual could be left at large.  The case is expected to lead to widespread criticism of the Metropolitan Police and the courts, with calls for systemic reform to prevent future tragedies. Levy is due to be sentenced next month, with judges anticipating a lengthy custodial sentence given the severity of the crimes.
