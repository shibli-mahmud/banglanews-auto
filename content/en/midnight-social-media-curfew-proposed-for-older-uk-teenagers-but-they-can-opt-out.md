The UK government is reportedly considering a significant intervention into the digital lives of older teenagers, proposing a nationwide midnight curfew for social media use. This measure, aimed at promoting healthier digital habits and addressing concerns about excessive screen time, would apply to individuals in their late teens.  However, the proposal is not without its concessions, as it includes a provision for teenagers to 'opt out' of the curfew, meaning the restriction would not be universally enforced without individual consent or parental oversight, depending on the final legislation.

Beyond the timing of access, the government is also targeting the design of social media platforms themselves. A key focus is on disabling features widely considered 'addictive,' such as the automatic playback of videos and the endlessly scrolling feeds that encourage prolonged engagement. The intention is to make these platforms less compelling and potentially reduce the time teenagers spend passively consuming content.  Details on how these feature restrictions would be implemented and enforced are still under development.  This initiative reflects a growing global concern about the impact of social media on young people's mental health and well-being, with policymakers seeking to strike a balance between digital freedom and protection.  The proposal is expected to face further debate and consultation before any potential implementation.
