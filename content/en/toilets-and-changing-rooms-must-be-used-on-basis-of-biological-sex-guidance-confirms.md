Guidance published on Thursday by the government has reinforced the principle that single-sex spaces, including toilets and changing rooms, should be designated and used according to biological sex. This clarification comes in the wake of a landmark Supreme Court ruling last year which affirmed the legality of single-sex spaces. The guidance aims to provide clarity for service providers and individuals alike, emphasizing that while transgender individuals may be treated with dignity and respect, the preservation of sex-segregated facilities remains a legal priority.

The document outlines that service providers can legally restrict access to single-sex spaces based on biological sex. This includes considerations for privacy, dignity, and safety, particularly for women and girls. The guidance acknowledges the complexities surrounding gender identity but firmly anchors the operational principles of these facilities in biological reality. It suggests that in most circumstances, it is lawful to exclude trans people from single-sex spaces if it is a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim. Examples of such aims cited include preventing trauma, ensuring privacy, and maintaining safety. The government stated that this guidance is intended to assist organisations in understanding and applying the law correctly, ensuring consistency and adherence to legal requirements across the country.
