The Prime Minister, Andrew Burnham, wasted no time in setting the agenda for his new administration, marking his first cabinet meeting with a clear and emphatic directive to his ministers: address the escalating cost of living crisis.  The meeting, held earlier today, was dominated by discussions on how to provide tangible relief to households grappling with inflation and soaring energy prices.

Burnham's government, still in its nascent stages, has already signaled its intent with a decisive move: a reduction in Value Added Tax (VAT) on household electricity bills. This measure, announced just prior to the cabinet session, is expected to offer immediate, albeit modest, financial breathing room for millions of homes. The Prime Minister emphasized that this is just the first step in a broader strategy to combat economic hardship.

"Our paramount concern must be the financial well-being of our citizens," Burnham stated during the meeting. "We understand the immense pressure many families are under, and this government will act decisively and compassionately to ease that burden." He urged his cabinet colleagues to "think creatively and act swiftly" in developing further policies aimed at reducing the cost of essential goods and services, improving wage growth, and providing targeted support for vulnerable populations.

Sources within the cabinet suggest that discussions covered a range of potential interventions, including reviews of energy market regulations, measures to stimulate competition in food production, and enhanced social welfare provisions. The Prime Minister stressed the need for a coordinated approach, ensuring that all government departments work in synergy to deliver effective solutions. The coming weeks are anticipated to see further announcements as the new cabinet begins to flesh out its economic agenda.
