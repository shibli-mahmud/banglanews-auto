Prosecutors have detailed a shocking alleged assault on former Conservative MP Ann Widdecombe, claiming she was struck on the head with a hammer no less than 21 times. The incident, as presented by legal representatives, occurred at Widdecombe's home in Devon while she was reportedly eating lunch. The alleged perpetrator, 28-year-old Joshua Kerry, is accused of entering her residence and launching the brutal attack before making off with her wallet.

Details emerging from the court proceedings paint a grim picture of the events. It is understood that Kerry is facing multiple charges, including grievous bodily harm and robbery. The prosecution's account suggests a targeted attack, with the hammer being the weapon of choice for the repeated blows to the head. The motive is believed to be robbery, with the theft of Widdecombe's wallet being the primary objective following the assault.

Ann Widdecombe, a well-known figure in British politics for her outspoken views and distinctive style, served as the Member of Parliament for Maidstone from 1987 to 1997 and for Romford from 1997 to 2010. Since leaving Parliament, she has remained a prominent media personality, frequently appearing on television and radio programmes. The alleged attack has sent ripples of concern through her supporters and the wider public, highlighting the vulnerability of individuals even within their own homes.

Legal proceedings are ongoing, and Joshua Kerry remains in custody. The court is expected to hear further evidence as the case progresses. The gravity of the alleged crime, involving a prominent public figure and a weapon as brutal as a hammer, has underscored the seriousness with which the matter is being treated.
