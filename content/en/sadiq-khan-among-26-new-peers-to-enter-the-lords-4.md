The Mayor of London, Sadiq Khan, is among 26 individuals who have been appointed to the House of Lords, marking a significant shift in the upper chamber of the UK Parliament. Mr. Khan, a Labour politician who has served as Mayor since 2016, will now add his voice to the legislative debates from the Lords. His appointment comes as part of a regular list of peerages, which often includes individuals recognised for their contributions to public life.

Joining Mr. Khan in the prestigious chamber will be broadcaster and diversity advocate June Sarpong, and Christina McAnea, the former General Secretary of UNISON, one of the UK's largest trade unions. These appointments highlight a diverse range of expertise and experience entering the Lords. Sarpong has been a prominent figure in the media for over two decades, championing diversity and inclusion across various platforms. McAnea, as the head of UNISON, represented hundreds of thousands of public service workers and played a crucial role in negotiations and advocacy for their rights.

The full list of 26 new peers encompasses individuals from various sectors, including politics, the arts, business, and charity. The House of Lords, comprised of life peers, hereditary peers, and Church of England bishops, serves as a revising chamber, scrutinising legislation passed by the House of Commons and offering expert advice. The appointments are made by the monarch on the advice of the Prime Minister, with some also nominated by the House of Lords Appointments Commission. The inclusion of Mr. Khan and other public figures suggests a continued effort to bring fresh perspectives and a broad spectrum of knowledge to the parliamentary process.
