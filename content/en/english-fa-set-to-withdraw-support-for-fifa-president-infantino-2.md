The English Football Association is on the verge of withdrawing its support for Gianni Infantino's bid to secure another term as Fifa president. This significant development, if finalized, would mark a substantial blow to Infantino's re-election campaign and highlight increasing dissatisfaction among key national football federations with his leadership. Sources close to the FA suggest that the decision has been largely influenced by the FAW's recent announcement to similarly withdraw their backing.

The FAW's move was reportedly driven by concerns over Fifa's governance, transparency, and the impact of its decisions on the wider football ecosystem. While the specific grievances of the FA are yet to be officially detailed, it is understood that similar concerns regarding the direction of world football's governing body under Infantino's leadership have been raised. These criticisms often center on accusations of a lack of accountability, controversial commercial deals, and the perceived marginalization of smaller football nations' interests.

Gianni Infantino, who first assumed the Fifa presidency in 2016 following the corruption scandal that led to Sepp Blatter's downfall, has sought to project an image of reform and modernization. However, his tenure has also been marked by a series of controversies and criticism from various stakeholders. The potential withdrawal of support from the FA, a major influential member of Fifa, coupled with that of the FAW, could embolden other European federations to reconsider their positions, potentially leading to a more contentious and unpredictable election process for the Fifa presidency.
