In a candid and at times tearful interview with the BBC's Laura Kuenssberg, Nicola Sturgeon, the former First Minister of Scotland, articulated the profound personal toll the Scottish National Party's financial investigation has taken on her. Confronted with questions surrounding the alleged embezzlement of party funds, Sturgeon asserted her innocence, stating, 'I feel like I'm serving a sentence for a crime I didn't commit.' The emotional weight of the situation was palpable as she recounted the relentless scrutiny and the impact it has had on her personal and public life.  The investigation, which has led to the arrest and questioning of her husband, Peter Murrell, and other prominent SNP figures, has cast a long shadow over the party's reputation and Sturgeon's legacy.  Despite maintaining her position that she has acted with integrity and transparency throughout her tenure, the persistent allegations have clearly taken a significant emotional and psychological toll.  Kuenssberg pressed Sturgeon on specific aspects of the financial irregularities and the timeline of events, to which Sturgeon responded with a mixture of defensiveness and deep personal anguish.  The interview provided a rare glimpse into the personal struggles of a leader who has dominated Scottish politics for years, now facing unprecedented challenges to her and her party's integrity.
