Tom Bradshaw, president of the NFU, has articulated serious concerns regarding the escalating threat to the UK's food security posed by persistent drought conditions and increasingly erratic weather patterns. He stated that the prolonged dry spells, exacerbated by unseasonable heatwaves and lack of rainfall, are severely impacting crop yields across various agricultural sectors.  Bradshaw specifically highlighted the vulnerability of staple crops and produce that are critical for domestic consumption.  He emphasized that this is not a distant possibility but a looming reality that requires urgent government intervention. "We are already seeing the effects of extreme weather on our farms, and without proactive measures, the availability of certain foods on supermarket shelves could be significantly reduced," Bradshaw commented. He is calling on the government to invest in resilient farming practices, improve water management infrastructure, and provide greater support to farmers facing unprecedented environmental challenges. The NFU president stressed that a failure to act decisively could lead to price hikes for consumers and a greater reliance on imported food, undermining national self-sufficiency.  He is advocating for a comprehensive national strategy that prioritizes agricultural resilience and ensures a stable and affordable food supply for the British public in the face of a changing climate.
