Two people have sustained serious injuries after a passenger train derailed in East Sussex this afternoon. The incident occurred near the village of Northiam, with emergency services including ambulances, fire crews, and police dispatched to the location.  Passengers on board described a terrifying experience, with one man recounting, "It was pandemonium... I could feel the carriage rocking." He was traveling with his six-year-old daughter, who, thankfully, appeared unharmed but was understandably shaken. Initial reports suggest the train, operated by Southern Railway, was en route from London to Brighton when the derailment took place. The exact cause of the incident is currently unknown and is expected to be the subject of a thorough investigation by the Rail Accident Investigation Branch (RAIB).  The extent of the damage to the train and the track is still being assessed.  Commuters are being advised to expect significant disruption to rail services in the affected area, with alternative transport arrangements being put in place.  Paramedics attended to several passengers at the scene, with the two most seriously injured being taken to hospital.  The condition of the injured has not yet been officially released, but authorities have confirmed they are receiving appropriate medical care.  Further updates will be provided as more information becomes available from the emergency services and the ongoing investigation.
