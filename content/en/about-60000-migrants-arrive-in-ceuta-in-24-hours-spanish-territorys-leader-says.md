Ceuta, a Spanish city situated on the northern coast of Africa, found itself at the epicenter of a major humanitarian challenge as approximately 60,000 migrants arrived over a roughly 24-hour period, according to estimates from the city's president. This staggering number is more than 70% of Ceuta's total population of around 83,600 people, underscoring the sheer scale of the event. The vast majority of these arrivals are believed to have originated from sub-Saharan Africa, undertaking a perilous journey across the Strait of Gibraltar. Many were seen scrambling over the border fence or arriving in small boats, overwhelming the capacity of local security forces and reception centers. 

Local officials have characterized the situation as a "humanitarian crisis" and appealed for immediate assistance from both the national government and international organizations. Images and videos circulating on social media depicted scenes of chaos and desperation, with migrants, including many children, congregating on beaches and streets. The Spanish government has pledged to send additional security forces and resources to manage the situation, with a focus on identifying and processing the arrivals, and facilitating deportations for those not granted asylum. The unprecedented nature of this influx raises serious questions about border security, the effectiveness of existing migration policies, and the humanitarian conditions faced by those seeking refuge in Europe.
