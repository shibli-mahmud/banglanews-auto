In a candid and at times emotionally charged interview with the BBC's Laura Kuenssberg, Scotland's former First Minister Nicola Sturgeon expressed a deep sense of personal injustice regarding the ongoing investigation into the SNP's finances.  Sturgeon, who stepped down as leader earlier this year, stated that she feels she is 'serving a sentence for a crime I didn't commit.'  The remarks come as the Scottish government continues to face scrutiny over allegations of financial mismanagement and a lack of transparency within the governing party.  Kuenssberg pressed Sturgeon on the specific details of the investigation and the impact it has had on her and the party.  During the exchange, Sturgeon's voice wavered, and she appeared to struggle to control her emotions, at one point visibly close to tears.  She reiterated her belief in her own integrity and the integrity of the processes underway, while acknowledging the immense pressure and the public perception that the scandal has created.  The interview offered a rare glimpse into the personal toll of such high-profile political investigations and underscored the deep personal commitment Sturgeon felt to her role and the public service.  She emphasized that she cooperated fully with all inquiries and was confident that the truth would eventually emerge, despite the current difficulties.
