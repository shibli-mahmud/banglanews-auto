The Football Governance (FGR), established to ensure good governance across English football, has confirmed it is engaging with West Ham United regarding serious allegations against David Sullivan, the club's co-owner and chairman. While specific details of the accusations remain undisclosed by the regulator, reports suggest that several women have lodged complaints against Sullivan. These allegations reportedly involve the misuse of his considerable influence and power, and predatory sexual behaviour, with some alleged incidents dating back to when the accusers were in their late teens.

The FGR's involvement signals a significant escalation in the matter, moving it beyond potential internal club investigations into a formal regulatory inquiry. The regulator's mandate includes upholding standards of integrity and accountability within football organizations. West Ham United has yet to issue a detailed public statement concerning the FGR's contact, though club sources indicate they are cooperating with the inquiry. The gravity of the allegations, if proven, could have profound implications for both Sullivan personally and the operational standing of West Ham United within the Premier League and the wider footballing community. Further details are expected to emerge as the FGR's investigation progresses.
