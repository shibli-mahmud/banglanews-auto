The government's initiative to offer early release to a segment of the prison population, originally slated to commence in September, has been put on hold by Prime Minister John Burnham. The decision comes amidst growing concerns and calls for a more thorough examination of the implications and potential risks associated with the early release program.  In a statement released this morning, the Prime Minister's office confirmed that the planned September rollout would be deferred indefinitely.  "The safety and security of our communities remain our utmost priority," the statement read. "Therefore, we have decided to pause the early release plan pending a full and comprehensive review of all relevant factors. No prisoners will be released under this scheme until this review has been concluded and its findings thoroughly assessed."

The government had previously outlined the criteria for eligibility, focusing on individuals with good behaviour records and those nearing the end of their sentences. The stated aim was to alleviate overcrowding in correctional facilities and to facilitate a more efficient and humane reintegration process for some inmates. However, opposition parties and victim advocacy groups had voiced strong reservations, urging for greater transparency and a more robust risk assessment framework.  Details of the review process, including its scope, timeline, and the bodies involved, are expected to be announced in the coming weeks. This pause signals a significant shift in the government's approach, prioritizing a cautious and evidence-based strategy over a potentially swift implementation.
