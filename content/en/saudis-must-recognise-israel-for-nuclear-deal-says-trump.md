Donald Trump, speaking to Reuters, revealed that the highly anticipated nuclear cooperation deal with Saudi Arabia would only proceed if the Kingdom formally recognized the State of Israel. This bold assertion places a significant political condition on a potential energy partnership that has been a subject of intense discussion and negotiation between the US and Saudi Arabia. The Abraham Accords, brokered under the Trump administration, saw several Arab nations normalize ties with Israel. Trump emphasized that his administration had already laid the groundwork for such an agreement and that the current US administration was attempting to finalize it. He suggested that the nuclear deal's progress was being deliberately slowed or complicated. "They want to do this deal, and they want to do other things too," Trump told Reuters, referring to Saudi Arabia. "But we have to have Israel recognize that this is happening." The former president's statement underscores the deep intertwined nature of US foreign policy objectives, particularly concerning the Middle East, and highlights his administration's continued influence on regional diplomatic strategies. The potential implications of this condition for both Saudi-Israeli relations and global nuclear non-proliferation efforts are significant, suggesting that any advancement in Saudi nuclear capabilities could be inextricably linked to a broader regional peace framework.
