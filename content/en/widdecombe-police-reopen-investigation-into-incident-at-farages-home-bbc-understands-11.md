Metropolitan Police counter-terrorism officers have reportedly reopened an investigation into an incident at the home of former Brexit Party leader Nigel Farage. The BBC understands that police are examining an 'attempted burglary' that took place at his Kent residence last year. 

While the initial incident was not widely publicised, sources suggest that the reopened investigation is linked to broader concerns about the security of prominent political figures. The Metropolitan Police have declined to comment on specific operational details, stating only that they are "reviewing information in relation to an incident at an address in Kent last year." 

Nigel Farage himself has not yet commented on the renewed investigation. The news comes at a sensitive time, as political figures often face heightened security risks. The focus on an 'attempted burglary' suggests that while the initial response may have treated it as a routine crime, new information or context has prompted the counter-terrorism unit to re-evaluate the matter. 

Further details are expected to emerge as the investigation progresses. The public will be keen to understand the full scope of the inquiry and any potential implications for security protocols surrounding high-profile individuals in the UK.
