In a deeply personal and at times heart-wrenching interview with the BBC's Laura Kuenssberg, Scotland's former First Minister Nicola Sturgeon asserted that she feels unfairly targeted by the ongoing investigation into the Scottish National Party's finances. When pressed about the embezzlement scandal that has engulfed the party and led to the arrest of her husband, Peter Murrell, Sturgeon declared, "I am serving a sentence for a crime I didn't commit." The weight of the accusations and the prolonged scrutiny appeared to take a significant toll, as she was observed to be visibly moved, at times struggling to hold back tears.

Sturgeon maintained her innocence and expressed frustration with the protracted nature of the investigation. The inquiry, which has seen the SNP headquarters searched and key figures questioned, has cast a long shadow over the party's reputation and leadership. She spoke of the personal impact of the scandal, the constant media attention, and the feeling of being judged for matters she maintains are not her direct responsibility. The former leader's emotional response highlighted the immense pressure and personal cost associated with such high-profile political investigations. While refusing to be drawn into specific details of the ongoing police inquiry, Sturgeon emphasized her full cooperation with the authorities and her commitment to seeing the truth emerge. The interview provided a rare glimpse into the personal toll of political crisis, as Sturgeon presented herself as a figure caught in a painful and unjust situation.
