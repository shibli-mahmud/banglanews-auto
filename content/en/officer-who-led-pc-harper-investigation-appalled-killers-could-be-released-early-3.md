Former Detective Superintendent Stuart Blaik, who led the investigation into the death of Police Constable Andrew Harper, has publicly stated he is 'appalled' by the prospect of the convicted killers being released from prison early. PC Harper was tragically killed in August 2019 while responding to a burglary call in Berkshire. He was dragged behind a car for over a mile by a group of teenagers who had been attempting to steal quad bikes.

The investigation, led by Blaik, was extensive and complex, resulting in the conviction of four individuals. Henry Long was sentenced to 16 years for manslaughter, while Albert, Jessie, and Tom Boggs each received 13 years for manslaughter. All four were acquitted of murder.

Blaik, speaking for the first time since the case concluded, has expressed his deep frustration with the possibility of early release, a decision that rests with the Parole Board. He emphasized the immense pain and suffering endured by PC Harper's family, his widow Lissie Harper, and the wider police community. The sentence handed down, while significant, is now being questioned in terms of its deterrent effect and ultimate justice for a fallen officer.

"We brought them to justice for their horrific crime, and to think they might be back on the streets before the full term of their sentence is deeply troubling," Blaik reportedly stated. The former senior detective's intervention highlights the enduring impact of PC Harper's death and the strong feelings within law enforcement regarding the finality of justice. The Parole Board's decision will undoubtedly be closely scrutinised, with many hoping for a outcome that acknowledges the severity of the crime and the victim's sacrifice.
