Buckingham Palace has announced that King Charles III has voluntarily released details of his tax payments, marking a significant departure from royal tradition. For the financial year 2024-25, the monarch has paid £12.9 million in income tax and capital gains tax. This transparency initiative aims to provide greater insight into the Crown's financial contributions and address public interest surrounding royal finances.

The substantial sum places King Charles III among the top 100 highest taxpayers in the UK, a testament to the significant income generated from his private estates, most notably the Duchy of Cornwall. While the Sovereign Grant, funded by taxpayers, covers official duties and the upkeep of royal palaces, the King's private income is subject to taxation, a practice initiated by his mother, Queen Elizabeth II, in 1993. However, this is the first time a monarch has publicly declared the exact figures.

This disclosure comes at a time when scrutiny of public finances and the wealth of prominent individuals is heightened. By sharing these figures, the Palace hopes to foster goodwill and demonstrate the King's commitment to contributing financially to the nation's economy. The exact breakdown of the £12.9 million tax bill includes income from the Duchy of Cornwall, which is managed for the heir to the throne, and other private investments. While details of specific deductions or allowances are not provided, the overall payment signifies a substantial financial commitment to the UK exchequer.
