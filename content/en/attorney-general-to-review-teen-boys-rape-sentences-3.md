In a move that has resonated with victims' rights advocates and the wider public, the Attorney General's office confirmed today that it will be scrutinizing the sentences imposed on three teenage boys found guilty of raping two girls. The decision to pursue a review comes after widespread criticism of the court's decision to refrain from imposing custodial sentences, with many arguing that the outcomes failed to adequately reflect the severity of the crimes committed.

The initial court ruling, which spared the teenagers jail time, sparked immediate public outrage. Social media platforms were abuzz with calls for justice, and numerous online petitions gained rapid traction, demanding a re-evaluation of the sentences. Critics pointed to the devastating impact of sexual assault and argued that non-custodial sentences, particularly for a crime as serious as rape, send an unacceptable message about accountability and the value placed on victims' well-being.

While the specifics of the review are yet to be detailed, it is understood that the Attorney General's office will assess whether the original sentencing decision was appropriate and in line with legal precedents and public interest. This process will likely involve a thorough examination of the evidence presented during the trial, the legal arguments made, and the judge's reasoning. The outcome of this review could potentially lead to an appeal for harsher sentences. The Attorney General's office has stated its commitment to ensuring that justice is served and that sentencing decisions are proportionate to the gravity of the offenses, especially in cases involving sexual violence.
