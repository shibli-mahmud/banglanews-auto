In a series of tweets, President Trump revealed that after hearing from his generals that Iran's downing of a U.S. drone would result in 150 deaths, he "immediately called it off." He further elaborated that the decision was based on the fact that Iran and "other countries" had made requests for a delay in any potential U.S. military response.  Trump asserted that a "deal being made rapidly" was the condition for the cancellation. He did not specify which other countries had made these requests or provide details about the nature of the proposed deal.  However, he indicated that the "perimeters" of such an agreement had been reached. This sudden reversal comes after days of escalating tensions in the Persian Gulf, following Iran's downing of an American surveillance drone, which the U.S. maintains was flying in international airspace. Iran, however, insists the drone was over its territory. The U.S. military had reportedly been preparing for strikes, but Trump's announcement suggests a de-escalation, at least for now. The situation remains fluid, with international observers closely watching for further developments and the potential for diplomatic engagement to avert a wider conflict.
