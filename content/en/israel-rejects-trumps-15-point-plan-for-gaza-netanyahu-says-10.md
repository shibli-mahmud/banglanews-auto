In a significant development regarding the future of Gaza, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has unequivocally rejected a proposed 15-point peace plan attributed to former U.S. President Donald Trump. Speaking to reporters, Netanyahu articulated his government's firm stance, emphasizing that a military withdrawal from the Gaza Strip would not occur until the Palestinian militant group Hamas is 'genuinely' disarmed. This declaration signals a continued divergence between Israeli security objectives and potential international mediation efforts.

The specifics of Trump's alleged proposal remain undisclosed, but sources suggest it aimed to outline a pathway towards de-escalation and a potential post-conflict arrangement for Gaza. However, Israel's current leadership appears unwilling to consider any scenario that does not prioritize the complete dismantling of Hamas's military capabilities. Netanyahu's statement underscores the paramount importance Israel places on its security, particularly in light of the October 7th attacks orchestrated by Hamas.

The Prime Minister's comments are likely to be met with mixed reactions both domestically and internationally. While his hardline position on Hamas aligns with many within his coalition government, it may complicate diplomatic efforts seeking a resolution to the ongoing conflict. The international community has been keenly observing the situation, with various actors attempting to broker a ceasefire and facilitate humanitarian aid. Netanyahu's rejection, however, places the onus squarely on achieving Hamas's disarmament as a prerequisite for any significant Israeli military repositioning, a goal that has thus far proven elusive.
