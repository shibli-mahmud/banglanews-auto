The United Kingdom has been gripped by an exceptional heatwave, with provisional figures from the Met Office indicating that the hottest May day on record was broken for the second day in a row. On Wednesday, temperatures soared to an astonishing 35.1C (95.2F) in Kew Gardens, located in south-west London. This sweltering heat follows a record-setting Tuesday, when the previous highest May temperature of 32.9C was recorded in Chertsey, Surrey.

Weather experts attribute this extreme heat to a high-pressure system drawing in warm air from continental Europe. The prolonged period of unusually high temperatures has prompted widespread public health warnings, with advice to stay hydrated, avoid strenuous activity during the hottest parts of the day, and check on vulnerable individuals. Many regions across England, Wales, and parts of Scotland have experienced temperatures well into the high 20s and low 30s Celsius.

The Met Office has issued amber extreme heat warnings for several areas, signaling the potential for widespread impacts on infrastructure and daily life. While the heat has brought out sun-seekers to parks and beaches, it has also raised concerns about potential wildfires, strain on the NHS, and the welfare of animals. Forecasters are monitoring the situation closely, with predictions suggesting that while some cooler air may begin to influence the far north of the UK later in the week, the south is likely to remain significantly warm for some time to come. The country is bracing for the possibility of further records being challenged as the heatwave persists.
