The rollout of these specialist courts represents a substantial commitment to addressing the low conviction rates and the often traumatic experience victims endure within the current judicial system. The Ministry of Justice has confirmed that funding has been allocated to equip courtrooms with the necessary technology and to train staff to handle these sensitive cases with greater expertise and empathy.

Victims will no longer be required to physically attend court to give evidence, a common source of anxiety and re-traumatisation. Instead, they will have the option to participate remotely from a designated safe space, connected by a secure video link. This approach is expected to not only mitigate the psychological impact on survivors but also potentially increase the number of cases progressing through the courts, as some victims may be more willing to testify knowing they have this level of protection and distance.

Furthermore, the specialist courts are intended to create a more supportive environment, with a focus on reducing the number of times victims have to recount their experiences. The government hopes that by streamlining the process and providing these victim-centric measures, there will be a tangible improvement in how sexual offense cases are prosecuted, ultimately aiming to restore confidence in the justice system for those who have experienced such serious crimes.
