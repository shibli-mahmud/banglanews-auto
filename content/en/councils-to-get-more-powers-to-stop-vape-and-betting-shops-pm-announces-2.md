In a significant announcement aimed at revitalizing high streets, Prime Minister Andy Burnham has declared that local councils will be granted increased authority to control the opening of new vape and betting shops. The Prime Minister articulated his concerns during a press conference, stating that town centres have been "hollowed out" by decades of decline, a trend exacerbated by the unchecked growth of certain retail outlets.

Mr. Burnham's government believes that giving councils more teeth will allow them to protect the character and diversity of their local commercial areas. Currently, planning laws offer limited recourse for local authorities to refuse applications for these types of businesses, even when they are perceived as contributing to a decline in footfall for more traditional retailers or as negatively impacting community well-being. The proposed changes are expected to include powers to limit the concentration of such shops within specific zones, preventing 'cluster effects' that critics argue can disproportionately affect vulnerable populations.

The Prime Minister emphasized that the move is part of a broader strategy to foster more balanced and community-focused town centres. "We need to ensure our high streets are places where families want to spend time, where local businesses can thrive, and where the unique identity of each community is preserved," he stated. "Too often, the spread of these shops has undermined that vision."

While specific details of the legislative changes are yet to be released, the announcement has been met with cautious optimism by local government bodies, who have long campaigned for greater control over the changing face of their urban centres. The aim is to empower councils to make decisions that genuinely reflect the needs and desires of their residents, thereby fostering a more sustainable and appealing retail environment.
