Joshua Bonehill-Paine, who was previously announced as a Conservative candidate, has withdrawn his name from the ballot following scrutiny of his past affiliations with far-right and neo-Nazi movements. The decision comes amid significant public and media attention surrounding his suitability for public office. 

Sources close to the Conservative Party, including reportedly Business Secretary Kemi Badenoch, have confirmed that Bonehill-Paine will not be standing as a candidate. However, in a surprising turn of events, it is understood that he will now be taking on a role advising the party on how to effectively tackle extremism. This move has raised questions and concerns among political commentators and anti-fascist groups who point to Bonehill-Paine's history of promoting hate speech and extremist ideologies.

Bonehill-Paine's past involvement in groups such as the British National Party (BNP) and his documented participation in neo-Nazi rallies have been widely reported. Critics argue that engaging such an individual, even in an advisory capacity, sends the wrong message and could undermine the party's commitment to inclusivity and combating hate. The Conservative Party has yet to issue a formal statement detailing the specifics of Bonehill-Paine's new advisory role or the rationale behind this decision. The opposition parties are expected to heavily scrutinize this development as the election campaign intensifies.
