Andy Burnham, the Mayor of Greater Manchester, has articulated a firm stance on his potential interactions with US President Donald Trump, asserting his willingness to confront the President if deemed necessary for the benefit of Britain. Speaking in an exclusive interview with the BBC, Burnham described his first conversation with Trump as 'good,' indicating a pragmatic approach to international relations.

However, he underscored a core principle that would guide his actions: 'you have to defend your own national interest.' This statement suggests that while diplomatic channels and initial positive exchanges are valued, any perceived threat to the UK's sovereignty, economy, or citizens' well-being would trigger a more assertive response.

The Mayor's comments come at a time of significant global political flux, where leaders are increasingly expected to champion their nations' priorities on the international stage. Burnham's remarks signal a departure from a purely deferential approach, particularly when dealing with powerful global figures like the US President.

He did not elaborate on specific scenarios that might prompt such a confrontation, but the implication is clear: that economic deals, security arrangements, or any other bilateral matters would be scrutinized through the lens of British advantage. This principled stand positions Burnham as a leader prioritizing national well-being above all else, even in the face of potential international pressure or diplomatic complexities.
