Sir Mark Rowley, the head of the Metropolitan Police, has publicly acknowledged that the force made errors in its handling of the investigation into serial offender Simon Levy. Levy was recently convicted of two murders, but concerns have been raised that the police may have missed opportunities to intervene sooner. 

Critics, notably Labour MP Jess Phillips, have voiced strong opinions, suggesting that the Met Police "buried" the first murder attributed to Levy, allowing him to evade justice for a period and potentially commit further crimes. Phillips, speaking in Parliament, highlighted that information about Levy's initial violent act was available to the authorities. The implication is that a more robust or timely response could have prevented subsequent tragedies.

In response to these criticisms, Sir Mark Rowley stated that while mistakes were made, he stands by the integrity and efforts of the officers involved in the investigation. He emphasized that the force is committed to learning from these errors and improving its processes to prevent similar failures in the future. The Commissioner stressed that identifying and apprehending Levy was a complex operation, but the acknowledgement of shortcomings is a significant step. The Metropolitan Police have launched a full internal review to scrutinize every aspect of the investigation and identify systemic weaknesses. The findings of this review are expected to lead to policy changes and enhanced training for officers dealing with serious offenders and intelligence gathering. The case has sparked a broader debate about police accountability and the effectiveness of intelligence sharing within law enforcement agencies.
