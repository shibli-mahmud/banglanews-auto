Tom Bradshaw, the head of the NFU, has raised serious concerns that persistent dry weather could significantly impact the availability of certain foods across the United Kingdom.  In a direct appeal to the government, Mr. Bradshaw emphasized the critical need for proactive strategies to mitigate the effects of extreme weather events on domestic food production.

According to the NFU president, the current weather patterns are already placing immense pressure on agricultural land.  A continuation of these drought conditions would not only jeopardize crop yields but also affect livestock through a scarcity of suitable grazing land and water resources.  This, in turn, could lead to a reduction in the supply of staple foods and potentially drive up prices for consumers.

Mr. Bradshaw called for the government to collaborate with the agricultural sector to develop robust contingency plans. These plans should aim to build resilience within the food supply chain, making it less vulnerable to the vagaries of climate.  He highlighted the importance of investing in water management infrastructure and supporting farmers in adopting drought-resistant farming practices.  The NFU president stressed that ensuring the UK's ability to feed itself is a matter of national security and requires immediate attention and decisive action from policymakers.
