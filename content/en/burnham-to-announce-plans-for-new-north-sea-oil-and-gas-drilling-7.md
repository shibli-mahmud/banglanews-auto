Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham is reportedly preparing to announce significant plans that would challenge the Labour party's commitment to ending new oil and gas exploration in the North Sea. According to sources, Burnham intends to outline proposals that could facilitate new drilling operations, a stance that starkly contrasts with the party's 2024 general election manifesto. Labour's manifesto explicitly stated a pledge to "not issue any new oil and gas exploration licences", aiming to accelerate the transition to renewable energy sources and address the climate crisis.

The potential announcement from Burnham has already sent ripples through the Labour party, sparking a vigorous debate amongst its members and representatives. Critics argue that such a move would undermine the party's credibility on environmental issues and send a mixed message to voters who have been assured of a commitment to a green future. Supporters, however, might contend that a pragmatic approach is necessary to ensure energy security and maintain jobs during the ongoing transition, particularly in regions heavily reliant on the oil and gas sector.

Burnham's office has not yet officially commented on the details of the rumoured plans, but the prospect of a prominent Labour figure deviating from the party line on such a critical issue highlights the complex and often contentious nature of energy policy. The North Sea oil and gas debate is a focal point for environmental groups, trade unions, and industry bodies, each with deeply held convictions. This potential announcement by Burnham is likely to intensify these discussions and put further pressure on the Labour leadership to clarify its position on the future of fossil fuels in the UK.
