The mid to late 20th century was a golden era for Bengali advertising. Before the ubiquitous screens and targeted algorithms of the digital age, advertisements were crafted with a different sensibility. Hand-painted billboards, adorning the bustling streets of Kolkata and Dhaka, were not just commercial tools but often works of art, employing skilled calligraphers and illustrators. These visual narratives, rich in local idioms and cultural references, resonated deeply with the audience, fostering a sense of connection and familiarity. 

Radio jingles, too, played a pivotal role. Catchy tunes, often set to traditional Bengali melodies, became earworms, embedding product names and brand messages into the collective consciousness. These sonic advertisements were more than just promotional; they were integral to the cultural soundscape, often sung by popular artists and becoming part of everyday life. 

The storytelling in these advertisements was also distinct. They often focused on values like family, tradition, and community, reflecting the societal ethos of the time. A simple soap advertisement might depict a mother showering her child, emphasizing purity and care, while a food product ad could highlight shared meals and familial bonds. This approach fostered a trust and emotional connection that is increasingly difficult to replicate in the fast-paced, often impersonal, digital marketing world. 

However, with the advent of television and, more recently, the internet and social media, the landscape of advertising has drastically changed. The personalized, data-driven approach of modern advertising, while effective, often lacks the handcrafted charm and cultural depth that characterized Bengali ads of yesteryear. The intricate artistry, the soulful melodies, and the earnest storytelling are being replaced by slick, often globalized, campaigns. This shift leaves us questioning whether we are losing something invaluable – a piece of our cultural heritage – in the relentless pursuit of contemporary marketing trends. The forgotten art of Bengali advertising serves as a poignant reminder of a time when commerce and culture were more intimately intertwined.
