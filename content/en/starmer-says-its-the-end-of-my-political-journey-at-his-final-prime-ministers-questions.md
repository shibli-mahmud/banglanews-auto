Sir Keir Starmer delivered his valedictory address at Prime Minister's Questions (PMQs) today, marking what he described as 'the end of my political journey' in this capacity. The Labour leader, who is stepping down from his role, received a surprisingly warm reception from Members of Parliament on both sides of the House of Commons.  In a departure from the usual fiery exchanges, the atmosphere was largely one of respect and reflection.  Tributes poured in from various corners of the chamber, with MPs from all parties acknowledging his tenure.  Beyond formal praise, the session was peppered with lighthearted moments.  Colleagues traded jokes and shared anecdotes, offering a glimpse into the personal relationships forged during his time in Parliament.  The Prime Minister himself offered a gracious farewell, reflecting on the debates and interactions they had shared.  Starmer, in turn, expressed his gratitude for the opportunity to serve and engage in the parliamentary process, acknowledging the significance of PMQs as a cornerstone of British democracy.  While the tone was predominantly one of amicable closure, the underlying political implications of his departure and the subsequent leadership race for the Labour party remained a topic of hushed discussion amongst observers.  His final appearance served as a poignant reminder of the transient nature of political careers and the complex tapestry of relationships that define parliamentary life.
