Downing Street has expressed disapproval over remarks made by US Vice President J.D. Vance, who, in a post on X (formerly Twitter), stated that the 'only response is righteous anger' following the death of Henry Nowak. The UK government, through its official channels, has pushed back against this sentiment, suggesting that such language is counterproductive and plays into the hands of those actively seeking to sow discord.  While the specific context of Henry Nowak's death and the reasons behind Vance's strong reaction are not detailed in this immediate statement, the official UK response emphasizes a desire for de-escalation and constructive dialogue.  A spokesperson for the Prime Minister's office stated, 'We are aware of the comments made. It is never helpful to engage in rhetoric that seeks to stir division, particularly in sensitive situations.'  The remarks underscore a broader concern within diplomatic circles about the potential for inflammatory online statements to derail efforts towards understanding and reconciliation.  The UK's stance suggests a preference for measured responses and a rejection of language that could be perceived as inflammatory or overly aggressive, particularly when addressing the aftermath of tragic events. This incident highlights the delicate nature of international communication and the impact of social media on diplomatic discourse, with Downing Street clearly signalling its disavowal of Vance's approach.
