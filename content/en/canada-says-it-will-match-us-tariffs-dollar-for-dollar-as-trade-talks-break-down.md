The Canadian government's decision comes as a sharp response to the US's imposition of new tariffs on Canadian imports, a move that has sent shockwaves through bilateral trade relations. Officials in Ottawa expressed deep disappointment that talks had collapsed at such a late stage, indicating that significant progress had been made before the final hurdles proved insurmountable.  The new tariffs, which target a wide array of products, are intended to exert considerable economic pressure on the United States, mirroring the financial impact of the American levies on Canadian businesses.  Analysts warn that this tit-for-tat escalation could have widespread repercussions, potentially disrupting supply chains, increasing costs for consumers on both sides of the border, and casting a shadow over the future of the long-standing trade agreement.  Businesses reliant on cross-border trade are bracing for uncertainty, with many scrambling to assess the financial implications and explore alternative sourcing or markets. The breakdown in talks raises serious questions about the willingness of both sides to compromise and the path forward for resolving this escalating trade conflict.
