U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) confirmed on [Date] that it had conducted strikes against multiple targets in southern Iran, including missile launch sites and boats.  According to a statement released by CENTCOM, these actions were a direct response to what they described as "imminent threats" posed by Iranian forces.  The military elaborated that the strikes were undertaken in "self-defense" to neutralize capabilities that could have been used to attack U.S. interests or regional partners.  Details regarding the specific locations and the extent of the damage caused by the strikes have not been fully disclosed, but CENTCOM indicated that the operation aimed to degrade Iran's ability to project power and launch attacks in the region.

The announcement comes amidst a period of heightened tensions between the United States and Iran, marked by ongoing concerns over Iran's nuclear program, its ballistic missile development, and its support for regional proxy groups.  The U.S. has consistently warned against Iranian aggression and has maintained a strong military presence in the Persian Gulf and surrounding areas to deter such actions and protect vital shipping lanes.  This latest round of strikes suggests a continuation of the U.S. strategy to proactively address perceived threats, even as diplomatic channels remain active in efforts to de-escalate regional conflicts.  The international community will be closely monitoring Iran's response to these developments.
