The anonymity granted to those involved in the Southport knife attack has, for some parents of the child survivors, led to a feeling of erasure. Speaking to the BBC, these parents expressed deep frustration that the legal requirement for anonymity has inadvertently diminished the public narrative surrounding their daughters' traumatic experiences. They feel their children, who bravely endured a horrific ordeal, are being overlooked in the broader conversation about the incident, with the focus often drifting towards the perpetrator rather than the profound impact on the victims.

This lack of public acknowledgement, they argue, has directly contributed to insufficient support systems being put in place. Without the ability to openly share their daughters' stories and the struggles they continue to face in their recovery – both physical and psychological – parents feel isolated and unheard. They are campaigning for greater recognition of the survivors' plights, emphasizing that while legal protections are necessary, they should not come at the cost of the victims' voices and the critical support they deserve. The parents' plea is for a more balanced approach, one that respects anonymity while still ensuring the survivors' bravery and the ongoing need for comprehensive care are not lost in the process.
