Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham is poised to unveil plans for new oil and gas drilling in the North Sea, a decision that has immediately sparked controversy and placed him at odds with the Labour party's official stance. The Labour manifesto for the 2024 general election explicitly pledged to cease issuing new licenses for oil and gas exploration and extraction in the region. This about-face from a prominent Labour figure raises significant questions about the party's internal cohesion and future energy policy direction. 

Sources close to Burnham suggest the announcement is motivated by a perceived need to secure the UK's energy independence and protect jobs in the North Sea region. Proponents of continued exploration argue that a phased transition away from fossil fuels is more pragmatic than an immediate cessation, citing the potential for significant economic disruption and job losses in traditional oil and gas communities. They also contend that domestic production, under stringent environmental regulations, is preferable to relying on imports from countries with potentially lower standards.

However, environmental campaigners and many within the Labour party are decrying the move as a betrayal of climate commitments. They argue that investing in new fossil fuel infrastructure is incompatible with the urgent need to tackle climate change and will lock the UK into polluting energy sources for decades to come. The announcement is expected to intensify the debate within the Labour party regarding its approach to the energy transition and its credibility on environmental issues leading up to future elections.
