In a deeply personal and emotionally charged interview with the BBC's Laura Kuenssberg, former Scottish First Minister Nicola Sturgeon has spoken out about the toll the ongoing Scottish National Party (SNP) embezzlement investigation has taken on her. She declared that she feels she is 'serving a sentence for a crime she did not commit,' a stark admission that underscores the personal impact of the prolonged scrutiny.

During the interview, Kuenssberg directly confronted Sturgeon with questions pertaining to the SNP's finances and the police investigation that has cast a shadow over the party and its former leadership. The line of questioning was pointed, focusing on the complexities and perceptions surrounding the financial probe. At several points during the exchange, Sturgeon appeared to be on the verge of tears, her voice wavering as she attempted to articulate her position and the immense personal burden she carries.

She reiterated her stance of having acted with integrity throughout her tenure and in her handling of party finances, while acknowledging the difficult and unprecedented nature of the situation. The interview offered a rare glimpse into the personal struggle of a prominent political figure facing severe allegations. Sturgeon's words suggest a sense of injustice and the profound emotional weight of being associated with an investigation where she maintains her innocence. The broader implications of the scandal on Scottish politics and the SNP's future remain a central point of public and political discussion.
