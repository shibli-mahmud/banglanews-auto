FIFA president Gianni Infantino has strongly refuted claims that Uefa paid a significant sum of money to a woman he allegedly had an affair with during his time as the organization's general secretary. The allegations, brought to light by a Daily Telegraph investigation, suggest that a confidential severance package was agreed upon with the woman, purportedly to settle the matter and prevent public disclosure.

According to the report, the payment was made while Infantino held the influential position of general secretary at Uefa, a period that preceded his election as FIFA president. The investigation cites sources who claim the payment was intended to resolve the alleged affair and maintain discretion. The Daily Telegraph's report details the circumstances surrounding the alleged payment, including the timing and the involvement of Uefa officials.

In response to the accusations, Infantino's representatives issued a firm denial, labeling the claims as "baseless and false." They stated that there was no wrongdoing on Infantino's part and that any suggestion of a payout related to a personal matter while he was at Uefa is untrue. The denial emphasized Infantino's commitment to transparency and ethical conduct throughout his career in football governance. The controversy is expected to put further scrutiny on Infantino's leadership and past dealings within football's administrative bodies.
