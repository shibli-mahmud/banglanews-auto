The Metropolitan Police's counter-terrorism command, which is currently investigating the death of former Conservative MP Ann Widdecombe, has reportedly reopened an investigation into an "attempted burglary" at the Kent home of veteran Brexit campaigner Nigel Farage. The BBC has learned that this separate inquiry is being conducted by the same unit handling the probe into Widdecombe's death, which was officially ruled a homicide by stabbing. While the details surrounding the "attempted burglary" are not fully clear, its reopening by the counter-terrorism command indicates it is being treated with significant seriousness. The incident, which is understood to have occurred last year, now forms part of a wider, albeit complex, police investigation. The juxtaposition of these two inquiries raises questions about potential connections or a pattern of activity being examined by the authorities. Sources close to the investigation have emphasized that the reopening of the burglary case is a procedural step and does not necessarily confirm a direct link to Widdecombe's death, but it highlights the sensitive nature of the overall inquiry. Further information is expected to be released as the investigations progress. The Met Police have declined to comment on live investigations.
