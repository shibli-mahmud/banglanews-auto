Airports across the UK, particularly the nation's busiest hubs, Heathrow and Gatwick, experienced significant operational disruptions on [Date] due to severe thunderstorms. Over 600 flights have been delayed, impacting thousands of travellers hoping to depart or arrive in London.

The primary cause of the extensive delays has been identified as adverse weather conditions, with intense thunderstorms and heavy rainfall dominating the skies over southern England. Air traffic control systems have been forced to implement revised procedures to ensure flight safety, which has inevitably slowed down operations. Aircraft have been subjected to holding patterns, and some departures have been temporarily suspended as a precautionary measure against lightning strikes and poor visibility.

Passengers at both Heathrow and Gatwick reported scenes of confusion and frustration as flight information boards displayed lengthy delays. Many took to social media to express their discontent, sharing images of crowded terminals and sharing stories of missed connections and disrupted travel plans. Airlines have issued statements apologising for the inconvenience and are working to re-accommodate affected passengers where possible.

While the majority of the delays are attributed to the immediate weather impact, the knock-on effect is expected to persist for some time. Experts warn that as the weather system moves, disruptions could continue to affect other parts of the UK and Europe. Travellers are being advised to check their flight status with their respective airlines before heading to the airport and to allow for extra travel time. The Met Office has issued severe weather warnings for the affected regions, urging the public to remain vigilant.
