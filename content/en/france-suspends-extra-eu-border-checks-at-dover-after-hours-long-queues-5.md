The port of Dover announced on Saturday that French border officials would be halting their "enhanced" EU border checks, a measure that had been implemented to bolster security and compliance with EU regulations. This decision was made after thousands of travellers and lorries experienced gridlock, with some facing delays stretching for many hours. The situation was exacerbated by the current heatwave gripping the UK and parts of Europe, with temperatures reaching concerning levels.

Sources close to the port have indicated that the suspension is temporary, and the French authorities have not provided a definitive timeline for their reintroduction. The extra checks, which involve more stringent document verification and scrutiny, are understood to be a routine part of EU border control for non-Schengen traffic entering the bloc. However, their intensified application at Dover, a key gateway for trade and tourism between the UK and the continent, has repeatedly led to significant congestion.

The delays have had a knock-on effect on supply chains and holiday plans. Ferry operators and logistics companies have expressed frustration, urging for a more streamlined and predictable process. Passengers have shared harrowing accounts of being stuck in sweltering conditions, with limited access to facilities. While the suspension offers immediate relief, questions remain about the long-term strategy to manage border flows efficiently and prevent future disruptions, particularly as peak travel seasons continue and the spectre of further heatwaves looms.
