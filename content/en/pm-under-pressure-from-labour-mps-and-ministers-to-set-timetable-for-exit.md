Sources within the Labour party have indicated to the BBC that a significant number of MPs and even some ministers are urging the Prime Minister to establish a definitive timeline for stepping down. The transport secretary is understood to be among those advising the leader to provide this clarity, signalling a growing internal consensus on the need for a transition plan. This pressure comes at a time when the party is grappling with various policy challenges and internal strategizing for future leadership.  The demand for a timetable suggests a desire for certainty and a structured approach to the upcoming political landscape, allowing for a smoother handover and continued focus on governance without the looming uncertainty of an indefinite tenure.  While no specific dates have been publicly announced or leaked, the consistent reports of this internal push highlight a significant development in the party's dynamics.  The Prime Minister's office has yet to issue a formal statement addressing these calls for a timeline, but the ongoing discussions are indicative of a critical juncture for the party's leadership and its forward-looking strategy.  This internal dialogue could shape future political announcements and the party's public perception.
