An undercover BBC investigation has exposed a shadowy network of illegal sperm donor services operating openly on social media, preying on desperate and vulnerable women seeking to conceive. These unregulated services bypass crucial legal and medical safeguards designed to protect both donors and recipients, raising serious concerns about the health and genetic implications for any resulting children.

The investigation, which saw an undercover reporter pose as a woman seeking a donor, revealed the shocking ease with which individuals can advertise and supply sperm outside of established clinics. In one particularly unsettling transaction, a donor agreed to provide sperm for £100, with the sample delivered via postal service. The 'package' arrived not in a sterile medical container, but alongside a common household item – a carton of passata, highlighting the clandestine and unhygienic nature of these arrangements.

Experts warn that such practices carry significant risks. Without proper screening for infectious diseases and genetic conditions, there is a substantial danger of transmitting serious illnesses to both the recipient and any potential offspring. Furthermore, the lack of legal frameworks surrounding these informal donations can lead to complex future legal battles regarding parental rights and responsibilities, leaving all parties in an uncertain and potentially damaging situation.

Campaigners and fertility experts are calling for greater regulation and enforcement to shut down these dangerous operations. They emphasize the importance of using licensed fertility clinics for assisted reproduction, where donors undergo rigorous health checks and recipients receive comprehensive counselling and support. The BBC's findings serve as a stark warning about the growing exploitation of vulnerable individuals by illicit online services.
