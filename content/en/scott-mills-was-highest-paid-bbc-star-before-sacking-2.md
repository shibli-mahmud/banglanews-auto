Radio presenter Scott Mills topped the list of the BBC's highest-paid stars for the financial year ending March 2023, earning an estimated £745,000 from the corporation. This significant sum was primarily attributed to his lucrative contract for the highly popular Radio 2 breakfast show, a role he held until his recent move to an afternoon slot. The figures, released as part of the BBC's annual report, highlight the substantial remuneration offered to key on-air talent.

Mills' earnings place him above other prominent BBC personalities, a detail that has drawn attention given his recent programming changes. While the exact breakdown of his earnings from different BBC activities was not fully detailed, his primary income stream was understood to be his flagship breakfast programme. The BBC, a publicly funded broadcaster, faces ongoing scrutiny over its expenditure on talent, particularly during times of budget constraints and public sector pay freezes. The revelation of Mills' substantial salary is likely to reignite debate about the pay structures within the organisation. Despite the high earnings, Mills has transitioned to a new weekday afternoon show on Radio 2, a move that has been met with mixed reactions from listeners. The BBC has defended its talent costs by emphasizing the competitive market for experienced broadcasters and the vital role they play in retaining audiences across its diverse radio services.
