Andy Burnham, the Mayor of Greater Manchester, has announced a comprehensive strategy designed to provide housing for every rough sleeper in the region by the festive season. The initiative, dubbed "A Bed for Every Night" (though not officially named as such yet, the sentiment echoes a similar successful campaign), aims to replicate the urgency and success of the government-backed 'Everyone In' scheme, which saw thousands of homeless individuals housed during the initial lockdowns of the pandemic. 

Burnham stated that the plan involves a multi-pronged approach, leveraging existing resources and partnerships with local authorities, charities, and the voluntary sector. This includes identifying individuals sleeping rough, offering them immediate shelter, and then working on longer-term housing solutions. The emphasis is on providing not just a roof over their heads, but also the necessary support services, such as mental health care, addiction support, and employment assistance, to help them rebuild their lives. 

The Mayor acknowledged the challenges ahead but expressed optimism, highlighting the collaborative spirit demonstrated during the pandemic. He stressed that this is not just a temporary fix but a commitment to tackling the root causes of homelessness. The plan is expected to be funded through a combination of government grants, local authority budgets, and potential private sector contributions. The success of 'Everyone In' proved that widespread housing of rough sleepers is achievable with political will and collective effort, and Burnham is hoping to harness that momentum once again.
