Greater Manchester Police (GMP) has publicly apologized after a critical review found that the mobile devices belonging to Jihad Al-Shamie, the perpetrator of an attack at a Manchester synagogue, were not subjected to a comprehensive examination before the incident. The revelation has sparked considerable alarm and scrutiny over the effectiveness of police procedures and intelligence sharing.

According to reports, the police had obtained Al-Shamie's phones but a thorough forensic analysis was not completed in the time leading up to the attack. This failure to properly scrutinize the devices means a potential opportunity to uncover vital intelligence about his intentions or any potential threats was missed. The exact nature of the information that might have been found remains speculative, but the implication is that preventative measures could have been taken.

The apology from GMP acknowledges the gravity of the situation and the distress it may cause to the community. A spokesperson stated that the force is committed to learning from this incident and is undertaking a full review of its internal processes to prevent similar lapses in the future. The incident is likely to lead to calls for greater accountability and a re-evaluation of the resources allocated to digital forensics and the handling of intelligence related to potential extremist threats. The community, understandably, awaits further details and assurances that such a significant oversight will not be repeated.
