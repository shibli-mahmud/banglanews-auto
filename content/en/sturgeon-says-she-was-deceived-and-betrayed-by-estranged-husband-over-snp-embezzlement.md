Former First Minister of Scotland, Nicola Sturgeon, has spoken out about the devastating impact of her estranged husband Peter Murrell's guilty plea in the Scottish National Party (SNP) embezzlement investigation. Speaking publicly for the first time since the development, Sturgeon revealed her profound sense of personal shock and betrayal, stating she is now 'coming to terms with being married to someone she did not know.'

Murrell, who was the SNP's chief executive, pleaded guilty to embezzling funds from the party, a scandal that has rocked the heart of Scottish politics. The former First Minister, who stepped down from her leadership role earlier this year, described the revelations as a deeply personal blow. She indicated that the legal proceedings have forced her to re-evaluate her understanding of her long-term partner.

'It is profoundly shocking,' Sturgeon stated in an interview. 'It is deeply distressing. And it has been a very difficult time for me personally, as I have had to come to terms with the reality of the situation and, frankly, coming to terms with being married to someone I did not know.'

The ongoing investigation has cast a long shadow over the SNP, a party Sturgeon led to significant electoral success for many years. The guilty plea by Murrell represents a major development in the case, and its ramifications continue to be felt within the party and the wider political landscape of Scotland. Sturgeon's comments underscore the immense personal toll the scandal has taken, extending beyond the political arena into her private life.
