The Metropolitan Police's counter-terrorism command, a unit typically tasked with investigating the most serious national security threats, is reportedly revisiting an incident at Nigel Farage's property. While initial reports focused on the circumstances of his death, it has now emerged that officers are examining an 'attempted burglary' that is understood to have taken place last year. Sources close to the investigation, speaking to the BBC, have indicated that this line of inquiry is being pursued as part of the broader review of events surrounding the former Member of Parliament.

The reopening of this investigation by a specialized unit has sparked considerable interest. While the precise details of the alleged burglary remain unclear, its re-examination by counter-terror officers suggests a potential for it to be linked to wider security concerns. The timing of this renewed focus, particularly in the context of ongoing inquiries into Mr. Farage's passing, has led to speculation about the nature of the incident. Further details are expected to emerge as the police continue their work, with the Metropolitan Police yet to issue a formal statement on the matter.
