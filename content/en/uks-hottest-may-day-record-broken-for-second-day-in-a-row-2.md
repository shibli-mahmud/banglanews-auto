A sweltering heatwave has gripped the UK, pushing temperatures to historic highs. On Wednesday, May 26th, the mercury climbed to an astonishing 35.1 degrees Celsius (95.2 degrees Fahrenheit) in Kew Gardens, south-west London, setting a new provisional record for the hottest May day in the country's history. This searing temperature surpassed the previous record set just the day before, underscoring the intensity of the ongoing heatwave.

The Met Office has issued amber warnings for extreme heat across large parts of England, urging the public to take precautions and stay hydrated. Forecasters anticipate that temperatures will remain exceptionally high for the coming days, with potential for further records to be broken. The unusual May warmth is attributed to a high-pressure system drawing in hot air from continental Europe. While many are enjoying the unseasonably warm weather, health officials are concerned about the risks posed by the extreme heat, particularly to vulnerable individuals. Advice includes staying out of the sun during peak hours, seeking cool environments, and ensuring pets and children are not left unattended in vehicles. The sustained high temperatures also raise concerns about potential impacts on agriculture and water resources.
