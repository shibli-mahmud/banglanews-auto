JERUSALEM – Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu declared on Tuesday that his government will not agree to a military withdrawal from the Gaza Strip until the Hamas militant group is "genuinely" disarmed. This statement comes in response to reports of a 15-point peace proposal allegedly presented by former U.S. President Donald Trump, aimed at resolving the ongoing conflict.

While details of Trump's alleged plan have not been officially released, sources close to the matter suggest it included provisions for a ceasefire, humanitarian aid, and a phased withdrawal of Israeli forces. However, Netanyahu's firm stance emphasizes Israel's unwavering commitment to dismantling Hamas's military capabilities as a prerequisite for any significant de-escalation or troop redeployment.

"We will not leave Gaza until we have achieved our objective of disarming Hamas," Netanyahu stated at a press conference. He reiterated that Israel's primary goal remains the complete eradication of Hamas's military infrastructure and leadership, ensuring that the group can no longer pose a threat to Israeli security. This position underscores the deep-seated security concerns that continue to guide Israel's strategy in the region.

The rejection of the reported Trump initiative highlights the significant gap in objectives between international peace proposals and the current Israeli government's security demands. It also signals a potential challenge for any future diplomatic efforts seeking to broker a lasting peace in Gaza, as Israel prioritizes its security imperatives above other considerations.
