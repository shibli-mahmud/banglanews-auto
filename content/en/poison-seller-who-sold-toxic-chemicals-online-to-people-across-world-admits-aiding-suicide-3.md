A man dubbed the 'poison seller' has admitted to charges of aiding suicides in Canada, following a widespread investigation into his online sale of dangerous chemicals. Kenneth Law, from Ontario, pleaded guilty in a Canadian court to offering or providing instructions or assistance for the purpose of causing death or self-induced bodily harm.  This admission comes after a lengthy probe that linked his online activities to multiple deaths across the globe.

While Law's guilty plea addresses victims within Canada, it has reignited calls for justice from families in the United Kingdom.  These families believe Law should also be held accountable for the deaths of 79 individuals in Britain, who allegedly purchased toxic substances from his websites.  Law's business, operating under various aliases, reportedly offered a range of chemicals that could be used to end one's life, often with minimal oversight or age verification.  Investigators in the UK have been working to build a case against Law, but the complexities of international law and jurisdiction have presented significant challenges.  The families of the British victims are now urging authorities to expedite any potential extradition or prosecution efforts, emphasizing the devastating impact Law's alleged actions have had on their lives.  The case highlights the growing concerns surrounding the online trade of potentially lethal substances and the difficulties in regulating such activities on a global scale.
