China has sharply criticised the United Kingdom's move to take British Steel into public ownership, labeling the decision as a potentially disruptive force in global markets. The UK government announced its intention to nationalise the struggling steel manufacturer, stating that the move was essential to safeguard "a vital national capability" and protect jobs. However, Beijing has interpreted this action through a lens of protectionism, suggesting it could create an uneven playing field for international competitors.

In a statement released by the Ministry of Commerce, a spokesperson for the Chinese government expressed concern that such nationalisation efforts could lead to unfair trade practices and discourage foreign direct investment. The statement highlighted that while governments have the right to manage their domestic industries, these actions should not negatively impact other countries' economic interests or undermine the principles of fair competition. China has previously expressed its commitment to open trade and a rules-based international economic order, and it appears to view the UK's decision as a deviation from these principles.

The nationalisation of British Steel comes at a time when global supply chains are already under strain, and trade tensions between major economies remain a significant concern. Beijing's reaction suggests that it will be closely monitoring the implementation of this policy and its broader implications for the steel industry worldwide. The UK government, meanwhile, has defended its decision as a necessary step to ensure the long-term viability of a critical industrial asset, emphasizing its commitment to its national interests while also aiming to maintain stable international economic relations.
