Ofcom's annual Online Safety Report, released this week, has delivered a stern verdict on the safety measures implemented by dominant video-sharing platforms. The report highlights that while platforms are aware of the risks, their efforts to shield children from inappropriate content, such as cyberbullying, exposure to violent or sexual material, and potentially harmful challenges, are falling short.  Ofcom's assessment suggests that the sheer volume of content and the sophisticated algorithms used by these platforms make it incredibly challenging for them to consistently enforce their own safety policies.

The watchdog's findings are particularly concerning given the significant popularity of TikTok and YouTube among children and teenagers. Many young people spend hours daily on these platforms, making them highly susceptible to the potential negative impacts of unsupervised online activity.  The report emphasizes the need for more proactive and effective safety mechanisms, rather than reactive ones that often come into play only after harm has occurred.

In response to the report, YouTube stated that it is "committed to providing an appropriate experience for younger viewers" and has "worked with experts to develop features designed to keep children safe."  They pointed to their existing age-gating features and educational resources.  TikTok expressed "disappointment" with Ofcom's assessment, asserting that they have "invested significantly in safety features and are committed to protecting our younger users." They claim their efforts have not been fully acknowledged by the regulator.  However, Ofcom's report suggests that further, more robust interventions are necessary to ensure these platforms truly prioritize the well-being of their youngest users.
