In the wake of the catastrophic earthquakes that have left a trail of destruction across Venezuela, the survival of an 18-day-old infant named Juan David has ignited a beacon of hope amidst the despair. Pulled alive from the rubble days after the tremors, the baby's rescue is being hailed as a miracle.  Even more remarkable is the story of his mother, who, despite being trapped in the collapsed structure, credits her infant son with giving her the will to live.

Speaking to the BBC from a temporary shelter, the mother, whose name has been withheld for privacy, recounted the terrifying moments following the earthquake. "When the building came down, all I could think about was Juan David," she stated, her voice still trembling. "I knew I had to get out. I had to be there for him. He was my only focus, my reason to fight."

Her account paints a harrowing picture of survival against all odds. "It was dark, and I couldn't move much. Every sound was terrifying. But every time I felt weak, I thought of his tiny face, his small hands. That's what kept me going."

The successful rescue of both mother and child has become a powerful narrative of resilience and the unbreakable bond between a parent and child. In a country grappling with immense loss and widespread devastation, the story of Juan David and his mother offers a much-needed glimmer of optimism, demonstrating the extraordinary strength of the human spirit in the face of unimaginable tragedy.
