The latest round of US sanctions, announced this week, targets entities and individuals involved in Iran's energy sector, along with those facilitating trade with the Islamic Republic. The stated aim is to further curtail Iran's oil exports and pressure the government to curb its nuclear program and regional activities. However, the broad scope of the sanctions, which include secondary sanctions on third-party countries, has drawn sharp criticism from Beijing.

China, a long-standing critic of unilateral US sanctions, argues that these actions undermine global trade and disrupt established international relations. "These measures are a blatant interference in the internal affairs of other countries and a violation of international law," stated a spokesperson for China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. "China opposes unilateral sanctions and the practice of 'long-arm jurisdiction.' We urge the US to adopt a responsible attitude and uphold the international order."

The US administration has made it clear that countries continuing to engage in significant transactions with Iran, particularly concerning oil sales, will face consequences. This puts nations like China, which relies on Iranian crude to meet its energy demands, in a precarious position. Beijing has consistently maintained that its trade with Iran is legitimate and in accordance with international norms. The escalating tensions highlight a growing divide between the US and China over global economic policy and the enforcement of international sanctions.
