The Police Federation of England and Wales (PFEW) is reportedly considering legal action over the decision to include two of PC Andrew Harper's killers in the government's new early release scheme. The scheme, which allows certain prisoners to be released up to 18 months before the end of their sentence, is scheduled to begin in October. 

PC Harper was tragically killed in August 2019 while responding to a burglary in Berkshire. He was dragged for more than a mile by a car driven by the convicted murderers. Henry Long and Albert Bowers were sentenced to 16 and 13 years respectively for manslaughter, but their convictions were upheld by the Court of Appeal. 

John Apter, the national chairman of the PFEW, expressed his profound disappointment and anger at the prospect of the killers being eligible for early release. He stated that the news had caused "immense distress" to PC Harper's family and his colleagues. Apter indicated that the Federation is actively seeking legal advice on how to challenge this decision, emphasizing that they will explore every possible avenue to prevent this from happening. The PFEW believes that the early release scheme, in this context, fails to deliver justice for the victim and his loved ones. The Ministry of Justice has defended the scheme, stating it is designed to encourage rehabilitation and reduce reoffending, with decisions on individual releases made by the Parole Board.
