Tensions ran high in Belfast last night as riot police faced a barrage of bricks, bottles, and pieces of wood during violent clashes. The disorder, which unfolded at a major roundabout in the north-west of the city, necessitated the deployment of a water cannon by the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI). This latest incident follows a stabbing that occurred earlier in the day, which is believed to have contributed to the escalating unrest.

Eyewitnesses reported scenes of chaos as a crowd, believed to be composed of youths, engaged in a sustained attack on police officers. The missiles were reportedly thrown from multiple directions, posing a significant threat to the safety of the law enforcement personnel. In response, police vehicles were seen positioning themselves to create a barrier, and the water cannon was activated in an attempt to disperse the rioters and regain control of the situation.

The PSNI confirmed their presence and acknowledged the challenging circumstances faced by officers. A spokesperson stated that efforts were underway to identify and apprehend those responsible for the violence and the attacks on police. The incident has once again highlighted the volatile security situation in certain areas of Belfast and the ongoing challenges in maintaining public order. Further investigations are expected into the specific catalysts for last night's disorder, beyond the initial knife attack, to understand the full context of the rioting.
