Lissie Harper, the widow of PC Andrew Harper, has fiercely criticized government proposals that could allow for the early release of prisoners, including those convicted of serious offenses. The outcry follows reports suggesting that two of the individuals responsible for PC Harper's tragic death in August 2019 could be considered for release significantly before the end of their sentences. 

Speaking out against the apparent review of early release policies, Mrs. Harper stated, "It's deplorable. They should serve their time." Her comments reflect the deep anger and hurt felt by victims' families when faced with the prospect of perpetrators being freed ahead of schedule. PC Harper was killed while responding to a burglary call, dragged behind a car by fleeing suspects. The case sent shockwaves across the country and highlighted the dangers faced by frontline police officers.

Reports indicate that the Justice Secretary, Conor Burns, is reviewing the mechanisms for early release, a move that has been met with widespread concern from victims' advocacy groups and the public. The specific criteria for such releases and the potential impact on public safety are now under intense scrutiny. Mrs. Harper's powerful statement underscores the ongoing emotional toll on those affected by violent crime and their strong desire for justice to be fully served. The government's position on this matter is expected to be clarified following the review, but Lissie Harper's unambiguous stance sets a firm tone for the debate.
