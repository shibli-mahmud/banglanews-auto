China has voiced its opposition to the UK government's recent decision to take British Steel into public ownership. The British government announced the nationalisation as a measure to safeguard what it described as a 'vital national capability', amidst growing concerns over the future of the company. This significant intervention comes after British Steel, a cornerstone of the UK's industrial landscape, faced severe financial distress, threatening thousands of jobs and broader economic stability.

Beijing's reaction suggests a potential diplomatic friction, with Chinese officials likely viewing the move through the lens of international trade and investment. The nationalisation could be interpreted by some as a protectionist measure, raising questions about foreign investment and market access. While the UK government insists the primary motivation is national security and job preservation, the involvement of a major global player like China in its criticism highlights the complex international dimensions of such industrial policies. The long-term implications of this nationalisation for both the UK's industrial strategy and its international economic relationships remain to be seen.
