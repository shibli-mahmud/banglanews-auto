Lord Blunkett, the former Home Secretary, has called for a significant 'ethical reset' in police leadership across England and Wales, following the publication of a damning report he co-authored. The comprehensive study, which involved extensive consultation, has concluded that the current operational and strategic management of police forces requires a 'fundamental overhaul'.

The report highlights a concerning decline in public trust, attributing it to a series of high-profile scandals and a perceived lack of accountability within the constabulary. Lord Blunkett, speaking to the BBC, stated that "there has been a loss of public trust, and that is not something we can ignore. We need to rebuild that trust, and that starts at the very top."

The findings suggest that leadership at all levels within police forces needs to be re-evaluated, with a stronger emphasis on ethical conduct, transparency, and responsiveness to public concerns. The report proposes a series of recommendations, including enhanced training for senior officers, more robust internal oversight mechanisms, and greater community engagement to foster a more positive relationship between the public and the police.

"It's not about individual failings, though those do occur, but about the systemic issues that have allowed these failings to happen and, in some cases, to be hidden," Lord Blunkett elaborated. He stressed that the proposed 'ethical reset' is crucial for ensuring that the police service not only upholds the law but also acts in a manner that commands the respect and confidence of the communities it serves. The report's authors are urging immediate action from the government and police chiefs to implement the suggested reforms.
