Former U.S. President Donald Trump has declared that a new agreement with Iran is "largely negotiated," a significant statement that has sent ripples through international diplomatic circles. During a recent rally, Trump specifically highlighted that the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, a vital chokepoint for global oil transit, was included in the purported negotiations. This assertion comes amidst ongoing, albeit often opaque, discussions between Iran and global powers regarding its nuclear program.

While Trump's pronouncements suggest a breakthrough, the Iranian side has offered a more nuanced perspective. Tehran has acknowledged progress in parallel talks but stressed that the paramount issue of its nuclear weapons program is not currently on the table for an initial framework agreement. This suggests that while certain points of contention, like regional security and maritime passage, may be nearing resolution, the core of the international community's concern regarding Iran's nuclear ambitions remains a separate and more complex negotiation.

The former president's claims could signal a potential shift in U.S. foreign policy towards Iran should he be re-elected. His administration previously withdrew from the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA), the 2015 nuclear deal, and reimposed stringent sanctions on Iran. The prospect of a "largely negotiated" deal, particularly one that addresses the Strait of Hormuz, indicates a potential return to engagement, though the specifics and guarantees for the international community remain to be seen. The divergence in emphasis between Washington and Tehran on the nuclear aspect underscores the intricate nature of any future agreement.
