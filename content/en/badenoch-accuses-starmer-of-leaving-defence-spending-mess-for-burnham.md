Kemi Badenoch, the Conservative Party's chair, has vehemently criticised the Labour Party's approach to defence spending, accusing Sir Keir Starmer of creating a substantial financial deficit that would burden his colleagues if they were to come into power. Speaking at a press conference, Badenoch stated that Labour's defence investment plan is missing an estimated £5 billion, a figure she described as a 'mess' that would fall to figures like Shadow Chancellor Rachel Reeves and a potential future defence secretary, such as Andy Burnham, to resolve.

Badenoch's remarks come amidst ongoing scrutiny of both major parties' economic and defence policies. She asserted that Labour's proposals lack concrete funding mechanisms for their stated defence ambitions, raising questions about their feasibility and credibility. The Conservative leader argued that such a significant funding gap demonstrates a lack of seriousness and preparedness from the opposition, particularly concerning national security.

She further suggested that this alleged financial shortfall highlights a broader issue with Labour's economic planning, implying that their spending commitments, particularly in crucial areas like defence, are not adequately backed by realistic financial strategies. Badenoch challenged Labour to provide a clear and detailed explanation of how they intend to bridge this £5 billion gap, questioning the impact this deficit would have on military readiness and the UK's standing on the international stage. The accusation intensifies the political debate surrounding defence expenditure ahead of any potential general election.
