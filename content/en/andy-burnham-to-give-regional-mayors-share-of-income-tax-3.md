Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham has put forward a groundbreaking proposal that would see a portion of income tax revenue devolved to regional mayors across England. The move, championed by Burnham, is intended to provide local leaders with greater financial autonomy and the ability to invest directly in their communities, aligning with the government's stated ambition to 'level up' the country and distribute power to 'every postcode'.

Burnham argued that by giving mayors control over a direct revenue stream, they could make more informed decisions about local priorities, whether that be in transport, skills, or infrastructure. This, he believes, would lead to more effective and responsive public services tailored to the specific needs of each region.

The proposal, however, has been met with a degree of skepticism. Critics have pointed out that the exact percentage of income tax to be devolved, the mechanism for its allocation, and the potential impact on national funding have not yet been clearly articulated. Concerns have also been raised about the potential for increased regional inequalities if the system is not carefully managed. The Treasury has yet to formally comment on the specifics of the proposal, indicating that further dialogue and detailed planning will be required to assess its feasibility and potential benefits.
