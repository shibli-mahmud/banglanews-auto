Prime Minister Rishi Sunak has revealed that the government is seriously considering a ban on disposable barbecues as a measure to mitigate the escalating risk of wildfires. Speaking to reporters, he stated that the public should not be using these single-use grills 'at this moment in time' due to the heightened danger. This statement comes amid a period of dry weather and high temperatures that have significantly increased the likelihood of fires breaking out, particularly in grassy and wooded areas.  Fire services across the country have been stretched thin responding to numerous incidents, many of which are believed to have been started by discarded barbecues. Environmental groups and fire safety experts have long campaigned for a ban, citing the environmental damage caused by disposable grills and the significant fire hazard they represent when improperly extinguished or discarded. The current consideration by the government suggests a potential shift in policy, moving away from advisories to outright prohibition.  Details of any potential ban, including its scope and enforcement, are yet to be announced, but the Prime Minister's firm stance signals a strong intent to address the issue proactively.  Further announcements are expected as the government assesses the full impact of current weather conditions and the feasibility of implementing such a ban.
