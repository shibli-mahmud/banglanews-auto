Andrew Malkinson, a man who endured 17 years of wrongful imprisonment, has branded the 24-year prison sentence given to rapist Paul Quinn as an 'insult'. Quinn was convicted of the brutal 2003 rape in Salford, a crime that saw Malkinson wrongly jailed for nearly two decades before his eventual exoneration. Speaking out after the sentencing at Manchester Crown Court, Malkinson expressed his profound disappointment and anger. He stated, "The sentence is an insult. It doesn't reflect the years I lost, nor the terror that woman must have gone through." Malkinson's fight for justice, which involved relentless campaigning and the discovery of new DNA evidence, ultimately led to his release in 2020 and the overturning of his conviction. The original investigation, which led to Malkinson's imprisonment, has faced significant criticism for its flaws and the failure to identify Quinn sooner. Quinn was arrested in 2022 after advances in DNA technology finally linked him to the crime scene. The lengthy period between the attack and Quinn's conviction, coupled with Malkinson's lost years, has fuelled anger and calls for a review of the initial police investigation. "This sentence is not justice. Justice would have been identifying the real perpetrator years ago and sparing me and the victim so much pain," Malkinson added, highlighting the systemic failures that allowed a guilty man to remain free while an innocent one suffered. The case has once again brought to the fore the devastating consequences of wrongful convictions and the imperative for robust investigative practices.
