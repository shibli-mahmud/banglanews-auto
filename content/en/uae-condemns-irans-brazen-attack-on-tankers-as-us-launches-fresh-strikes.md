The escalating crisis in the Persian Gulf has seen the United Arab Emirates (UAE) take a firm stance against Iran, denouncing the "brazen" attacks on commercial tankers as a grave threat to maritime security and regional peace. The UAE's foreign ministry issued a statement emphasizing the need for de-escalation and adherence to international law, while holding Iran responsible for the recent incidents. This strong condemnation from a key regional player underscores the growing international concern over Iran's actions.

Meanwhile, the United States has continued its military response, launching a third consecutive night of strikes targeting what it described as Iranian military infrastructure and missile facilities. These retaliatory actions, according to US officials, are aimed at deterring further aggression and protecting vital shipping lanes. The US has also indicated a new phase of its strategy, with President Trump reportedly announcing a "20% charge" as part of a new blockade initiative on the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil trade. This move is seen as a significant escalation, potentially impacting oil prices and international commerce. The ongoing military exchanges and the imposition of new economic measures signal a period of heightened geopolitical uncertainty in the Middle East, with far-reaching implications for global energy markets and diplomatic relations.
