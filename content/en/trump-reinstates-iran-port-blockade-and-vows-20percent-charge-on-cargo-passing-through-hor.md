President Donald Trump has dramatically intensified sanctions against Iran by reinstating a de facto blockade on its ports and imposing a new punitive measure: a 20% tariff on all cargo passing through the strategically critical Strait of Hormuz. The announcement, made through official White House channels and amplified via presidential statements, marks a significant escalation in the U.S. administration's 'maximum pressure' campaign against the Islamic Republic.  The blockade, while not a physical interdiction of ships, implies a severe restriction on vessels attempting to dock at Iranian ports, effectively cutting off major trade routes.  The additional 20% charge on cargo transiting the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway through which a substantial portion of the world's oil supply flows, is designed to inflict further economic pain on Iran and its trading partners.  Administration officials stated that the objective is to deny Iran the financial resources it uses to fund what they describe as destabilizing activities in the region.  The move has already sent ripples through international markets, with oil prices showing volatility and global shipping companies assessing the potential impact on their operations.  Critics of the policy argue that such stringent measures could lead to humanitarian concerns and further destabilize an already volatile region, while supporters maintain it is a necessary step to curb Iran's alleged illicit activities.
