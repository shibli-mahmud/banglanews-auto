Prime Minister Rishi Sunak has declared his government will be 'relentless' in its pursuit of stopping illegal immigration via small boats crossing the English Channel. Speaking on the issue, Sunak stated that enforcement capabilities have been significantly enhanced, with more resources dedicated to intercepting these dangerous journeys.  He highlighted the government's commitment to breaking the business model of people smugglers who exploit vulnerable individuals.  However, the Prime Minister also acknowledged that a purely enforcement-led approach is insufficient.  He stressed the necessity of providing more 'safe and legal' routes for individuals seeking to come to the UK, suggesting this is a crucial component in managing overall migration flows and reducing the incentive for perilous sea crossings.  The dual approach aims to deter illegal entry while offering legitimate pathways for those with genuine claims.  Details on the specific expansion of safe routes were not elaborated upon, but the acknowledgment signals a potential shift in the government's strategy, moving beyond solely punitive measures.  The ongoing challenge of managing irregular migration remains a significant political and humanitarian concern, with the government indicating a long-term commitment to addressing it through a multi-faceted approach.
