Spain has launched a strong rebuke against several fellow European Union countries, labelling their responses to the recent influx of migrants into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta as 'selfish' and counterproductive. The strong condemnation comes in the wake of Italy’s decision to temporarily suspend the Schengen agreement, a move that allows for passport-free travel within most EU member states. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni described the scenes witnessed in Ceuta as 'shocking,' a statement that has clearly irked Spanish authorities.

The Spanish government has argued that such unilateral actions and critical rhetoric undermine the principles of EU solidarity and shared responsibility in managing external borders. Officials in Madrid emphasized that the situation in Ceuta is a complex challenge that requires a coordinated European approach, not isolated, nationalistic reactions. They have accused some EU partners of failing to grasp the gravity of the humanitarian and security implications, prioritizing narrow self-interest over collective action. Spain has consistently called for greater burden-sharing and for the EU to engage more actively with countries of origin and transit to address the root causes of migration. The ongoing diplomatic friction highlights the deep divisions within the EU on migration policy and the ongoing struggle to forge a unified and effective response to the continent's persistent migration challenges.
