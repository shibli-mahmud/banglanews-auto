Alex Davies-Jones, the Minister for Victims and Sentencing, has indicated that Prime Minister Rishi Sunak is actively considering the establishment of a public inquiry into the Jeffrey Epstein affair. Speaking to the BBC's Newsnight program, Davies-Jones confirmed that she had discussed the matter with the Prime Minister during their initial meetings following her recent reappointment to her ministerial position. This suggests a potential shift in the government's stance, as previous calls for a comprehensive inquiry have faced resistance.

The minister emphasized that the government is 'looking into' the prospect, a phrase that, while not a definitive commitment, signals a willingness to explore the possibility further. The revelation comes amid ongoing pressure from victims' advocates and parliamentarians who argue that a formal, independent inquiry is necessary to fully understand the scope of Epstein's operations and the failures in law enforcement and legal systems that allowed his alleged abuses to persist for so long.  The nature and remit of any potential inquiry, should it proceed, remain unclear. However, the minister's comments offer a glimmer of hope to those who have been campaigning for greater accountability and a deeper examination of the systemic issues that may have facilitated Epstein's crimes.
