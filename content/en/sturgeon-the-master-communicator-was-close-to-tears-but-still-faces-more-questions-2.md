The once formidable Nicola Sturgeon, who steered Scotland through a tumultuous period of political upheaval, is now facing a different kind of storm. Recent public appearances have seen the former First Minister on the brink of tears, a raw display that has surprised many accustomed to her steely resolve. This emotional vulnerability, writes Laura Kuenssberg, signals a significant shift in her public persona and, more importantly, in her perceived authority.

Sturgeon's departure from the helm of the Scottish National Party (SNP) and her subsequent grilling by parliamentary committees have cast a long shadow over her legacy. The questions continue to circle, probing the decisions made and the processes followed during her eight years in office. While she has maintained a dignified front for the most part, the visible strain suggests the weight of scrutiny is taking its toll. Her ability to command attention and project unwavering confidence, a hallmark of her leadership, appears to be waning.

The narrative now is one of a leader confronting the inevitable reckoning that follows extended periods of power. The initial confidence and security that characterized her time as First Minister seem to have been eroded by the relentless demands for accountability. As the inquiries continue, the focus remains on what was done, why it was done, and the ultimate impact on Scotland. Sturgeon's emotional display, though humanizing, underscores the precariousness of political power and the enduring need for transparency, even for those who have held the highest offices.
