The Spanish government has sharply criticised the reaction of some of its European partners to the escalating migrant situation in Ceuta, a Spanish city located on the northern coast of Africa. In a recent period, thousands of sub-Saharan African migrants managed to enter the enclave, leading to a humanitarian crisis and a diplomatic row. Spain's Foreign Minister, José Manuel Albares, stated that the response from certain EU countries has been 'selfish' and has failed to recognise the shared responsibility in managing external borders.

Italy's Prime Minister, Giorgia Meloni, described the scenes in Ceuta as 'shocking' and her government subsequently announced a temporary suspension of the Schengen travel agreement with Spain. This move, intended to bolster border controls, has been interpreted by Madrid as an uncooperative and isolating tactic. Albares emphasised that the challenges faced by Spain at its southern border are a European issue, not solely a Spanish one. He urged for greater solidarity and a coordinated approach to migration management, highlighting that countries like Italy, which have historically been at the forefront of migrant arrivals, should be more understanding of Spain's predicament.

The Spanish administration believes that instead of resorting to punitive measures or isolating Spain, EU member states should be working together to develop sustainable solutions. This includes addressing the root causes of migration, strengthening cooperation with North African countries, and implementing fair and humane asylum procedures. The government fears that such 'selfish' reactions could undermine the spirit of European cooperation and the principles of shared responsibility that underpin the EU.
